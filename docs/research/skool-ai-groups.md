## What Jack is building (Perfect Product, summarized)
The Perfect Product page defines a product that clears two bars:
1. It pays more than once: recurring or repeat revenue.
2. It is built once and sold many times: the next sale costs close to nothing.
It draws 11 shared traits from seven examples, including \$7 Ad Coaching, Stream Deck, the Carex Hip Kit, Zoom, [OnlineJobs.ph](http://OnlineJobs.ph), [PLR.me](http://PLR.me), and EZInspo. The traits include:
- a one-sentence promise
- a cheap first yes
- a value ladder
- self-serve delivery
- deleting a bottleneck
- evergreen demand
- a founder scratching their own itch
- a niche beachhead
- built-in distribution
- a moat that isn't the product
- a core that never gets rebuilt
It ends with an 8-question scorecard. The related OpenClaw Selling Ideas work, in Maggie's feedback page, points to two tracks:
- a managed **AI Website & Marketing Assistant** for local businesses
- a lower-ticket IM product ladder, **Talk To Your WordPress Site With OpenClaw**
A Skool community is a natural container for both: a recurring membership for the IM ladder, plus a free group as the top of the funnel for managed services.
## About OpenClaw (verified)
OpenClaw is an MIT-licensed, self-hosted AI agent gateway that connects chat apps (Telegram, WhatsApp, Slack, and others) to LLM agents. It was created by Peter Steinberger. Its names were Warelay (Nov 2025), then Clawdbot (Jan 2, 2026), then Moltbot (Jan 27, after Anthropic trademark complaints), then OpenClaw (Jan 30, 2026). Steinberger joined OpenAI in Feb 2026, and the project is now stewarded by the OpenClaw Foundation, a 501(c)(3). It has no paid tier. The software is free, so the money is in setup, hosting, configs and skills, training, and done-for-you services. That is exactly what the Skool groups below sell. Sources: Wikipedia, GitHub README, CNBC 2026-02-02.

## Which models work best, and why
1. **Mid-ticket, outcome-guaranteed groups fed by YouTube (\$87–\$184/month).** Maker School, AIS Plus, and AI Automations by Jack each make \$200K+ MRR with only about 2.5K–3.7K members. They sell a concrete result, like "first client in 90 days" or "AI partner retainers", not access to a content library. Delivery is daily or weekly help. Every one of them had a YouTube audience first.
2. **Freemium funnel: a giant free group upselling to paid.** AIS (464K free, 3.5K paid), RoboNuggets (82K free, 1.3K paid), AI Money Lab (92K free, 3.4K paid), and AI Workshop (64K free, 1.2K paid). Free-to-paid conversion looks like roughly 1–5% on these counts. The free group gives away templates and runs a short challenge, and the owner collects email in the join survey.
3. **Low-ticket volume with price-lock scarcity (\$9–\$33/month).** AI Video Bootcamp, Claude Code Club, and AI Tools & Training Club all use "only N spots left at \$9, then \$50". It's the same wedge as Laurel's \$7 ad coaching. It works, but it needs tens of thousands of members to reach \$200K.
4. **High-ticket vertical coaching (\$1,000+/month).** The biggest money on Skool is Blue-Collar Biz Accelerator: \$1,000,380 MRR from 824 trades and home-service owners at \$1,597/month. No AI group plays at this level yet for a specific local-business vertical.
5. **Training bundled with software.** Chameleon Mode, RoboNuggets' Rubric OS, AI Workshop's packaged agents, and the Realtors group's upcoming SaaS. Owning the tool creates the "moat that isn't the product".
## Common patterns
- **Pricing:** Paid AI groups cluster at \$9, \$27–\$49, \$87–\$99, and \$129–\$184 a month. Most show Standard and Premium tiers with an annual discount (30–45% off, or "save \$749"). Members who join early keep their price when it goes up (grandfathering), and "price rises at X members" is used everywhere.
- **Offer stack:** Classroom courses, weekly (up to daily) live calls, template and n8n libraries, daily prompts, software discounts (\$3M–\$20K "value"), jobs boards, giveaways and hackathons, 1:1 onboarding in the premium tier, and guarantees (7-day refund, 90-day client, 30-day ROI).
- **Lead magnets:** Free template packs, 7-day challenges with a certificate (AIS), free courses "worth \$997", one-click install scripts, and GitHub repos.
- **Community mechanics:** Levels that unlock content or rewards (the AAA scholarship, Imperio's free lifetime membership at level 8). Win posts and proof-of-income posts. Surveys that collect email, phone, or Telegram. Affiliate programs at 20–50%. Skool Games badges and Top-1% badges used as social proof.
- **Distribution:** Almost always YouTube first, then Instagram, X, or Facebook. Skool Discovery helps once a group has reviews and activity. Skool's Growth Boost takes a 30% recurring cut, per Steal What Works.
- **Tool churn:** The course content changes constantly. Pages say "updated Sep 24/25 2026", and groups switch from n8n to Claude Code to Hermes. One OpenClaw group already rebranded away from OpenClaw. The Perfect Product trait "the core never gets rebuilt" is the hardest one for AI education to meet.
## Gaps and opportunities
- **No one owns OpenClaw on Skool.** The largest OpenClaw group has 875 members. Facebook has 34K–139K-member OpenClaw groups (see the Facebook research page). The demand exists, but Skool communities haven't captured it.
- **Few vertical local-business AI groups.** Realtors (824) and Local AI Agency Accelerator (4.6K, aimed at agency builders) are the closest. Nothing on Skool targets "AI marketing assistant for local service businesses" with safe setup, even though trades coaching is the #1 money-maker on the platform.
- **Safety and approval mode as the hook.** OpenClaw's creator told CNBC it's "not meant for non-technical users" yet. Only Openclaw Builders Club leads with security. Maggie's "approval mode" message fills that gap.
- **Done-for-you or managed tiers are rare and priced vaguely.** Only Joel Yi (\$200/month) and Jeff's premium onboarding come close. A clear "we host and manage it" tier is open.
- **Proof from operating many agents.** Most owners teach one assistant. Jack running about 18 agents in real business is rare, credible proof.

## Recommendations
1. **Don't launch a generic "make money with AI" group.** Skool Discovery shows 1,000+ results for "ai money". The named benchmark makes about \$9–\$12K MRR, and the direct clone (AI Money Hub) already outranks it. Pick a narrow beachhead: local service businesses and the marketers who serve them.
2. **Build a two-level Skool funnel that matches Maggie's two tracks.**
	- (a) A free Skool group, something like "AI Marketing Assistant for Local Business (OpenClaw)", with a one-click or scripted install, 5 ready marketing skills, and a 7-day challenge. The join survey collects email.
	- (b) A paid group at \$29–\$49/month Standard (SOP classrooms, skill and template library, a monthly call) and \$97–\$149 Premium (2 calls a month, 1:1 onboarding, config reviews).
	- (c) A managed ZedBiz service at \$300–\$500/month per business for people who want it done for them.
3. **Lead with safety and approval mode, and show proof from operations.** "Runs 18 agents in a real agency" plus "nothing publishes without your OK". Nobody else on Skool combines those two.
4. **Use the audience Jack already has.** The Facebook OpenClaw groups (34K–106K members) are where to post value and send people to the free Skool group. On Skool alone, OpenClaw demand is thin.
5. **Copy the mechanics that work.** Price-lock scarcity ("\$19 for the first 200, then \$39"), grandfathering, an annual discount, levels that unlock bonus skills, win posts, a 20–40% affiliate program, and a narrow guarantee ("assistant live in 7 days or refund").

### Verdict
**Where it fits.** The Practitioner Briefing is a newsletter model, not a Skool model. None of the Skool top 100 entries reviewed is a pure written briefing. Every group that makes money sells live help, implementation, tools, or a peer room alongside the content. The briefing belongs in the "cheap first yes" slot of the ladder, like Laurel's \$7 offer. Delivered by email, it works as a paid lead generator, not the profit center.
**What's strong.** The proof can't be faked: a live fleet of about 17 agents. The per-subscriber cost is close to zero. The anti-hype tone works on Skool too (Early AI-dopters, Agentic AI for Founders). And practitioner write-ups do sell on Skool: AI SEO RAINMAKERS makes \$85K MRR at \$299 by selling tests with full methodology.
**What's missing compared with the successful groups:**
1. **Distribution.** Every AI winner has a feeder: YouTube, a viral video, or a free group of 26K–464K. At \$20/month, 1,000 subscribers is only \$20K MRR, less than half the top-100 cutoff. Low-price Skool groups needed thousands of members and large traffic to get there.
2. **An outcome promise.** "Agent fleet ops" names a topic. The winners promise a result: "first client in 90 days", "never run out of leads", "44 books closed monthly".
3. **A defined buyer with money at stake.** The page says "busy operators" but doesn't say who. On Skool, local and trade owners pay \$99–\$2,500 a month. AI hobbyist-builders pay \$9–\$184.
4. **Artifacts.** Skool buyers pay for configs, templates, and tools (Pennyworth's courses, Early AI-dopters' OS, AI SEO Mastery Pro's software). A briefing without versioned files is easier to copy and easier to cancel.
5. **Cadence.** The page's own decay thesis argues for weekly issues, and all 10 examples publish weekly or more often. Monthly is the slowest cadence of the group.
6. **Mid and high tiers with prices.** The ladder is named but has no prices.
### Specific changes to consider
1. **Keep the briefing pure** (email, \$15–\$20/month or \$150–\$200/year), but **go weekly**: a short weekly field note plus a monthly deep incident review. Add a free public edition as the lead magnet, the way Growth Memo and 2PM do.
2. **Rewrite the promise around an outcome for a named buyer.** For example: "Run AI agents in your business without them breaking. A weekly field report from a 17-agent live fleet." Choose between agency owners and small-business operators.
3. **Ship something versioned with every issue:** a config or SOP diff, an incident postmortem, a prompt or skill file. That also seeds tier 2.
4. **Price the ladder** using the Skool evidence:
	- Tier 2: a Fleet Playbook with templates and a quarterly or monthly office hour, at \$97–\$149/month (Agentic AI for Founders at \$97, Pennyworth at \$149). Skool is a reasonable home for this tier.
	- Tier 3: advisory, or a vertical "AI marketing ops" implementation program for one kind of local business at \$1,500–\$2,500/month, sold through a strategy call (the Blue-Collar, MiNT, and MedSpa pattern).
5. **Solve distribution before launch.** Post short fleet-incident videos (Vivian), post value in the Facebook OpenClaw groups (34K–106K members), and cross-post the free edition. Pennyworth (20 members at \$149) shows that a strong concept with no audience stays small.
6. **Reconcile the fleet count.** The Briefing page says 17 agents; other notes say about 18.
