## Cross-read hypotheses to test

| Tension | Hypothesis | Evidence that would distinguish the possibilities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Content versus obligation | A small member-action unit—mission, comment-a-word prompt, or homework—may be more useful than another tip | Owner posts it; members complete an action; owner uses the next unit and pays |
| B2B2C structure | Rejected agency packaging does not disprove selling through an upstream payer | A named payer with a repeated costly problem and an existing route to end users |
| Owner credit and voice | Owners may prefer a fresh signal they can remake over finished copy | Compare what an owner uses, how much they rewrite, and whether preparation time actually falls |
| Free mega-groups versus paid Skool owners | Large audiences may lack buying intent; established paid owners may already solve the job | Evidence of actual spend, current work, unmet friction, and switching reasons by segment |
| Consumed versus accumulated | This week's completed action may carry the value; a vault may be incidental | Repeat use and purchase tied to the new unit, rather than archive size |
| Door Opener versus another Shovel feature pass | Agency acquisition pain may deserve a separate question | Owner evidence that a prospect-facing win differs from the free audits already offered |

Engagement counts alone do not prove retention, revenue, or willingness to pay. A member-action unit is a format hypothesis, not a newly approved product definition.

## Suggested order, still unrun

1. Listen to owners and document what they already do, buy, and leave undone.
2. Qualify a small named shortlist; group size alone is insufficient.
3. Apply the Shovel proof gate before adding features or building a vault.
4. If the evidence supports a test, show one complete item and record use, results, payment, and repeat demand.
5. Make a dated decision against criteria chosen before the test.

Door Opener can be investigated as a separate question. No outreach or pilot is authorized by this document.
