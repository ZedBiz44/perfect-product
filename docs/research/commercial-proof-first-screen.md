**Status:** Research screen complete. No product is approved for launch.
## What the evidence eliminates
The market evidence does **not** support a generic digital guide, checklist, prompt pack, or template as Jack’s first product.
### Rural land and acreage buyer guides
There is a genuine, expensive buying moment. Land buyers need to understand access, water, zoning, drainage, septic, utilities, taxes, and financing. That proves pain, not a paid-product opening.
- Major land lenders and marketplaces already give detailed buyer guides away.
- Etsy shows **1,000+ results** for “land buyer guide,” including editable guides and bundles at roughly €5 to €68. Several listings have hundreds or thousands of reviews.
**Decision:** Do not build a direct-to-consumer acreage checklist or generic realtor buyer-guide template.
### Business buyer and seller guides
Buying or selling a business is a serious transaction moment. But BizBuySell, brokers, banks, and business-development centres already give away detailed checklists and seller guides.
- BizBuySell provides free buyer and seller guides, including a detailed digital-asset transfer guide.
- Business-broker vendors sell much broader operating toolkits, including a current \$97 broker toolkit.
**Decision:** Do not sell a general business-buyer, seller-preparation, or digital-asset checklist directly to owners.
### Membership and sponsorship kits
Membership renewal is a real annual pressure point. But association and event software vendors give away renewal templates, checklists, prompts, and sponsorship toolkits as lead-generation material.
**Decision:** Do not build a generic association renewal or sponsorship template bundle.
## The usable insight
The moments are real. The information products are weak because generic information is used as **free lead material** by the companies that already own the customer relationship.
> The possible business is not a generic guide. It is a narrow client-acquisition and decision system sold to the channel owner who already meets the end buyer at the moment of need.
The end buyer receives a useful worksheet, tool, checklist, calculator, or AI-assisted planning path. The actual payer is the broker, lender, adviser, publisher, or similar channel owner who can use it to attract, qualify, or serve their buyers more efficiently.
A plain white-label PDF remains a commodity. A serious system must have a narrow buyer moment, non-generic local or industry detail, lead capture, automatic delivery and follow-up, and a clear way for the channel owner to benefit.
## The two channel-owner hypotheses
### Rural property buyer system
- **End-buyer moment:** A buyer is considering rural or acreage property and needs to decide whether it can support their intended use.
- **Payer:** A rural real-estate agent, land lender, or close partner.
- **Potential value to payer:** More qualified leads and a useful pre-tour or pre-application conversation.
- **Potential product:** A region-specific Rural Property Pre-Offer Reality Check with a lead-capture page, property-visit scorecard, professional-question list, and automatic follow-up copy.
- **Founder fit:** Rural and farming background, commercial-lending view, and local marketing.
- **Red flag:** Generic versions are cheap commodities. Local rules and specialist review are needed. Do not build until a specific channel owner agrees to a fixed, no-customization founding licence.
### Business-sale digital-readiness system
- **End-buyer moment:** An owner is preparing a business for sale and needs digital assets, customer information, operating records, and transition planning organized.
- **Payer:** A business broker, succession adviser, accountant, or lender.
- **Potential value to payer:** Less repeated chasing, a stronger seller intake process, and more organized sale files.
- **Potential product:** A broker-branded Digital Sale File Starter System with a seller workbook, no-password asset inventory, operations and key-person capture prompts, broker intake form, and a non-legal transition checklist.
- **Founder fit:** Marketing, websites, directories, systems, business ownership, and commercial-lending experience.
- **Red flag:** Free seller guides already exist. Do not build until a broker identifies a material gap and agrees to pay for a fixed-scope founding licence.
## What changes now
Jack should not build a guide and then look for buyers.
- Find a channel owner who already has the end buyer at a transaction point.
- Identify a repeated friction in that channel owner’s current process.
- Define one fixed asset that removes the friction.
- Get paid commitment for a no-customization founding licence.
- Only then make the reusable product.
No full product, community, course, marketplace listing, or software build comes before that commitment.
## Strategic fork
### Build a channel-owner product
Build one narrow fixed-licence system only after a channel owner pays a refundable founding deposit. The two possibilities above are only hypotheses.
### Acquire or partner for existing buyer access
If the goal is immediate sales rather than a channel-owner proof cycle, the honest route is not invention. It is acquisition, licensing, or a revenue-share partnership with an existing small product, publication, directory, or marketplace seller that already has customers. That route requires its own financial and operating review.
