## Why this page exists
The goal is deep research on what works in AI, OpenClaw and marketing communities on Skool and Facebook, so Jack can copy what works.
The angle is **selling shovels**. Instead of being a guru, Jack would supply ready-to-use weekly asset six-packs to community and membership owners and their members. The owner keeps the audience and the conversation. Jack supplies one key usable thing each week.
The **Groups & Offers** database below tracks two kinds of rows:
- **Communities** (Platform = Skool or Facebook): what they sell, what members get, how often, and who runs them.
- **Suppliers** (Platform = Other): businesses that already sell content, templates or done-for-you assets to course creators and community owners.
Skool rows come only from [Skool AI Groups Research - Sep 2026](../../docs/research/skool-ai-groups.md). Supplier rows come from a web research pass on Sep 28, 2026, with the source URL on each row. Blank fields mean the data wasn't on the source.

### Skoolers: retention and owner-pain threads
Private club for Skool owners: 154.3k members, about 4.1–4.4k online, 15 admins. The feed was searched, not fully scrolled.
**Retention:**
- "100% Retention" (Tommy Driscoll, 89 comments): retention is engagement, not more content. Personal welcome, reply to every post, check on inactive members, value in every post, celebrate small wins.
- "The Burn of Churn" (Edwin Melendez, 64 comments): 1% create, 9% contribute, 90% lurk, so empty rooms churn. People who leave rarely say why. Annual plans cut monthly churn.
- "Retention - Question" (Ricardo La Rocca, 42 comments): 580 members, 84% historical retention. Advice: over 90% is great; DM failed card payments; weak onboarding overwhelms new members; if the only asset is a course, people leave after finishing it unless something is still coming (a workshop in 30 days, a course that unlocks later).
- Patrick Horta: locking DMs behind levels increased churn. Sonja Ray: win people back the moment they hit cancel.
**Weekly content pain:**
- "How do you plan out the content" (Ashley Cham, 33 comments, 40 likes): owners have no shared system and invent their own week (value + engagement + live; Sunday reset, Win Wednesday, hot seat; one video a day; quizzes; viral-hook templates swapped into their niche). Maggie Kelly: "I dream of being able to plan a content calendar."
- Skool has no post scheduler (Elsa Jenner). Email-all-members is limited to once per 72 hours (poll: 95 comments, 115 likes).
- Bradley Riley (£1M lifetime): weekly content drops, classroom updates, days split into themes.
**Template demand:**
- Bijan Izadi: affiliates churned because setting up a course for a non-technical buyer took hours on Zoom. Course templates fixed it.
- Ryan Duncan shipped Skool design templates after people commented "template."
- Feature request: "Allow us to save posts as a template."

## Facebook research (Social Shit)
Full report: [Facebook Research (OpenClaw/AI groups)](../../docs/research/facebook-groups.md) (read-only, Sep 28, 2026; 15 largest OpenClaw/AI groups, 18K to 451K members, 10 read in depth, 23-post asset-demand log; feed samples partial).
1. **Grab-and-use assets win engagement.** "Comment WORD, get the asset" posts far outpull normal posts (an n8n template bundle in AI Hub, 154K, got 211 comments; a Hermes "PACK" post got 73). Almost all were posted by members, not owners.
2. **The biggest owners have audience but no product.** The 451K, 428K, 154K, 123K and 103K groups show no priced owner offer. Few visible sellers: an AWS/OpenClaw setup funnel (106K), AgentMail (78K), agency webinars (140K). Owners have nothing to hand members weekly.
3. **Pain is operational:** updates breaking setups, memory and context loss, agent hand-offs, capping tool calls, installs crashing on small servers, local hardware, beginner roadmaps.
4. **Agent operating assets are an open gap.** Zero shares found of [SOUL.md](http://SOUL.md), skill files or agent configs. n8n workflows, Claude cheat sheets and [CLAUDE.md](http://CLAUDE.md) templates get shared; nobody supplies ready configs, hand-off SOPs, memory cleanup or health checks.
5. **Feeds are flooded with spam and low-effort prompt graphics**, and members complain. Concrete help and failure stories get the engagement, so a curated weekly drop would stand out.
Not covered: Chinese and Vietnamese groups; full two-week reads.
## Findings
### Skool: what drives retention and what owners need supplied
From the Sep 28 Skool deep dive only.
1. **Live working sessions beat more videos.** Members bring their own work on screen: AI Money missions (bring the offer), AI SEO Pro (rank map Tuesday, build pages Thursday), Rainmakers, Facebook Ads Q&A, Maker School daily replies, Jack Roberts daily calls.
2. **Retention comes from engagement, not more content.** Welcomes, replying to every post, checking on inactive members, and celebrating wins (Tommy Driscoll, 89 comments).
3. **Something has to still be coming.** People leave after finishing the course unless there's a workshop in 30 days or a module that unlocks later. AIS+ time-locks modules at 30 and 90 days.
4. **Owners can't keep up with the weekly post.** No shared planning system, no post scheduler, email-all capped at once per 72 hours. They hand-build weekly themes (Sunday reset, Win Wednesday, hot seat).
5. **Owners ask for templates.** Course templates stopped affiliate churn. Skool design templates shipped because people commented "template." A feature request asks to save posts as templates. One owner dreams of a content calendar.
6. **Top groups put ready-to-use assets on the sales page.** Maker School has 50+ templates, Jack Roberts 110+ blueprints, AI Money 58+ affiliate templates plus prompt packs and snapshots, and SEO Pro a software seat.
7. **What members want, in their words:** a first client or first \$1K, a posted offer with a number, prompts and swipes to paste, a weekly place to bring unfinished work, and help when a tool breaks.
8. **Complaints are about broken tools, not community tone.** Custom GPT retirement, a Manus restore, OpenClaw updates. Support and migration kits are a product.
9. **New members ask "where do I start?"** Numbered start-here posts and homework threads beat news posts on likes.
