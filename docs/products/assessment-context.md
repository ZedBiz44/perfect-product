## Assumptions used for WC1

- Assume competent setup with existing categories of AI, workflow and publishing tools, followed by a practical steady state. Do not assume arbitrary integration access, flawless AI output, zero costs or immediate commercial success.
- Shared-pack models use AI drafting and packaging, human review once per edition, and customers handling their own audiences. Reports, data pipelines, negotiation and social moderation retain their real per-customer or per-account human burdens.
- Founder-hour ranges are illustrative workload assumptions, not Jack's approved time allowance or measured performance. Ordinary product oversight does not justify a strong independence score when the original promise depends on Jack regularly delivering the value.
- Proposed prices use existing catalog bands where possible. Single working prices make the scenario concrete without replacing the recorded alternatives. Currency remains as stated in the original entry; US$ is retained where explicit. Prices without a currency are not converted or asserted to be CAD or USD.
- Referral shares and reseller margins are proposed incentives, not agreed commercial terms. Distribution descriptions name a channel role and a path, not an invented signed partner or guaranteed first ten customers.
- Rights to reusable IP, permissioned observations and assignable contracts are conditional design assumptions. Customer data is not automatically an owned resale asset.
- Structures get an explicitly illustrative first product and payer. Their scores evaluate that application and the structure's additional costs; they cannot be read as a score for every future portfolio product. Product 35 remains an internal cost centre, not a fabricated factory subscription.
- Working examples leave exploration open. Selecting a dental pack, review-request kit, chamber or regional brokerage for an assessment does not make it Jack's chosen market. Alternatives may produce different scores and require a separately labelled scenario.
