# 23. Sponsor layer on weekly item

[Index](../README.md) · [Rubric](../../framework/perfect-product-rubric.md)

- **Name:** Sponsor layer
- **Idea:** A tool company or supplier pays so audience owners can share a useful weekly item (item stays useful without the logo).
- **How:** Sponsor funds distribution of an already-useful weekly unit; agency/owner keeps client relationship; voluntary next step only.
- **Buyer:** Tool vendors / suppliers as payers; owners as distributors of free-to-them value.
- **Price:** Sponsor pays ~$500–$5,000/mo per niche (wild guess); owners may get free.

- **Shape:** Six Pack of Beer Desire (for end users)

## Working concept for assessment

Version: WC1 · 2026-09-30 · Cody · Proposed assumptions for scoring, authorized by Jack; not a launch decision.

**Working scenario:** Tool-vendor marketing team pays an illustrative $1,000/month for a defined niche placement in an existing weekly item. Hosts distribute; members remain separate users.

**Primary satisfaction shape:** Harvest Gala. This is the working interpretation for this scenario; the original Shape field above is retained.

All bullets below are working assumptions or inferences, not observed operating results. The original description and status above remain the recorded history. [Context and assumption basis](../assessment-context.md).

- **Repeat payment:** Sponsors renew for continued qualified attention, but budget cycles and campaign results make recurrence conditional rather than automatic.
- **Reusable production and costs:** Reuse the content and placement format, but sponsor acquisition, approvals and reporting add account-level work.
- **What accumulates and transfers:** Owned niche publication rights, audience relationships and assignable sponsor contracts accumulate; sponsors do not own the host's customer data.
- **Operator and Jack’s role:** A partnerships operator handles routine terms; Jack may still negotiate key accounts, assumed 1–3 hours/week and more during setup.
- **Satisfaction and success signal:** Sponsor sees qualified voluntary visits over a campaign period; the payer's harvest differs from the member's weekly useful-item satisfaction.
- **Buying trigger, price and alternatives:** A vendor launch creates a trigger, but price cannot be attractive without plausible reach and relevance. This is a proposed placement, not a sold audience.
- **AI and human work per unit:** AI drafts placement variants and reports; human negotiation, sponsor approval and publisher coordination remain core per-account work.
- **Route to the payer:** Existing hosts could introduce relevant suppliers for a revenue share; the first sponsor still needs proof of audience fit and an operator-led sale.
- **Purchase-to-value journey:** Sponsor reviews inventory, agrees terms, approves message and waits for reporting. A useful placement requires coordination across both sides.
- **Advantage from greater scale:** More participating hosts can increase niche reach and lower content cost per placement; permissions and commercial terms must support reuse.

**Unresolved decision:** The payer, price, scope and channel above are a proposed scenario for evaluation. Confirm or change them before any launch; no supplier, partner or customer commitment is implied.
