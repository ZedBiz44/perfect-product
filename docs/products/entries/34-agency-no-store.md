# 34. Agency "No" Store

[Index](../README.md) · [Rubric](../../framework/perfect-product-rubric.md)

- **Name:** Agency "No" Store
- **Idea:** Sell the jobs below an agency's usual scope so they can say no cleanly and still serve the need via a productized handoff.
- **How:** Catalog of below-scope jobs as fixed products; agency refers or resells; channel conflict unresolved.
- **Buyer:** Agencies who turn away small jobs; possibly end SMBs via agency channel.
- **Price:** $29–$149 per job product.
- **Tier:** low–mid
- **Shape:** Tool in the hand
- **Type:** distribution model
- **Status:** hopper
- **Related ideas:** [33. Mary's one-job shelf](33-marys-one-job-shelf.md) · [25. Client Action Layer](25-client-action-layer.md)
- **Price basis:** Unvalidated catalog hypothesis; not evidence of willingness to pay. Sources below establish the concept; no verified market price is claimed.
- **Sources:** `docs/perspectives/manus.md`, `docs/ingredients.md`
- **Notes:** Structural observation; channel conflict unresolved.

## Working concept for assessment

Version: WC1 · 2026-09-30 · Cody · Proposed assumptions for scoring, authorized by Jack; not a launch decision.

**Working scenario:** Illustrative first handoff: an agency refers a $79 reopening announcement kit to a below-budget prospect; the SMB pays and the agency earns a disclosed referral share.

**Primary satisfaction shape:** Tool in the hand. This is the working interpretation for this scenario; the original Shape field above is retained.

All bullets below are working assumptions or inferences, not observed operating results. The original description and status above remain the recorded history. [Context and assumption basis](../assessment-context.md).

- **Repeat payment:** Agencies regularly reject small jobs, creating ongoing one-time buyer flow; repeat payment from each SMB is less likely.
- **Reusable production and costs:** Reuse a fixed editable kit and self-serve delivery. Neither ZedBiz nor the referring agency promises bespoke design as part of the purchase.
- **What accumulates and transfers:** Owned kit assets, referral relationships and transaction records accumulate into transferable distribution and product assets.
- **Operator and Jack’s role:** Operator manages fulfilment and refunds; agency makes a simple referral. Jack handles rare product decisions, assumed 0–2 hours/week.
- **Satisfaction and success signal:** The small business publishes a usable announcement instead of leaving the job undone after an agency declines it.
- **Buying trigger, price and alternatives:** A reopening deadline creates urgency, but $79 competes with free templates and the buyer may still expect done-for-you help.
- **AI and human work per unit:** AI drafts variations and populates approved fields; humans QA shared releases and exceptions. Buyer checks facts before publication.
- **Route to the payer:** Below-budget agency enquiries are the specific route; a referral share and goodwill provide incentives, but cannibalization and support expectations remain conflicts.
- **Purchase-to-value journey:** Receive referral, inspect scope, buy, add details and publish. The handoff fails if the buyer lacks the ability to edit independently.
- **Advantage from greater scale:** More referring agencies reuse the same product shelf and fulfilment system; low-support products are necessary to retain the advantage.

**Unresolved decision:** The payer, price, scope and channel above are a proposed scenario for evaluation. Confirm or change them before any launch; no supplier, partner or customer commitment is implied.
