# Definition feedback (Grok, Sep 29)

[Repository home](../../README.md) · [Perfect Product framework](perfect-product.md) · [Decision guide](../current-direction.md)

Jack asked whether anything else belonged in the Perfect Product definition. This is Grok's reply, kept as a dated note. Jack later expanded the definition to ten bars in Notion; this note is why some of those additions matter, and how to keep overlapping bars distinct.

## What was solid in the seven

Bars 1–4 (repeat pay, build once, sellable asset, runs without you) plus satisfaction, No Brainer, and Creates Gold are a strong core. Satisfaction correctly stopped being cold-beer-only.

## What I recommended adding

### 1. Own distribution → now bar 8, "A natural route to buyers"

Almost every working example carries a buyer channel inside the product or rides a flow someone already owns: Zoom invites, Laurel's YouTube, Carex via surgeons and pharmacies, OnlineJobs marketplace. Without that, you keep inventing marketing. The scorecard already asked this; it belonged in the definition.

### 2. Deletes a bottleneck → partly bar 9, "Easy to get the value"

Stream Deck, Hip Kit, PLR, and $7 coaching remove skill, time, or expertise — not just deliver content. Tips fail this. Tools and harvests pass. "Easy to get the value" covers self-serve delivery and low effort to realize the promise. Keep "deletes a bottleneck" visible in commonalities even if bar 9 is worded around ease, so tips-as-product still fail the test.

### 3. Tighten Creates Gold vs sellable asset

Bar 3 = something a buyer would pay for on exit (list, brand, systems).
Bar 7 = the asset gets *more* valuable with use (data compounds, network effects, archive that ages well).
If it doesn't compound, it's income, not gold.

Bar 10 (Newton's Rule: more mass → more gravity → stronger as it grows) is the growth version of the same idea. Use bar 7 for *asset quality over time* and bar 10 for *network / flywheel strength as customers increase*. Don't treat them as duplicates.

### 4. Optional founder filter, not a universal bar

No community-host treadmill stays a Jack filter (commonality #12), not a Perfect Product bar for every business on earth — unless Jack wants every idea that needs daily charm automatically killed.

### 5. What I would not add as its own bar

Low price. No Brainer already covers price friction. Paint, Event, and Harvest can be higher ticket and still perfect.

## How bars 8–10 fit Jack's ten

| Bar | Jack's label | How to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | A natural route to buyers | Named buyer + path that already exists; product rides a flow |
| 9 | Easy to get the value | Self-serve, fast to realize; ideally deletes a bottleneck |
| 10 | Newton's Rule | Gets stronger as it grows (mass → gravity) |

## Overlap watchlist

- Bar 3 (sellable asset) vs bar 7 (Creates Gold) vs bar 10 (Newton's Rule)
- Bar 8 (natural route) vs commonality #9 (built-in distribution) and #15 (named buyer with reachable path)
- Bar 9 (easy value) vs commonality #4 (self-serve) and #5 (deletes a bottleneck)
- Bar 6 (No Brainer) vs commonality #2 (low-friction entry pricing)

Overlap is fine if the definition stays scannable and the scorecard stays one checklist. When scoring an idea, fail it once for the same weakness rather than three times under three labels.

## Bottom line from the original reply

Keep the satisfaction expansion. Clarify Gold vs sellable asset. Promote distribution and bottleneck into the definition. That was the missing commercial spine — and bars 8–10 are Jack's version of that spine.
