## Product and material assumptions

- **Buyer/payer, offer, delivery and working price:** Agency payer serving seasonal home-service businesses; assess a $399 spring-opening campaign edition, run by the agency for its own clients.
- **Historical catalog price field, retained:** ~$199–$997 per season per niche edition. Neither this nor the working price is validated willingness to pay; no unspecified currency is invented.
- **Primary satisfaction shape:** Harvest Gala
- **Repeat-payment mechanism:** Distinct seasonal buying windows recur annually, creating a concrete reason to buy refreshed offers and later-season editions.
- **Founder/operator model:** Campaign operator maintains the seasonal edition; agencies run client campaigns. Jack contributes occasional positioning input, assumed 0–2 hours/week.
- Competent steady-state execution, bounded support and lawful reusable rights are assumed. Proposed operators and partners are roles, not assigned staff or signed agreements. No precise profit, renewal rate or automatic distribution is assumed.
- **Marsha’s central qualification:** Seasonal home-service scope supplies a real replenishment cycle; the agency still owns every client campaign.

## Result and interpretation

- Coverage: **10/10**. Concept-fit total: **17/20**. Strong fits: **7/10**.
- Zero conflicts: None.
- Founder constraints: **holds**. Seasonal home-service scope supplies a real replenishment cycle; the agency still owns every client campaign.
- Shortlist signal: **Yes, conditional on the stated design assumptions; not selection or launch approval**.
- Remaining work before commercial reliance: confirm the price, delivery/support boundaries, reusable rights and actual channel access. Numeric concept scores do not settle those operating questions.

## Sensitivity and improvements — not credited as current features

- Seasonal home-service scope supplies a real replenishment cycle; the agency still owns every client campaign.
- A shift from shared editions to seller-delivered bespoke work can lower bars 2, 4, 7 and 10. A genuinely repeatable paid channel can improve bar 8, but an imagined audience cannot.
- An operator handoff that truly removes regular founder delivery can improve bar 4; changing the product’s promised personal expert is a different assessed version.
- Improvements should address the specific limitations in the table and be scored as a new scenario, not silently added to this one.
