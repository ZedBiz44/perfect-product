## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** The entry names **agencies and chambers** as buyers and says they administer the score; they are the most directly described payer for multi-client seats or scores. The **end user** is a local SMB that displays the certificate. Whether the SMB instead pays directly, whether the intermediary bundles it, and the reseller arrangement are not specified.
- **Promise and delivery:** A scored local-business marketing certificate that an SMB can hang/display. An agency or chamber administers a **fixed rubric**; the proposed Think and Grow Rich data layer may later feed the scoring as stickiness.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** **Unvalidated** proposal of about **$49–$149 per score**, **annual recertification**, and multi-client agency seats. The explicit repeat mechanism is annual recertification; no observed renewals or willingness to pay are claimed.
- **Founder role:** No recurring founder role is stated. The design delegates administration to agencies/chambers and uses a fixed rubric, rather than founder hosting; the roles for rubric governance, quality assurance, onboarding, support, and exceptions are not specified.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Primary: Trophy.** The end SMB is meant to display the scored certificate. **Supporting shapes: none stated.**

### Material assumptions

1. **Annual recertification assumption:** A current local-presence score/certificate has enough continuing relevance that the same payer would renew it annually. If recertification has no meaningful currency or expiry, Bar 1 would be weaker.
2. **Reusable delivery assumption:** The fixed rubric can be administered per SMB without bespoke expert diagnosis or extensive support. If each score requires material manual audit, Bars 2, 4, and 9 would weaken.
3. **Channel assumption:** Agencies/chambers have a reason and practical access to administer or resell the certificate to their SMB clients/members. The entry names them but documents neither incentive nor access.
4. **Earned-standard assumption:** The rubric can create a certificate that recipients and their audiences regard as meaningful rather than an empty badge. The entry provides no criteria, verifier, or reputational mechanism.
5. **Data-rights assumption:** Scores can lawfully and consistently create usable benchmark data. The cited hopper source says no proprietary campaign-results dataset is demonstrated, so the future data layer is a design intention, not established evidence.
6. **AI-workflow boundary:** No AI/human task split is assumed. The catalogued version does not identify automated tasks, human verification, or exception handling; this produces the Bar 7 **U** rather than an invented C3PO design.
