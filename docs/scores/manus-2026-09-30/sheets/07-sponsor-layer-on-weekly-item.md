## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** A tool company or supplier is the proposed **payer/sponsor**. An existing audience owner (including an agency in the cited exploration) is the proposed free-to-them distributor and keeps its client relationship. The owner's audience receives the useful item; it is not the payer.
- **Promise and delivery:** The sponsor funds distribution of an **already-useful weekly unit** that an audience owner can share. The unit is meant to remain useful without the sponsor logo. Any sponsor contact or next step is voluntary; the cited source says the larger model involving agency selection, opt-ins, campaign coordination, and results management was superseded because it risks service work.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** Catalog hypothesis: **about $500–$5,000 per month per niche**, paid by the sponsor; owners may receive the item free. The intended repeat mechanism is continuing monthly sponsorship of recurring weekly distribution. The entry expressly labels the price unvalidated and does not establish that a sponsor values the resulting voluntary action enough to renew.
- **Founder role:** **Not specified for the catalogued version.** A cited exploratory proposal says ZedBiz would supply the useful item and sponsor relationship, but it does not specify a transferable operating process or the AI/human split.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Primary (recipients/end users): Six Pack of Beer Desire** — one fresh, useful item each week. No separate payer-side satisfaction shape is named. Supporting shapes: none specified.
- **Material assumptions (not source facts):**
  1. **A1 — Sponsor renewal:** A named relevant sponsor considers repeated access to this particular audience and its voluntary next action valuable enough to fund monthly.
  2. **A2 — Distributor use:** Audience owners will share the item without bespoke curation, relationship conflict, or a paid implementation burden.
  3. **A3 — Standalone usefulness:** The underlying weekly item is useful, original, and easy enough to use without its sponsor branding.
  4. **A4 — Standardized coordination:** Sponsor placement, delivery, measurement, and exception handling can stay standardized rather than recreating the rejected multi-party service model.

## Possible improvement (not part of the assessed version)

Specify one sponsor category, one owner/distributor cohort, the sponsor's voluntary next action and privacy-safe measurement, standardized sponsorship rights and renewal terms, and an AI/human operating map that keeps coordination out of routine founder labour. Then test whether the item remains useful without branding and whether both the sponsor and distributor will commit. These changes are **not** credited in the scores above.
