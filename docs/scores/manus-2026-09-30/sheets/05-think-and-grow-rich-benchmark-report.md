## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** The entry names agency owners or community owners seeking proof of what works; the payer is intended to be that owner’s business, either directly or through an unspecified higher tier. For the partial buyer-value judgments below, the narrower, explicitly named **agency-owner** use case is used because the cited source describes showing the report in client reviews. No single community-owner use case or host-product payer is defined.
- **Promise and delivery:** Aggregate outcome evidence into a recurring or annual report about what works across businesses, intended to give owners a reason to stay. The report is a stickiness ingredient for another product, not the main offer. Delivery format, data pipeline, and evidence standard are not specified.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** Included in an unspecified higher tier, about $49–$99 per report, or about $199 annually. These are explicitly unvalidated catalog hypotheses. An annual cadence is stated, but the source says a repeat reason to pay still must be established.
- **Founder role:** Not documented. Neither the entry nor its cited sources assign data collection, normalization, report production, review, support, or exceptions between Jack, AI, a manager, or a partner.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Primary:** Trophy (catalog hypothesis). **Supporting:** Tool in the Hand is a reviewer inference only: the cited agency use is to show evidence in a client review. No supporting shape is declared in the entry.
- **Material assumptions (labelled):**
  - **A1 — shared-edition interpretation:** “Aggregate … into a recurring or annual report” means one standardized edition is prepared for many buyers, rather than a bespoke agency analysis. This supports the partial build-once judgment; a bespoke report would change it.
  - **A2 — agency-owner branch:** The cited source’s agency owner who uses a report in client reviews is a legitimate subset of the entry’s named buyer. This does not establish the broader community-owner buyer or a host-product payer.
  - **A3 — conditional data scale:** The cited proposed mechanism—agency opt-in supplying campaign results at growing scale—could make later benchmark reports more valuable. It is not proof that agencies will share data, that privacy permissions exist, or that the business owns transferable rights.
  - **A4 — low-ticket comparison:** A $49–$99 report or $199 annual price could be small relative to retaining a client for an agency, but no buyer purchase, retention result, budget, or willingness-to-pay evidence is claimed.
