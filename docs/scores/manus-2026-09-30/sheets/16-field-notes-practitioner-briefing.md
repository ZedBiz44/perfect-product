## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** Proposed buyers are **agency owners / marketing operators**. The sources imply the subscribing operator or their agency would pay, but do **not** distinguish the economic payer, budget line, or decision-maker beyond that broad category.
- **Promise and delivery:** A short weekly/periodical operating note based on real practitioner work, plus one reusable artifact. It is a self-serve paid letter rather than a community, coaching program, calls, or per-client service.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** About **$7–19/month** or **$190/year**, explicitly an unvalidated catalog hypothesis. The designed repeat mechanism is a paid stream: a current field changes or breaks, so the next weekly note, judgment, and artifact are what the subscriber continues to buy; the archive is not the principal retention mechanism.
- **Founder role:** The source model requires a practitioner who does consequential ongoing work and extracts personal judgment from it. Jack would therefore be a regular author/curator of the weekly unit, even though subscriber fulfilment itself has no calls or community labor. Later source material records that Jack-as-author is ruled out: he receives many free letters he judges better and does not consider himself a strong writer.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Primary: Six Pack of Beer Desire** (catalogued). **Supporting: none established.** A reusable artifact could become a *Tool in the Hand*, but the assessed entry does not specify one sufficiently to claim that shape.

### Material assumptions

1. **[A — B1]** The chosen practitioner field changes often enough that weekly judgment and artifacts become stale and are worth renewing for. The archive presents this as a model requirement; the catalog does not identify the exact field or prove it for the proposed agency-owner buyer.
2. **[B — B2, B9, B10]** Each issue and artifact can be distributed digitally in the same form to all subscribers, with no material custom installation, diagnosis, or routine support. This follows the described no-calls/no-community design, not observed operations.
3. **[C — B3]** The publisher owns or can transfer the subscriber relationship and archive. No ownership terms, subscriber data rights, or transaction evidence are supplied.
4. **[D — not used to fill B6 or B8]** Agency owners/marketing operators have a relevant budget and can be reached. This is **not** assumed as fact: the absent buyer trigger/value relationship leaves B6 unjudged, and the absent acquisition route leaves B8 unjudged.

- **Assumptions that could change scores:** A named, high-velocity field and a proven buyer trigger could resolve B6; an owned partner/referral/search channel could resolve B8; and a materially different AI-led editorial production system or transferable contributor newsroom could improve B7/B4. - **Unresolved question:** **What specific agency-owner or marketing-operator problem, paid budget, and non-founder acquisition channel would make a $7–19/month practitioner letter plus artifact an obvious recurring purchase despite abundant free newsletters?**

If research were ever separately authorized, define one named agency-operator trigger and a measurable artifact outcome, secure a channel owner with an incentive to distribute it, and test whether an AI-led, human-verified editorial system can preserve useful practitioner signal without requiring Jack’s weekly authorship.
