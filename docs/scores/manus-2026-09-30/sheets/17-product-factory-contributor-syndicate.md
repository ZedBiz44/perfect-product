## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** **Internal first:** ZedBiz/Jack is the stated cost bearer. The entry says it may later supply distributors, but names neither a distributor nor an end user or payer.
- **Promise and delivery:** An internal machine intended to repeatedly discover and produce narrowly targeted products, optionally using outside expertise. Its proposed pipeline is **Market → problem → research → asset → campaign → QA → package → test**, with AI/agents as the manufacturing floor. It is not itself described as a sold customer deliverable.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** **N/A as a customer offer; cost center until a product sells.** No price, product-level payment, or repeat-payment mechanism is specified.
- **Founder role:** Not specified for a steady state. The design names AI/agents as manufacturing infrastructure, while the cited Z3 proposal says a future manual MVP would record what still requires Jack; it does not allocate the ongoing jobs of opportunity selection, QA, contributor management, or distributor sales.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **None declared / not assessable.** This is a meta structure with no described purchaser experience, so it cannot be credibly mapped to a primary shape.
- **Material assumptions (labelled):**
  1. **Ownership assumption:** ZedBiz can own and document the proposed pipeline, templates, and any contributor arrangements; no IP-rights or contributor terms are stated.
  2. **Output assumption:** A later product would need its own wanted problem, named buyer/payer, value, price, and delivery model. This is not assumed to exist for scoring; the entry itself says the structure needs a wanted product first.
  3. **Reuse assumption:** The Z3 discussion's target of roughly 70–80% shared production across vertical editions is a proposed test condition, not an achieved result or a property established for this entry.
  4. **Channel assumption:** No distributor relationship, channel access, or incentive is assumed merely because possible distributor categories are discussed.

## Result

- **Assessment summary:** This hopper item is a potentially reusable internal production structure, not a defined customer product. Its planned transferable operating IP and conditional shared-production benefit support only two partial numeric fits; the missing offer, payer, experience, task split, operations, and channel require eight **U** scores rather than failures.
- **Coverage /10:** 2
- **Concept-fit total /20 (blank if any U):**
- **Strong fits /10:** 0
- **Every zero and its conflict:** None. No bar is scored 0 because the record documents absent essential design details rather than a described model conflict.
- **Shortlist signal under rubric v1.1:** No. The assessment has eight U scores, no total, and zero strong fits; it is not eligible for the rubric's shortlist signal. This does not change its hopper status.
- **Assumptions that could change scores:** A specified wanted product with a named upstream payer and price could make bars 1, 5, 6, and 9 assessable. A documented shared process and explicit AI/human operating system could make bars 2, 4, and 7 assessable. A committed distributor with an incentive and access could make bar 8 assessable. Evidence is not required for those concept scores, but the mechanisms must be described.
- **Unresolved question:** What single wanted product, named external payer, price/repeat mechanism, AI–human workflow, and incentivized distributor route would this factory actually deliver without bespoke rebuilding?
- **Possible improvement — outside the assessed version:** Before treating the structure as a product, define one narrow output and its buyer/payer; set its delivery and renewal mechanism; document IP/contributor rights and reusable modules; assign AI routine work versus human QA and exception roles; and secure or specify a distributor incentive. This is a possible redesign, not evidence about or a score change for Entry 17.
