## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** The entry states “any operator with a weekly customer-value obligation” and “distributors who own audiences.” Either the operator or an upstream distributor could pay. It does **not** choose one payer, segment, budget, or economic case; cited material offers possible segments but does not settle the catalogued version.
- **Promise and delivery:** “This week is handled”: a completed weekly campaign-in-a-box that removes a recurring customer/member-value obligation rather than selling undifferentiated “content.” The cited design describes a shared weekly package of a useful concept, engagement posts, supplementary asset(s), and delivery via email/vault; white-label use is contemplated.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** The catalog price is a **founding pilot of about $29–$49 for the first month**. Weekly delivery is the intended repeat-value mechanism. The entry does not state a post-pilot renewal price, contract, or renewal terms. The cited Shovel page's proposed monthly ladder is a related, explicitly untested proposal and is not substituted for this entry's price.
- **Founder role:** In the cited operating design, AI agents research, draft, adapt, design, QC, and ship; Jack selects the weekly concept, reviews/edits quality, adds practical experience where helpful, spot-checks, and approves shipment. The cited estimate is a proposal, not operating evidence.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Primary: Six Pack of Beer Desire** (explicitly named in the entry): one fresh, ready-to-use weekly unit that relieves an immediate recurring obligation. **Supporting: Tool in the Hand**: a ready campaign-in-a-box helps the operator act rather than start from a blank page.
- **Material assumptions:**
  - **A1 — shared-edition assumption:** One completed edition can be sold/delivered to multiple buyers in the same niche rather than custom-built per buyer.
  - **A2 — usable-output assumption:** The source-checked AI workflow can produce material an owner can credibly post under its own brand with bounded human review.
  - **A3 — recurring-job assumption:** The target payer experiences a weekly obligation often enough for a weekly kit to remain useful; this is a design premise, not demand evidence.
  - **A4 — payer/channel restraint:** No particular operator, distributor, budget, partner access, or channel-owner incentive is assumed merely because broad categories are listed. This restraint produces the two U scores below.

- **Assumptions that could change scores:** A selected payer with a documented recurring budget/pain/trigger could resolve bar 6. A named partner or owner community with a credible incentive and access route could resolve bar 8. Evidence that renewal, unit economics, or editorial substitution works could strengthen or weaken bars 1, 2, 4, 7, 9, and 10.
- **Unresolved question:** **Which single upstream payer will repeatedly buy the kit after the pilot, because of what specific weekly economic pain, and through which reachable channel owner with an incentive to distribute it?**

## Possible improvement — not part of the assessed version

Narrow the broad “any operator/distributor” proposition to one named payer and one audience-owning route, then define a continuing post-pilot offer and the minimum shared-edition workflow. This could make the No-Brainer and route-to-buyer tests assessable, but it is an improvement proposal—not a fact used to score catalog entry 36.
