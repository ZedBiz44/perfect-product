## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** The intended payer is an upstream business whose economics suffer when its end users fail to get results. The entry lists course companies, associations, SaaS businesses, franchises, directories, and consultants as possible white-label licensees. **No one named payer, vertical, or budget owner is selected.** End users receive the layer but are not described as the payer.
- **Promise and delivery:** ZedBiz would manufacture an unspecified implementation, engagement, customer-success, or marketing “ingredient” that the payer white-labels into its existing offer to make that offer more useful or sticky. Delivery form, implementation work, cadence, integration, and the fixed reusable unit are not specified.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** The catalog proposes white-label licences of about **$197–$997 per month or per seat**. This is explicitly an **unvalidated catalog hypothesis**, not price or willingness-to-pay evidence. Monthly/per-seat licensing is a proposed billing mechanism, but the version does not identify a recurring delivered unit or a reason a particular licensee renews.
- **Founder role:** Not specified. Z3 frames AI, agents, automation, research, and creative tools as a possible manufacturing floor and proposes measuring what still requires Jack in a manual MVP; it does not assign a steady-state sales, production, QA, onboarding, or exception role for this entry.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Not specified / cannot judge.** The entry expressly labels the idea structural and says its end-user shape varies. It therefore does not establish a buyer-facing primary shape or a supporting shape.

### Material assumptions

1. **Assumption — reusable IP:** A licensable white-label layer would ordinarily create supplier-owned IP and contractual/licensee records. This supports the limited numeric asset assessment only; the entry does not name the asset or prove that it accumulates.
2. **Assumption — scale path:** Supplying a common layer through a channel owner could let one licensee reach many downstream users. This supports the limited Newton’s Rule assessment only; cross-licensee network effects, low support costs, and partner access are **not** assumed.
3. **Not assumed:** Any particular licensee has a retention problem, budget, authority, willingness to pay, access arrangement, successful underlying product, or renewal behavior.
4. **Not assumed:** AI can routinely produce, QA, integrate, or support the layer without material human work. The entry and cited source do not provide that task split.
