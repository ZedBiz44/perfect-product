## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** The named buyer is a **group, chamber, or membership owner**. The likely payer is that owner/operator (or its organization) purchasing an event pack; this payer identification is an inference from the named buyer and listed sale prices, not a verified budget-holder or purchasing behavior.
- **Promise and delivery:** A ready-to-run member-night kit: **theme, mission, guest prompt, photo moment, and run-of-show**. The owner’s staff or VA executes the night; the stated hard rule is that **Jack never hosts**.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** The catalog hypothesizes **about $49–$149 per night pack** or **about $199–$399 for a seasonal pack**. Repeat payment is proposed through additional nights or seasonal packs, rather than an asserted subscription or observed renewal.
- **Founder role:** Jack supplies the product rather than the event performance and is explicitly off stage. The entry does **not** allocate ongoing pack creation, fulfilment, support, sales, or management between Jack, systems, AI, or trained people.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Primary: Event Excitement** — anticipation, a peak member night, and a photo/afterglow moment. **Supporting: Belonging without hosting** is possible for the owner’s members, but it is not an explicit operating-system or community design in this entry.
- **Material assumptions (labelled):**
  - **A1 — standardized reuse:** Each night/seasonal pack is a fixed, licensable package that can be sold to more than one unrelated owner without custom redesign. The entry calls it a product and lists fixed components, but does not expressly state reuse or licensing.
  - **A2 — recurring occasion and discretion:** Some named owners hold enough member events to buy another pack or a seasonal pack and have discretion to spend at the stated prices. Neither recurrence nor willingness to pay is evidenced.
  - **A3 — owner-side execution:** The owner’s staff/VA can coordinate the expected event work (including timing, people, and event use) without Jack hosting. The entry assigns execution to them but gives no operating proof.
  - **A4 — transferable ownership:** The seller retains rights in a growing event-pack catalog and may lawfully retain/use purchaser records. This is necessary for the proposed catalog and organizer-list asset.
  - **A5 — no unstated automation or channel:** No AI task split and no route by which ZedBiz reaches owners are assumed; those omissions produce U scores rather than failure scores.

- **Strong fits /10:** **3/10** — Bars 1, 3, and 5.
- **Every zero and its conflict:** **None.** No described model conflicts with a bar; the material gaps are recorded as U rather than converted to zero.
- **Shortlist signal under rubric v1.1:** **No.** A shortlist signal requires ten numeric scores, no zeros, at least seven strong fits, and at least 17/20. This version has three U scores and only three strong fits; no total is calculated.
- **Assumptions that could change scores:** Validated repeat event demand and a known budget/trigger could raise Bar 6 and strengthen Bar 1. A documented fixed-pack delivery and margin scenario could raise Bar 2 and clarify Bar 10. A transferable operating system with assigned routine responsibilities could resolve Bar 4. A named AI production/delivery workflow could resolve Bar 7. A channel with credible owner access and incentive could resolve Bar 8. Reducing owner coordination could improve Bar 9.
- **Unresolved questions:** **Who specifically reaches and pays which group/chamber/membership owners for a pack, through what repeatable channel, and why would that payer choose a $49–$149 night pack or $199–$399 seasonal pack over free AI, PLR, or in-house planning?**
