## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** Proposed payer/customer: a business broker, succession adviser, accountant, or lender buying a broker-branded licence. Proposed end user/beneficiary: the business owner preparing for sale, with the payer's staff using the intake process. The entry does not select one payer segment.
- **Promise and delivery:** A fixed-scope, broker-branded **Digital Sale File Starter System** intended to organize a seller's digital assets, customer information, operating records, key-person information, and transition planning. Listed components are a seller workbook, no-password asset inventory, operations/key-person prompts, broker intake, and a non-legal transition checklist.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** Founding licence hypothesis of approximately **$497–$2,000**. The price is explicitly unvalidated. The described fixed-scope licence states no renewal, update fee, per-sale fee, additional-seat fee, or continuing one-time buyer flow; it is therefore assessed as a one-time licence for Bar 1.
- **Founder role:** The entry names founder-fit experience but specifies neither Jack's operating tasks nor a manager/partner handoff. No operating role is assumed.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Primary: Tool in the Hand.** Supporting shape: none stated. The broker receives a structured intake tool; the owner is asked to organize a sale file.

### Material assumptions

1. **Scope assumption:** This scores only the listed fixed-scope workbook/inventory/prompts/intake/checklist licence. It does not assume lead capture, automated follow-up, local or industry customization, a software platform, or a data product; these are not in the entry.
2. **Licence assumption:** “Founding licence” is a one-time price hypothesis because no repeat-payment or update design is stated. This is a design reading, not an assertion about future sales.
3. **Delivery assumption:** The named components are reusable digital assets, but the entry does not state whether broker branding, installation, support, or updates are automated, delegated, or bespoke. This uncertainty limits Bar 2 and makes Bar 4 unassessable.
4. **Commercial-evidence assumption:** A serious sale-preparation moment and the stated payer benefits are design facts/hypotheses, not evidence of paid demand. No broker gap or paid commitment is assumed; the cited research requires both before building.
5. **Legal-boundary assumption:** The non-legal checklist is assessed as stated. No legal review, regulatory sufficiency, or advice function is assumed.
6. **Status assumption:** The generic-guide rejection applies as a documented commodity conflict, not as a status change to this broker-channel system. The catalogued entry remains **hopper**.

- **Assumptions that could change scores:** A documented renewal or per-sale licence could change Bar 1; standardized automated/delegated branding and support could improve Bars 2 and 4; a concrete AI/human workflow could resolve Bar 7; a channel agreement or repeatable inbound route could resolve Bar 8; and an owned, accumulating update/data/licence asset or a distinct scale flywheel could improve Bars 3 and 10. - **Unresolved question:** Will a named business broker identify a material seller-intake gap not already served by free seller guides and pay for a fixed-scope founding licence?
- **Assessment summary:** Catalogued as a hopper, the fixed-scope broker licence has plausible reusable tool value but lacks a repeat-payment mechanism and leaves founder transfer, AI task split, and acquisition route unspecified; free alternatives and the missing paid broker commitment limit commercial fit.
