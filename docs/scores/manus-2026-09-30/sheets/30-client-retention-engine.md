## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** A GoHighLevel (GHL) agency owner/the agency is both buyer and payer. The agency's client is the intended recipient of the white-labeled narrative and asset, not the buyer of this assessed offer.
- **Promise and delivery:** Each month, Jack supplies a white-labeled **“Your Marketing This Month”** narrative and one client-usable asset **per agency client**. The implied outcome is to help the agency make its marketing work visible when a client asks what it is paying for. The agency holds the GHL keys. Format, inputs, customization, and delivery workflow are not specified.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** The catalog hypothesis is about **$5–$10 per client per month**. The stated repeat mechanism is a monthly, per-client charge for a fresh narrative and asset. The entry expressly says this price is unvalidated and is not evidence of willingness to pay.
- **Founder role:** Jack supplies the narrative and asset. No production system, trained-operator role, AI role, quality-control process, or exception path is described.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Primary: Six Pack of Beer Desire** (catalogue fact: a fresh monthly unit). **Possible supporting shape: Tool in the Hand** (inference from “client-usable asset”), but the entry does not establish that the asset is immediately usable.
- **Material assumptions (labelled):**
  1. **A — Recurring-use assumption:** An agency with continuing client accounts has a recurring monthly need to explain visible marketing work; this supports the repeat-mechanism judgment only, not willingness to pay.
  2. **B — No hidden standardization assumed:** This assessment does **not** assume that the narrative/asset is a reusable template, AI-generated, or bespoke. That missing design fact causes bars 2, 9, and 10 to be unscorable.
  3. **C — No buyer acceptance assumed:** The reported agency churn/reporting pain is treated as context, not as evidence that agencies would buy this offer or renew it.
  4. **D — Killed-status interpretation:** The documented reason for killing the model—selling GHL agencies their own retention job—is treated as a design conflict for the assessed offer; it does not alter its status or substitute an improved offer.

## Result

- **Coverage /10:** 5/10
- **Concept-fit total /20 (blank if any U):**
- **Strong fits /10:** 1/10
- **Every zero and its conflict:**
  - **Bar 6 — No Brainer:** The documented kill reason says the offer sells GHL agencies their own client-retention job; this is a buyer-value/positioning conflict, not a proof gap.
  - **Bar 7 — C3PO:** The described core unit is recurring human work supplied by Jack, with no AI/human exception split; this conflicts with AI-core fulfillment.
- **Shortlist signal under rubric v1.1:** **No.** The assessment has five U scores and two model-conflict zeros; therefore it cannot qualify regardless of the blank total.
- **Summary:** The explicit monthly charge earns the sole strong fit, but the killed buyer-value conflict and human-core fulfillment are zeros; missing production, ownership, channel, use-journey, and scale facts leave five bars unscorable.
- **Assumptions that could change scores:** If the assessed version documented one standardized, low-support production unit with retained IP, an AI-led workflow, a channel with access/incentive, and self-serve use steps, bars 2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9, and 10 could be reassessed. Those are not facts of this catalogue version and are not incorporated into its scores.
- **Unresolved question:** **Could a $5–$10 per-client monthly asset be a standardized, self-serve product rather than Jack-supplied tailored service?**
- **Possible improvement (separate from assessed version):** Define a fixed reusable/AI production unit, ownership terms, self-serve agency workflow, and a credible buyer channel; first test whether agencies see it as a tool they deploy rather than outsourced retention. This is not a rescoring of entry 30 and does not revive its dead-but-ingredient status.
