## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** No single buyer is selected. The intended **payer** is a channel owner for the chosen micro-brand—illustratively a realtor, broker, community owner, course creator, or chamber. The likely **end user** is that payer's client, member, learner, or local-business audience, but it varies by unselected brand. This is a material unresolved design choice, not a claim of buyer access.
- **Promise and delivery:** At the factory level, the promise is to create small standalone brands around one problem. The stated process is **research → micro-brand → IP → distribution → systemize → hold/license/sell/kill**. No specific micro-brand's customer promise, fixed deliverable, licence rights, implementation, or support journey is defined in entry 38.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** The entry says **per-brand founding licences** and points to entries 14–15. Those related entries state an unvalidated founding-licence hypothesis of approximately **$497–$2,000** (with a possible **$97/month** only in entry 14). Entry 38 itself gives no factory-level price, renewal, multi-seat/right-to-use terms, or reason a payer would pay again; “portfolio economics” is not a repeat-payment mechanism.
- **Founder role:** ZedBiz is intended to be the holding/manufacturing company. The catalogued sequence includes systemizing, and the cited architecture anticipates documented production and operating systems with minimal founder dependency. It does **not** assign Jack, AI, staff, or partners concrete ongoing responsibilities for research, QA, channel acquisition, support, or brand operations.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Unselected / varies by brand.** The entry records “Shape: varies”; it does not identify a primary shape or a buyer experience for the factory itself. The illustrative referenced products may be tools, but their shapes cannot be imported into every potential micro-brand.

### Material assumptions

1. **Bounded interpretation — licence:** “Per brand founding licences” is read as a proposed licence of a completed micro-brand asset, not proof of bespoke factory work or a successful sale. The entry does not define the licence.
2. **Bounded interpretation — transferable asset:** A successfully operated micro-brand would retain its named IP, brand, customer/partner list, distribution relationships, and operating documentation. This supports bar 3 because those assets are expressly contemplated; it does not assume customers or revenue exist today.
3. **Bounded interpretation — shared factory:** The central factory could reuse some research, production, QA, and operating infrastructure across brands. This supports only a partial bar-10 inference because the cited source treats common production as something to test, not an established result.
4. **No gap-filling assumption:** No payer has made a paid commitment; no channel access, demand, renewal behavior, per-brand margin, fixed deliverable, AI/human task split, or end-user journey is assumed. Using any of those to turn a `U` into a numeric score would assess an improved invention rather than entry 38.

## Result

- **Coverage /10:** **3/10** (bars 3, 4, and 10 are numerically assessable).
- **Concept-fit total /20 (blank if any U):**
- **Strong fits /10:** **1/10** (bar 3).
- **Every zero and its conflict:** **None.** No bar is scored `0`: the limitations are missing essential design facts, not documented conflicts in the catalogued model.
- **Shortlist signal under rubric v1.1:** **No.** Seven bars are `U`, so there is no total and the all-ten-numeric, no-zero, seven-strong-fit, at-least-17/20 conditions cannot be met.
- **Assumptions that could change scores:** Selecting one fixed micro-brand, documentable licence/delivery economics, a repeat mechanism, a specific payer/channel route, and an AI/human production map could make bars 1, 2, and 5–9 assessable. Demonstrating that most factory production is common across brands could raise bar 10; showing irreducible brand-by-brand bespoke work could lower it.
- **Unresolved questions:** **Which one micro-brand has a named channel-owner payer with a paid, fixed-scope licence commitment, a repeated reason to renew or buy again, and a reachable acquisition channel—and what fixed AI-led delivery and customer journey does that licence contain?**

### Possible improvement — not part of the assessed catalogued version

Choose **one** micro-brand rather than a portfolio. Specify its channel-owner payer, end-user outcome, fixed licence rights and deliverables, price and renewal/repurchase trigger, acquisition route to the payer, AI-versus-human production/QA/support tasks, activation path, and per-unit cost assumptions. Obtain the screen's required paid fixed-scope commitment before treating the factory architecture as a validated business. This is a possible improvement only; it was **not** scored above.
