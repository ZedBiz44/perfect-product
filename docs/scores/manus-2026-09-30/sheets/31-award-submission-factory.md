## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** GHL agencies seeking awards/proof; the agency is both the intended user and payer. No end-client payer is specified.
- **Promise and delivery:** Convert an agency's raw GHL data into polished, white-labeled case studies and award packets for quarterly award occasions.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** A quarterly pack at approximately **$199–$499**, explicitly marked by the entry as an unstated, unvalidated guess. The only stated repeat cadence is quarterly; the model names no valid award-submission cycle that gives an agency a reason to buy the next pack.
- **Founder role:** Not specified. The word “fleet” describes conversion, but neither the entry nor its cited source assigns Jack, trained staff, or automation the ongoing work of data access, verification, design, support, or award handling.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Primary: Trophy**, as labeled in the entry (agency recognition/award). **Supporting: none specified.** The cited rejection says the award occasion is unsupported, so the primary shape is not credibly delivered by this version.
- **Material assumptions (labelled):**
  1. **Source fact applied to the catalogued award premise:** The cited source says GHL awards use GHL referral/affiliate data and have no submissions; it calls the award occasion “fake.” This assessment therefore treats that documented conflict as applying to the entry's intended GHL award packet, rather than substituting some unmentioned award program.
  2. **Design fact, not operating proof:** “Fleet converts raw data” is a planned reusable conversion mechanism. It is not evidence of viable data access, automation, margin, buyer demand, or successful output.
  3. **No silent salvage:** The cited source's churn-moment proof-asset factory is a salvage direction, not this Award-Submission Factory version, and is not used to repair any score.
  4. **No buyer behavior assumed:** The catalog price is treated as a hypothesis only; no willingness to pay, award demand, renewal, channel access, or measurable benefit is inferred from it.

## Result

- **Coverage /10:** 6
- **Concept-fit total /20 (blank if any U):**
- **Total explanation:** Intentionally blank because Bars 4, 7, 8, and 10 are **U**.
- **Strong fits /10:** 0
- **Every zero and its conflict:**
  - **Bar 1:** The described quarterly repeat relies on award submissions, while the cited source says the intended GHL awards have no submissions.
  - **Bar 5:** A Trophy cannot be delivered through an unsubmitable award packet.
  - **Bar 6:** The nonexistent award-submission occasion removes the proposed trigger/value relationship; the price is also only a guess.
  - **Bar 9:** The buyer cannot complete the promised award use case because there is no stated submission destination.
- **Shortlist signal under rubric v1.1:** **No.** Coverage is incomplete, the total is blank, there are four model conflicts, and there are no strong fits. This is not a status change or launch decision.
- **Assumptions that could change scores:** Evidence of a named, repeatable award program that accepts these agency-submitted GHL-data packets, plus a documented workflow, AI/human split, channel, and scale effect, could change the applicable bars. It would need to be assessed as evidence for this version; the cited churn-moment salvage is a different version.
- **Unresolved question:** Does any named award program genuinely accept a repeatable agency-submitted GHL-data packet, and would agencies pay $199–$499 quarterly to submit it?
- **Assessment summary:** The catalogued Award-Submission Factory is a dead-but-ingredient concept whose central award-submission occasion is contradicted by its cited source; it retains only partial factory/IP logic, while four essential operating and scale details remain unjudgeable.
