## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** The catalog names **local marketers, agencies, and capable local operators** as buyers. It implies that whichever of those purchasers selects a tool is also the payer, but does not designate one first payer for one named tool.
- **Promise and delivery:** Fixed, productized one-job kits for named local-marketing moments (examples: profile fixer, review-ask, reactivation dialer), intended to give competence on first use rather than coaching. Niche packs are proposed as a ladder. The entry does not specify kit contents, installation/use steps, support, or whether a tool is software, templates, or another format.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** **~$79–$249 per tool**; extra niche packs are the only stated additional-purchase mechanism. There is no subscription, renewal, replenishment, or documented reason a buyer needs a second tool at a particular interval.
- **Founder role:** Jack's 25 years of local-marketing experience is the stated source of the tools. The version is explicitly not coaching; ongoing production, QA, support, updates, and channel-partner responsibilities are unspecified.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Primary: Tool in the hand.** The stated experience is competence on first use. **Supporting shapes: none specified.**
- **Material assumptions (labelled):**
  - **A1 — reasonable operating assumption:** Each fixed kit can be delivered repeatedly without bespoke diagnosis, rebuilding, or routine founder fulfilment. This supports bars 2 and 4; it is not evidence of actual margin or support performance.
  - **A2 — reasonable scope assumption:** A buyer can use the same standard version of a tool without a different per-customer build. This supports the reuse finding in bar 2.
  - **Not assumed:** demand, willingness to pay, marketplace admission, agency resale/access, Facebook-group access, buyer results, a recurring need, or an AI/human task split. Those omissions remain limitations rather than being assumed away.

## Possible improvement — not part of the assessed version

Define and test **one** initial tool rather than the family: name its sole first payer, their moment of need and costly workaround; make the standard kit, prerequisites, first-use steps, support boundary, and price-to-payoff explicit; document which AI task produces, delivers, or supports each standard unit and which human only verifies exceptions; then secure a channel owner's incentive/access and state a scale flywheel distinct from the product catalog asset. This is a possible later design, **not evidence for or a change to the hopper version scored above**.
