## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** An existing relationship owner — named examples are agencies, chambers, associations, SaaS companies, franchise systems, employers, and credit unions — is both buyer and payer. Its customers, members, employees, franchisees, or subscribers are recipients/end users of the benefits.
- **Promise and delivery:** Centrally negotiate and maintain one reusable portfolio of tangible benefits, package it under a channel owner's brand, and let the owner make its existing relationship more visibly valuable through savings or useful access.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** **Not set.** The entry offers unselected possibilities (B2B licence, per-member economics, vendor funding, or a blend) but names no price, contract term, renewal trigger, or chosen payer flow. A repeat mechanism therefore cannot be judged.
- **Founder role:** **Not specified.** The described model has a central function to negotiate and maintain benefits, but neither entry nor cited source allocates that work between Jack, staff, partners, or systems.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Primary: Tool in the Hand** (declared by the entry): a relationship owner receives a packaged member-value tool. **Supporting: none established**; a recurring savings experience for recipients is possible but the benefit and redemption design are unspecified.
- **Material assumptions (labelled):**
  - **A1 — unverified buyer condition:** A relationship owner has an economically material retention, differentiation, or value-perception problem and will regard a benefits portfolio as a solution. The source frames this as a thesis, not a purchase result.
  - **A2 — design fact, not proven cooperation:** The catalogue version intends centrally negotiated benefits that can be branded for multiple owners; supplier willingness, rights to white-label, and redemption operations are unproved.
  - **A3 — material unknown:** No benefit catalogue, vendor economics, contract liability, integration/access method, support burden, or selected payment model is supplied. These omissions drive the U scores rather than a zero.

## Possible improvement — not scored and not part of the assessed version

Choose one payer segment and one concrete benefits portfolio; document supplier rights and economics, recipient access/redemption and support, a selected licence/per-member/vendor-funded payment and renewal trigger, the AI-versus-human operating split, and a non-founder-led route to that payer. Those decisions would make a later assessment possible; they do not change the **exploration** status or this score for the catalogued version.
