## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** The intended buyer is the existing audience owner: chiefly a small-to-mid paid Skool, membership, or community owner, with local-business coaching groups also named for Shovel. The intended payer is that owner/operator (or its operating business), assumed to control the community-content budget. The entry says only “same as Shovel owners”; it does not establish a particular owner has budget or will buy.
- **Promise and delivery:** Give the owner one small, fresh, useful weekly item they can share under their own name—one concept, 2–4 engagement posts, and a small supporting pack—then add a periodic report intended to benchmark outcome evidence and give a reason to stay. The documented Shovel delivery is a weekly email link plus a searchable vault; the higher-tier report’s data collection, cadence, contents, and delivery process are not defined.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** **Hypothesis, not a validated price:** Shovel base price plus about **$10–$30/month**, or an annual report upsell. The related Shovel concept proposes a $19–$29/month base subscription. A new weekly drop is the stated recurring reason to pay; the periodic report is intended as added retention/stickiness, not the main business.
- **Founder role:** In the Shovel pipeline, AI agents research, draft, adapt, design, quality-check, upload, and send; Jack selects the concept, reviews it, contributes practical judgment, and approves shipment. The core-drop estimate is 1–1.5 shared hours weekly for the first edition plus time per added edition. The role, data rights, and verification process for the outcome report are unspecified.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Primary: Six Pack of Beer Desire**—one small, fresh, useful weekly item that can be used immediately. **Supporting: Trophy**—the proposed benchmark/outcome evidence report. The Trophy component is conditional: no proprietary campaign-results dataset is demonstrated.

### Material assumptions

1. **Owner-budget assumption:** Paid community owners have authority and enough repeated pain around fresh member value, engagement, or churn to pay the proposed base price and report increment rather than use free AI, free templates, or PLR.
2. **Shared-edition assumption:** One niche edition can serve many owners with only light support and editing, so recurring production is shared rather than bespoke.
3. **Quality-transfer assumption:** The documented selection, review, and QC rules can preserve quality while Jack’s weekly judgment is trained or delegated rather than remaining a permanent bottleneck.
4. **Outcome-evidence assumption:** A lawful, comparable, sufficiently reliable source of outcome data can be obtained for the report without turning fulfilment into custom client work. This is not established by the assessed version.
5. **Channel assumption:** Affiliate, reseller, marketplace, sample-drop, or owner-community routes can reach the payer at an acceptable acquisition cost; no access is secured.

## Result

- **Coverage /10:** 10/10
- **Concept-fit total /20:** **15/20**
- **Strong fits /10:** **5/10** (bars 1, 5, 7, 9, and 10)
- **Every zero and its conflict:** **None.** This assessment identifies material limitations and unknown commercial proof, but no described-model conflict that warrants a zero.
- **Shortlist signal under rubric v1.1:** **No.** Although all bars are assessed and none is zero, the assessed version has 15/20 and five strong fits, below the required 17/20 and seven strong fits. This is not proof of demand, launch approval, or commercial authorization.
- **Assumptions that could change scores:** Evidence that owners repeatedly pay for the report because it changes a retention/content decision could raise bars 3 and 6. A defined lawful data source, data-quality system, and delivery cadence could strengthen bars 3, 4, 6, 7, 9, and 10. A partner route with demonstrated access and economics could strengthen bars 2 and 8. A manager-operated quality process could strengthen bar 4.
- **Unresolved question:** **What lawful, comparable outcome data can this version actually collect, and will a named paid community owner pay again for the resulting report rather than use free AI, templates, or their own engagement data?**

## Possible improvement — not part of the assessed version

Define an opt-in, privacy-safe outcome-data specification before presenting the report as a retention mechanism: which owner metrics are contributed, the comparison cohort and minimum sample rules, what recurring owner decision the report changes, and a standard automated collection/verification workflow. Pair it with a single compensated affiliate/reseller channel test. These are possible changes and tests only; they are **not** treated as features, evidence, or scores for the catalogued version assessed above.
