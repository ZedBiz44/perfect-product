## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** Agencies and local marketers with dormant client/customer lists are both the named buyers and intended payers. The end customer receiving reactivation messages is not the payer.
- **Promise and delivery:** A short, fixed reactivation-messages/workflow campaign pack intended to help an owner or agency obtain cash this week. The cited source describes a niche-written version as reactivating an old customer list and booking jobs within seven days. Pack format, installation, and support are not specified.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** Approximately **$99–$199 per campaign pack**; the catalog expressly labels that an unvalidated hypothesis. No subscription, renewal, campaign calendar, repeat purchase reason, or continuing one-time buyer flow is described.
- **Founder role:** Not specified for this product. Neither the entry nor its cited sources says who creates/updates packs, installs them, supports buyers, or handles exceptions.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Primary (declared): Harvest Gala** — the hoped-for short-window pile of cash/booked jobs. **Supporting (inference only): Tool in the Hand** — a fixed workflow could function as an operator tool; this is not a documented part of the offer.
- **Material assumptions (labelled):**
  - **A1 — reusable digital delivery (reasonable assumption, not a source fact):** The fixed messages/workflow can be sold as one standard digital pack rather than rebuilt for each purchaser. This supports only the partial Bar 2 assessment; acquisition, installation, and support costs remain unknown.
  - **A2 — buyer prerequisites (not assumed as satisfied):** A buyer must have a usable dormant list, authority to contact it, and a compatible way to run the workflow. The entry identifies the audience and lists but does not establish list quality, consent, compatibility, or implementation steps.
  - **A3 — scope boundary:** The broader agency shelf, retention-drop, and benchmark/data ideas in the cited Grok source are not treated as mechanisms of Cash This Week. Its recorded verdict instead calls this entry a basic reactivation plan and an ingredient.
  - **A4 — no demand/result assumption:** No willingness to pay, partner cooperation, bookings, cash result, or differentiation from existing GHL practice is assumed.

## Result

- **Coverage /10:** 5/10
- **Concept-fit total /20:**
- **Strong fits /10:** 0/10
- **Every zero and its conflict:** None. The recorded lack of differentiation is a meaningful limitation for Bars 5–6, but it does not alone establish a model conflict under the rubric.
- **Shortlist signal under rubric v1.1:** No. Five material bars are unjudgeable and there are no strong fits, so the all-numeric/no-zero/at-least-seven-2s/17-of-20 condition is not met.
- **Summary:** Five bars are numeric—each a partial fit—and five are U because the catalogued single-pack sketch omits repeat economics, an accumulating transferable asset, an operating/founder model, an AI task split, and an acquisition route. The stated generic-GHL conflict limits differentiation, but does not by itself prove a zero.
- **Assumptions that could change scores:** A documented repeat/renewal mechanism could resolve Bar 1; a named transferable asset that accumulates could resolve Bar 3; a system/staff operating model could resolve Bar 4; a core AI/human task split could resolve Bar 7; and a specific incentive-compatible channel could resolve Bar 8. Those would be a changed design, not facts of this assessment.
- **Unresolved question:** Why would a named agency or local marketer buy this undifferentiated $99–$199 pack again instead of using its existing GHL reactivation workflow?

## Possible improvement — not assessed version

Create and evaluate a **new version** only if it specifies a differentiated niche/trigger and repeat-use proposition; a bounded self-serve delivery and permissions journey; the AI production/support tasks and human exception role; and an incentive-compatible route to agency/local-marketer payers. It would also need to explain why the version is meaningfully better than the basic GHL reactivation workflow. None of those changes is credited in the scores above.
