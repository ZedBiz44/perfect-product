## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** A GHL/marketing agency is both buyer and payer, proposed to pay a flat monthly licence. Its local-business clients are downstream deployment targets, not documented buyers or payers of this offer.
- **Promise and delivery:** Twelve seasonal/local campaign kits per year, white-labelled for deployment across the agency's whole client base. The entry does not specify the kit format, integrations, client approvals, implementation steps, or promised client outcome.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** A flat **~$197–$497/month** licence for the stream of twelve annual kits. This is an unvalidated catalog hypothesis, explicitly not evidence of willingness to pay; term, renewal, cancellation, and usage rights are unstated.
- **Founder role:** Not stated. The record does not allocate annual kit creation, quality review, licensing/account administration, support, or agency acquisition between Jack, systems, AI, or staff.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **None stated.** The catalog Shape field is `—`. A campaign kit could be intended as a Tool in the Hand, but that is not a declared buyer experience and is not assumed for the score.

### Material assumptions

1. **Reusable-unit assumption (Medium):** Each campaign kit is a standard, reusable white-label asset rather than bespoke client work. This follows the flat licence and whole-client-base deployment design, but delivery format and support burden are not stated. If substantial adaptation is required, Bar 2 would weaken.
2. **Seasonal-use assumption (Medium):** The twelve-kit cadence is intended to give an agency a new use occasion through the year. It supports assessment of the designed repeat mechanism only; it is not evidence that agencies renew.
3. **No unlisted operating mechanism (High):** No AI workflow, manager, channel access, owned audience, or customer success process is assumed where the entry does not name one. Those omissions produce U scores where material.
4. **Status-conflict assumption (High):** The cited source's documented kill reason — generic commodity / a collection of kits offered in hope GHL agencies buy — is treated as a conflict in the described model. It is not overridden by the separate, unselected one-job shelf idea.

- **Shortlist signal under rubric v1.1:** **No.** Coverage is incomplete and the assessment contains zeros; it also falls short of the no-zero, seven-strong-fit, 17/20 condition.
- **Assumptions that could change scores:** A specified low-touch operating system could resolve Bar 4; a named AI production/support workflow could resolve Bar 7; and a genuine seller-to-agency channel could resolve Bar 8. A single, buyer-validated job and a clear deployment/outcome path could alter Bars 5, 6, and 9, but would be a different, improved design rather than evidence for this generic shelf.
- **Summary:** Wholesale Shelf has reusable, recurring licence mechanics, but its documented generic-commodity design conflicts with a clear satisfaction experience and no-brainer purchase; founder operations, AI task split, and route to agency payers remain unspecified.
- **Unresolved question:** Which specific agency job has a triggered, paid need for a non-generic kit, and through what repeatable channel can its payer be reached without Jack selling it personally?

### Possible improvement — not assessed and does not revive the killed version

Validate one named, high-friction agency job with its payer before constructing a catalogue; only after that, define a self-serve delivery path, bounded AI/human workflow, and a real seller-to-agency channel. This is a possible future direction, **not** a change to or re-score of Wholesale Shelf.
