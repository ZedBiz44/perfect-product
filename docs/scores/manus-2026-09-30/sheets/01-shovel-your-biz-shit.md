## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** Primary buyer/end user and payer are the same: a small-to-mid paid Skool, membership, or community owner/operator. Local-business coaching-group owners are included as an edition; large free Facebook AI-group owners are explicitly secondary and unproven. The owner posts the material to their own audience.
- **Promise and delivery:** Each week, give the owner one small, fresh, useful item to share under their own name: one niche-relevant concept, 2–4 ready engagement posts (including a comment-to-get post), and a small supplementary pack. It is delivered by weekly email link and a searchable vault; the owner reads, optionally edits, and posts it. Jack stays the quiet supplier rather than hosting the community.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** No price is approved. The original proposal is **$19–$29/month** (anchored at $27); the catalog also records a separate **~$29–$49/month founding** hypothesis. The intended repeat mechanism is a monthly subscription for a fresh, consumable weekly drop that handles the recurring “what useful thing do I give members this week?” job. A starter crate, annual plan, Pro tier, and white-label/agency tier are proposals, not part of this scored core offer.
- **Founder role:** Agents research, draft, angle, design, QC, upload, and send. Jack selects the concept, reviews/corrects it, adds practical experience where useful, spot-checks editions, and approves shipment. The source estimates 1–1.5 hours weekly for the first edition plus about 15 minutes per added edition. Owners—not Jack—post and interact with their members.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Primary: Six Pack of Beer Desire.** The designed unit is small, usable the same day, has a day-3 follow-up, then makes room for a fresh weekly unit. **Supporting: Tool in the Hand** for the owner’s ready-to-post concept, prompts, and supplementary asset.

### Material assumptions

1. **Production/quality assumption:** The named AI-agent pipeline can produce sufficiently current, source-checked, niche-appropriate drops, while Jack’s selection/review remains bounded rather than becoming per-customer fulfillment.
2. **Value assumption:** Paid audience owners experience the weekly blank-page/engagement job often enough, and regard curation, freshness, and niche fit as sufficiently better than free AI, free templates/PLR, or their own ideas to justify the proposed monthly price.
3. **Channel assumption:** A material number of the stated owners can be reached through owner communities, samples, affiliates/resell-rights partners, bundles, forums, marketplaces, or niche outreach without continuous founder-led selling; access and partner incentives are not yet secured.
4. **Adoption assumption:** An owner can use a drop with only light editing, post it manually despite platform limits, and obtain the intended useful/engagement experience without ZedBiz joining the group or providing ongoing support.
5. **Economics/asset assumption:** Digital delivery, agent use, review/QC, acquisition, support, licensing, and compliance costs fit beneath the low proposed price; the vault, IP, buyer list, and production system can legally be retained and transferred.

## Result

- **Coverage /10:** 10/10
- **Concept-fit total /20:** **17/20**
- **Summary:** 17/20 concept fit with seven strong fits and no zeros; no shortlist signal because the catalogued operating design still needs Jack’s routine weekly selection and quality review.
- **Strong fits /10:** 7/10
- **Every zero and its conflict:** **None.** No documented model conflict warrants a zero for the catalogued weekly-drop version; the unresolved issues are limitations and untested assumptions rather than contradictions.
- **Shortlist signal under rubric v1.1:** **No.** The arithmetic conditions are met (10 numeric scores, no zero, seven strong fits, 17/20), but founder constraints do not yet hold: the operating design still requires Jack’s routine weekly selection and quality review (bar 4 = 1). This is not approval or a launch recommendation.
- **Assumptions that could change scores:** If paid owners repeatedly choose the subscription over free alternatives, show use/renewal, and an affiliate/reseller channel becomes available, bars 1, 6, and 8 would be materially clearer. If quality requires substantial bespoke editing, owner support, or Jack review per customer/edition, bars 2, 4, 7, and 9 would weaken. If archives, feedback, and licenses cannot be transferred or do not improve future production, bar 3 would weaken; if scale adds management faster than shared production reduces costs, bar 10 would weaken.
- **Unresolved question:** Will small-to-mid paid audience owners repeatedly pay the unapproved ~$19–$49 monthly price for an original, useful, easy-to-share weekly drop instead of using free AI, free PLR/templates, or their own ideas?

### Possible improvement — not part of the assessed version

Validate the core with a paid sample/pre-sale and a defined owner-use/renewal measure; at the same time, document an editorial selection rubric and trained-editor/QC handoff that reduces Jack to true exceptions, and secure one incentive-aligned affiliate or reseller route. These are possible changes/testing steps, **not** evidence for or alterations to the catalogue version scored above.
