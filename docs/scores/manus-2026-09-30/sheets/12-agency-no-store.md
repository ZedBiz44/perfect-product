## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** The stated buyer is **agencies that turn away small jobs**. The entry also says end SMBs may receive the offer through the agency channel. It does **not** resolve whether the agency buys wholesale, resells a ZedBiz product, merely refers an end SMB buyer, or whether another party pays; therefore the payer is unknown.
- **Promise and delivery:** A catalog of fixed, below-agency-scope job products lets an agency say no cleanly while still offering a productized handoff. The cited design describes simple, self-serve business boxes, with the agency as retailer or referral partner and prospective GHL-triggered email, checkout, or delivery; the agency does not implement the box.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** **$29–$149 per job product**, explicitly an unvalidated price hypothesis. The assessed version specifies individual job-product purchases; it names no subscription, renewal, commission arrangement, or explicit repeat-payment mechanism. A repeat need from later below-scope jobs is an inference, not a documented mechanism.
- **Founder role:** Not specified. The source says an agency should not implement the box, but it does not allocate ZedBiz/Jack's product creation, updating, support, quality control, partner management, or exception work.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Primary: Tool in the hand** (declared in the entry). **Supporting: none stated.**
- **Material assumptions (labelled):**
  - **Assumption — repeat occasion:** A participating agency encounters more than one below-scope situation over time and would consider presenting a relevant fixed product again. This is plausible but untested and does not establish payment.
  - **Assumption — self-serve boundary:** The cited "business boxes" can be delivered and used without custom implementation or routine rescue work. The catalog entry does not specify individual boxes, contents, or support burden.
  - **Not assumed — channel access/incentive:** Agency cooperation is not assumed: the cited source records that agencies may build their own box, keep a lead that might later become valuable, or create a service-business burden if ZedBiz serves the small client.
  - **Not assumed — demand or economics:** No willingness to pay, agency take rate, conversion, support cost, margin, or recurring use is treated as proven.

- **Shortlist signal under rubric v1.1:** **No.** Four bars are U, the total is therefore blank, and the assessment has no strong-fit (2) scores; it cannot meet the no-zero/seven-strong-fit/17-of-20 condition.
- **Assessment summary:** This hopper distribution model has a plausible agency-channel and reusable fixed-product structure, but the catalogued version leaves the payer, specific jobs, founder operating model, AI/human split, and scale mechanism unresolved; the source also documents a direct agency-channel incentive conflict.
- **Assumptions that could change scores:** Resolving a named payer and merchant/referral arrangement could make bar 6 assessable and strengthen or weaken bars 1 and 8. Defining one fixed self-serve box, its support boundary, and its operating/AI workflow could make bars 2, 4, 5, 7, and 9 assessable differently. A documented scale mechanism separate from catalog ownership could make bar 10 assessable.
- **Unresolved question:** Who pays the $29–$149, under which resale/referral/checkout arrangement, and why would an agency repeatedly present the shelf rather than build the box or retain the lead?

## Possible improvement — not part of the assessed version

Specify one below-scope job box, its self-serve delivery and support boundary, the payer and agency compensation/lead-ownership rule, and an AI-plus-human operating split. Then establish a scale mechanism distinct from merely owning a larger catalog. This is a possible revision for later evaluation, **not** a claim about the current hopper entry or a status change.
