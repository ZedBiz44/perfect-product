## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** The named buyer is a **paid community owner** with lurkers, an empty room, or churn. The assessment treats that same owner as the **payer**: the entry states a monthly price to the buyer and identifies no agency, sponsor, or member as a separate payer. End users are the owner's members.
- **Promise and delivery:** Reframe the weekly Shovel drop as one fresh member-action unit — a mission, comment-word prompt, and homework — so members take an action and the owner has a next unit to reuse. The entry explicitly retains Shovel's weekly cadence. Its cited Shovel concept describes a shared, owner-ready digital drop; email/vault delivery, AI drafting, and the fuller supporting pack remain documented proposals rather than proof of operation.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** **~$29–$49/month**, expressly unvalidated. The repeat mechanism is a new weekly action unit that the owner can post and reuse each week, supporting a month-to-month subscription; no renewal, paid commitment, or retention result is recorded.
- **Founder role:** Jack is designed to be the quiet supplier, not the host: the cited Shovel workflow assigns AI agents research, drafting, design, QC, and shipping, while Jack selects and reviews the weekly concept, adds judgment where useful, and approves shipment. The documented estimate is roughly 1–1.5 hours weekly for the first edition, plus time per additional edition; no trained replacement editor is specified.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Primary — Six Pack of Beer Desire.** A fresh, small weekly action is meant to be immediately usable and renewed next week. **Supporting — Tool in the Hand:** the owner receives a ready-to-post member action. The satisfaction-shapes document itself gives a weekly mission/comment-word/homework unit for audience owners as the Shovel example; this does not establish actual use or satisfaction.

### Material assumptions

1. **A1 — shared unit, not bespoke work (reasonable operating assumption):** Within an edition, the same digital weekly unit is supplied to multiple subscribing owners. This is consistent with the cited Shovel design, which says editions are the same for buyers in a niche; custom per-owner writing would reduce bars 2, 4, 7, and 10.
2. **A2 — transferable IP is retained (reasonable operating assumption):** The supplier retains rights to the growing unit archive, editorial system, and subscriber relationship despite buyer-use licensing. If these rights are not retained or transferable, bar 3 would fall.
3. **A3 — routine support remains self-serve/exception-based (reasonable operating assumption):** Digital delivery and a clear use pattern avoid material per-owner support. The catalog entry does not specify support load; sustained hand-holding would weaken bars 2, 4, and 9.
4. **A4 — no demand, payment, channel access, or outcomes assumed:** The repository records no direct Shovel interviews, paid commitments, proof-gate completion, member-action use log, paid renewal, engagement lift, or retained-member result. These absences are uncertainty, **not** a model conflict.

- **Every zero and its conflict:** **None.** No described-model conflict is scored 0; the documented weaknesses are limitations or untested commercial facts, not proof of a conflict.
- **Shortlist signal under rubric v1.1:** **No.** All ten bars are numeric and none is zero, but the assessment has six rather than at least seven strong fits and totals 16 rather than at least 17.
- **Summary:** A well-specified recurring, AI-assisted shared-unit concept with credible reuse, asset, satisfaction, and scale mechanisms; its limiting issues are founder editorial dependence, unproven buyer economics, channel access, and owner-use friction rather than a documented model conflict.
- **Unresolved question:** Will a named paid community owner repeatedly pay **$29–$49/month** for a weekly member-action unit — and use it with less editing than free AI/PLR — rather than treat it as optional content?
