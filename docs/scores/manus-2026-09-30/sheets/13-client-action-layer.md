## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** The entry names course creators, coaches, SaaS companies, and agencies whose clients do not implement. It does **not** select one. The proposed payer is alternatively the upstream organization buying a white-label licence, or the end user buying a $29–$99 add-on; the actual payer is therefore unresolved.
- **Promise and delivery:** Productize the gap between buying and using with implementation/action packs bolted onto an existing course, retainer, or software product. The cited agency illustration is a fixed handoff box stating what the owner does this week, what a staff member says, what proof to look for, and when to stop and ask for help.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** Unvalidated hypothesis of a $97–$497/month white-label licence or a $29–$99 end-user add-on. A monthly licence is a proposed repeat charge, but the record does not state what makes a licensee renew after a fixed handoff action layer has been used.
- **Founder role:** Not specified for this design. The source frames ZedBiz as a prospective supplier, while explicitly warning that ensuring behavior change and follow-through could make ZedBiz the service provider again.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Primary: Tool in the hand.** A small, finished next-action box could make a client or agency operator capable at the handoff moment. No supporting shape is specified.
- **Material assumptions (labelled):**
  - **A1 — standardization:** The licensed layer is a fixed, reusable pack for a defined service line rather than custom implementation work for each agency or end client.
  - **A2 — renewal use:** A licensee has enough continuing client handoffs or changing action needs for a monthly licence to be useful after the first pack.
  - **A3 — self-serve behavior boundary:** The owner/client can act from the pack without ZedBiz repeatedly coaching, chasing, diagnosing, or implementing.
  - **A4 — transferable asset:** Seller-owned action-pack IP and licence relationships are assignable to a successor. Neither ownership nor transfer terms are stated.

Then specify which AI steps produce/maintain the shared pack and which human checks handle exceptions, and secure a channel with a reason to license it.
