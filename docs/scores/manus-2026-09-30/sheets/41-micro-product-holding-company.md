## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** The holding-company-level buyer/payer is **not specified**. The entry says “Portfolio model; end buyers vary by acquired product”; a seller of an acquired product may receive an acquisition payment or revenue share, while an acquired product's end buyer may pay separately. Neither buyer nor payer is named for a particular offer.
- **Promise and delivery:** Acquire or revenue-share with small, already-proven products that have real customers but weak marketing, distribution, automation, recurring economics, or product ladders; then apply ZedBiz packaging, channel distribution, automation, product ladders, and repeat economics while retaining winners as standalone micro-brands. No particular product, deliverable, or end-user outcome is selected.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** At holding-company level, the stated economics are acquisition or revenue share; **no retail price is set**. “Repeat economics” and product ladders are planned interventions, not a defined subscription, repeat purchase occasion, or continuing buyer flow for any acquired product.
- **Founder role:** Not specified. The entry assigns ZedBiz improvements but does not allocate acquisition selection, negotiation, integration, operating, support, or exception work between Jack, AI, systems, and staff.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Unspecified / varies by acquired product.** No primary or supporting satisfaction shape can be assigned to the catalogued holding-company structure itself.

### Material assumptions

1. **Acquisition-rights assumption:** For the bar-3 score, a winning acquisition conveys transferable rights to the brand, customer relationship, and relevant IP; a revenue-share-only deal could leave less ownership. This is a reasonable condition for the entry's stated “standalone micro-brands,” not a documented term.
2. **Product-quality assumption:** “Proven products” means they have real customers and useful outcomes, as the entry states; it does **not** establish recurring demand, margin, product-market fit after an ownership change, or buyer willingness to accept changes.
3. **No invented portfolio assumption:** No particular product category, end buyer, price, acquisition target, channel partner, AI workflow, or operating team is assumed where the entry leaves it open. Those omissions drive the U scores.
4. **Source-scope assumption:** The 2026-09-29 Z3 and key-info material establishes the exploration design and its intended levers; it does not establish a selected target, completed acquisition, paid result, or approved launch.

- **Concept-fit total /20 (blank if any U):**
- **Strong fits /10:** 1
- **Every zero and its conflict:** None. No bar is scored 0; the unresolved bars lack essential model details rather than conflict with the criteria.
- **Shortlist signal under rubric v1.1:** No. Coverage is incomplete, so there is no total; it also does not meet the no-zero, at-least-seven-strong-fits, and at-least-17/20 conditions.
- **Assessment summary:** The catalogued version has a credible asset-accumulation thesis—buy or participate in proven products and keep successful micro-brands—but it is a portfolio structure without a selected product, payer, offer, price, AI workflow, channel, customer journey, or scale mechanism. It therefore earns one strong fit and nine U scores rather than failures.
- **Assumptions that could change scores:** Selecting a specific acquired product and documenting ownership rights, end buyer/payer, price and repeat mechanism, reusable delivery economics, operating/task split, channel incentives, buyer journey, and cross-portfolio scale mechanism could make most U bars assessable. Those would be a later version, not evidence for this score.
- **Unresolved question:** Which specific proven digital product, end buyer/payer, price and repeat mechanism, channel, ownership terms, AI/human work split, and customer journey will the holding company use?
- **Possible improvement (separate from assessed version):** Before treating this as a candidate offer, define one acquisition screen and one target archetype: transferable IP/customer rights, a named payer and retail offer, existing repeat or renewal reason, a verifiable channel-owner incentive, a self-serve customer journey, a bounded AI/human operating map, and one shared portfolio-scale mechanism. This is a possible improvement only and is not credited to the catalogued version.
