## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** A marketing agency or GHL-style agency owner who struggles to get clients is both the buyer and the proposed payer. The agency's **prospect** receives the free win but is not described as the payer.
- **Promise and delivery:** Give the agency one packaged, tested, niche-specific prospect-facing free win, with evidence of what works—e.g., a Google-profile improvement for dentists. The agency, not Jack, sets it up for the prospect and uses the free setup to start a sales conversation.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** The catalog lists approximately **$99–$249 one-time per niche pack**, **or a low monthly refill of new niche wins**. The monthly refill is the stated repeat mechanism; whether agencies find it different enough from free audits to renew is unvalidated.
- **Founder role:** The source explicitly keeps Jack out of the agency's sales work. It does **not** state who researches/tests wins, produces and refreshes packs, verifies evidence, supports agencies, or manages the operation.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Primary: Tool in the hand.** The agency obtains a ready prospecting tool. **Supporting: none specified** in the catalogued version.
- **Material assumptions (labelled):**
  - **A1 — reuse and implementation:** A fixed niche pack can be delivered to more than one agency and an agency has the skill, prospect access, and permissions to implement its promised free win without Jack doing bespoke fulfilment. This supports the partial B2 judgment only; the actual buyer path remains insufficiently specified for B9.
  - **A2 — refill demand:** Agencies continue to need new prospect conversations and regard new niche wins as sufficiently differentiated from their existing free audits to consider a monthly refill. This supports B1; it is not evidence of renewal or willingness to pay.
  - **A3 — transferable IP:** The supplier owns or can transfer the niche-pack catalogue and its associated evidence. This supports B3; ownership rights, result-data rights, and buyer demand for the asset are not documented.
  - **Not assumed to avoid unknowns:** No AI/human production split, acquisition channel, exact implementation workflow, result-data capture, or scale advantage is supplied for this version. Those omissions produce U scores rather than being filled in.

## Possible improvement — not part of the assessed version

Specify a constrained pack workflow (agency actions, permissions, expected time, and prospect handoff), an AI-versus-human production and verification system, a repeatable self-serve or partner buyer channel, and an opt-in result-data mechanism that strengthens later packs. This would be a changed version, not evidence that the current hopper entry works.
