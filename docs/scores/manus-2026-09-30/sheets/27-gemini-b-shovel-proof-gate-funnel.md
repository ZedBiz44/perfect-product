## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** The catalog does not select one. It names (1) a prospect for ZedBiz offers, for whom the gate is free and a failure might buy DIY, or (2) another operator who might buy the gate. The proposed payments are therefore either a failed prospect's DIY purchase or an operator's one-time purchase; no payer is defined for the free gate itself.
- **Promise and delivery:** An automated GoHighLevel assessment is to grade business stability from exploration gaps, harshly reject unqualified prospects, route failures to Sponsor Layer DIY, and route passes to consulting. The source identifies the latter as the original path, not a finished self-serve product.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** Free gate; failure-route DIY at approximately **$7–49**; a productized gate for operators at approximately **$99–299**. These are unvalidated catalog hypotheses. No subscription, renewal, repeat purchase occasion, or continuing one-time-buyer flow is specified.
- **Founder role:** Not specified. Routine gate automation could be system-operated, but the original pass path is high-ticket consulting/core ZedBiz services. The current-direction record says that consulting path conflicts with the no-retainers/client-work constraint; no transferrable delivery or staffing system is described.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Primary stated shape: Tool in the hand.** No supporting shape is stated. At most, the instant score can orient a prospect; the catalog does not specify a completed capability or outcome after rejection/routing.
- **Material assumptions (labelled):**
  - **A1 — Automation boundary:** “Automated GHL assessment” means the screening questions and routing can execute routinely without a person operating each screen; it does **not** establish an AI workflow.
  - **A2 — Reuse boundary:** One gate configuration can be reused for multiple screens or operators, but the catalog does not establish whether either buyer path shares a single configuration or needs bespoke diagnosis.
  - **A3 — Sponsor Layer boundary:** The entry’s “Sponsor Layer DIY” is only a proposed destination. The cited Sponsor Layer note says it is a possible later funding layer, not the current product answer; this assessment does not assume a sponsor, DIY contents, or sponsor revenue.
  - **A4 — Demand and economics:** No willingness to pay, budget, conversion, acquisition cost, support cost, or consulting capacity is assumed. Price ranges are design facts, not commercial proof.

- **Assumptions that could change scores:** Selecting one payer and standalone offer could resolve Bars 6 and 8; defining a renewable/repeat use could change Bar 1; specifying owned, accumulating assessment IP/data could resolve Bar 3; an explicit AI/human workflow could resolve Bar 7; and a documented self-serve output/journey could resolve Bar 9. Those would be a changed specification, not evidence for this version.
- **Unresolved question:** Which single payer buys which defined post-gate outcome on what recurring or repeat-payment model, rather than a free funnel into consulting?
- **Possible improvement (not scored; separate from assessed version):** If reconsidered as a new version, choose one buyer and make the gate a fixed self-serve diagnostic product with a defined output, ownership/data policy, AI-plus-human exception workflow, repeat occasion, and channel. Do not treat this possible redesign as a result for the catalogued funnel or as approval to reopen consulting.
