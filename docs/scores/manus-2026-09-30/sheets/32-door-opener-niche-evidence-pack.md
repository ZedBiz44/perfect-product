## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** A marketing agency serving a named local-business niche (examples in the entry: dentists or trades) is both buyer and payer. The agency’s prospect is the downstream recipient of the agency’s free win, not the payer.
- **Promise and delivery:** A per-niche pack gives the agency a prospect-facing free-win script/checklist plus niche-specific proof points about what works. The agency uses the pack to offer a prospective client a free setup or improvement and begin a sales conversation; Jack does not perform the agency’s sales work. The example is a Google-profile improvement for dentists.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** The catalogued price is **about $149–$299 per niche pack**. It explicitly labels this an **unvalidated catalog hypothesis**, not willingness-to-pay evidence. No subscription, refill cadence, renewal, licensing model, or explicit repeat-payment reason is stated. A possible multi-niche purchase is only an inference, not a specified mechanism.
- **Founder role:** The entry specifies that the agency runs the prospect-facing win and Jack does not take its sales work. It does **not** say who researches, verifies, refreshes, packages, supports, or quality-controls the niche evidence; no trained-operator or manager handoff is described.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Primary: Tool in the hand.** The intended agency experience is immediate competence in making a bounded prospect offer. **Supporting: none specified.**
- **Material assumptions (labelled):**
  - **A1 — reusable, non-exclusive delivery:** The pack can be delivered as a reusable digital product to more than one agency in the same niche. The entry describes reusable templates, but does not state delivery or licensing terms. This affects bars 2 and 10.
  - **A2 — bounded agency implementation:** An agency can use the script/checklist in its ordinary prospecting workflow without material custom help from Jack. This affects bars 5 and 9; the hopper note expressly leaves custom-fulfilment feasibility unresolved.
  - **A3 — one client makes the price attractive:** A buyer regards one incremental agency client as sufficiently valuable that $149–$299 is small by comparison. This is a value hypothesis, not an observed purchase, and affects bar 6.
  - **A4 — evidence is lawful and decision-relevant:** Public or licensed niche evidence can credibly substantiate the proof points. The repository explicitly says no proprietary campaign-results dataset is demonstrated; no outcome-evidence process or access is specified. This affects bars 3, 5, and 6.
