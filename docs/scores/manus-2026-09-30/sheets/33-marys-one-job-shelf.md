## Product and assumptions

- **Buyer and payer:** The stated buyer is “operators who buy at midnight (blunt buyers),” with agencies/chambers only a later possible distributor. **No distinct payer is named.** The only supportable reading is a direct-paying operator purchasing an individual kit for $29–$79; that reading is an assumption, not a documented budget or buyer segment.
- **Promise and delivery:** A small, “ruthless” catalog of job-labeled, self-serve one-job kits rather than guru-led offers; the entry does **not** name the initial job, kit contents, prerequisites, or the specific completed outcome. Delivery is stated as self-serve, not further specified.
- **Price hypothesis and repeat-payment mechanism:** $29–$79 **per kit**, explicitly an unvalidated catalog hypothesis. The design says to add kits only after demand. It specifies neither a subscription nor why one purchaser returns for a different kit.
- **Founder role:** The source intends “no discovery calls, no Jack” in customer delivery. It does not document who produces, verifies, refreshes, supports, or selects kits in steady operation.
- **Primary and supporting satisfaction shapes:** **Primary: Tool in the hand** (the entry’s declared shape). **Supporting: none specified.** A practical first-use competence experience cannot be evaluated until a particular job and kit are specified.

### Material assumptions

1. **Direct-payer assumption:** An operator, rather than an agency/chamber, pays the listed per-kit price. The entry does not state this separately.
2. **Standardized-digital assumption:** “Self-serve” means a reusable, standardized kit with no routine custom fulfillment. If a kit needs diagnosis, installation, or bespoke work, bars 2, 4, and 9 would weaken or become unjudgeable.
3. **Directory-fit assumption:** Jack’s “own directories” are accessible for placement, as Mary proposes; neither directory audience, traffic, nor fit with the vague buyer is documented. This limits bar 8.
4. **No-repeat assumption:** A growing catalog does not itself prove that a buyer has repeated, distinct job triggers. The cited shelf source explicitly leaves that question unresolved.
5. **No AI-workflow assumption:** No AI/human production, delivery, review, or support split is stated. It must not be inferred from the general project direction.

## Result

- **Assessment summary:** The catalogued version is a potentially transferable shelf structure, not yet a sufficiently specified product: its strongest documented feature is the accumulating catalog/list/history asset, while its buyer, first job, repeat rationale, AI workflow, usable outcome, and scale mechanism remain unknown.
- **Coverage /10:** 4/10 (numeric assessments at bars 2, 3, 4, and 8)
- **Concept-fit total /20 (blank; U at bars 1, 5, 6, 7, 9, and 10):**
- **Strong fits /10:** 1
- **Every zero and its conflict:** **None.** The related **Wholesale Shelf** remains killed as a generic commodity proposal, but the entry and its cited source expressly distinguish it from this hopper structure; that documented conflict is not a zero for entry 06.
- **Shortlist signal under rubric v1.1:** **No.** Coverage is incomplete, the total is blank, and the assessed version does not meet the no-zero/all-numeric/at-least-seven-strong-fits/17-of-20 condition.
- **Assumptions that could change scores:** A specified standardized first kit, a named direct payer and trigger, a documented owned-channel audience, a repeat/cross-sell mechanism, and an explicit AI/human workflow could resolve the U bars. They are not credited here because they are not part of the catalogued version.
- **Unresolved question:** Which specific one-job kit solves a named, urgent buyer problem well enough that the same buyer will buy again—and through which proven-fit channel will they find it?

### Possible improvement — not part of the assessed version

Define one initial kit with its exact job, direct payer, delivered components, prerequisites, first-use result, existing workaround/spend, and $29–$79 price logic; then document a specific matching directory or distributor route, the AI-versus-human production/support split, and the mechanism by which later kits create repeat purchases or lower acquisition cost. This is a possible improvement only and does not alter the above scores or the hopper status.
