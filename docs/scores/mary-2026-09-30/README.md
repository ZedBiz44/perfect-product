## Scoring stance (calibration)

How I used the scale, so the numbers can be compared honestly with Grok's and Cody's:

- **2** requires evidenced fit: a named recurring/repeat mechanic (bar 1), a documented reusable design with zero marginal cost per sale (bar 2), a documented founder-off-stage hard rule (bar 4), or a documented trivially-easy value path (bar 9). Unbuilt ideas can earn 2s on design bars; they cannot earn them on buyer-behavior bars.
- **1** requires an evidenced partial fit from repo docs: named-but-unbuilt assets (bar 3), founder-adjacent weekly production (bar 4), proposed-but-unproven automation or reusable pipelines (bars 2/7), thin or optional payment mechanics (bar 1).
- **0** only for documented conflicts: an explicitly ruled-out buyer (bar 8, #28), a community model whose social engine demonstrably failed (bar 4, #18/#19), a funnel into ruled-out consulting (bar 4, #20), writing-dependence and non-automatable judgment (bars 4/7, #29), a killed value proposition (bar 6, #30/#31/#32), a cost center with no payment mechanic by design (bar 1, #17).
- **U** for everything needing buyer-behavior evidence: satisfaction (bar 5), willingness to pay (bar 6), AI doing the work (bar 7), route to buyers (bar 8), and scale advantages (bar 10) - unless the record documents it for that specific offer. A named shape, a proposed channel, or general AI capability is not evidence.

Where this lands vs the others: stricter than Grok on bars 7/8/9/10 (Grok sometimes scored 1 on proposed-but-unbuilt mechanics; I keep those U unless the design itself evidences them) and on giving 2s to unheld assets; more willing than Cody to score 1/2 on documented design properties (Cody left most design bars U; I score the design where the entry or repo docs describe it specifically, e.g. self-serve delivery on bar 9).
