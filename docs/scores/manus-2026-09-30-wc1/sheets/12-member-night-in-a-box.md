## Assessment frame
- **Buyer and payer:** A membership or chamber owner paying for a shared event format for its own member event.
- **Offer and delivery:** A self-serve shared digital event pack: theme, mission, guest prompt, photo moment, invitations and run-of-show; the buyer's staff or VA prepares and hosts the night.
- **Price hypothesis:** $99 per event pack in the WC1 scenario; the recorded, unvalidated range is about $49–$149 per pack and $199–$399 for a seasonal pack.
- **Repeat-payment mechanism:** Fresh calendar slots and seasonal bundles can prompt further purchases, although an owner can reuse a successful pack without paying again.
- **Founder role:** Jack supplies occasional product input (assumed 0–2 hours weekly) but neither hosts events nor performs buyer-specific event logistics; a transferable operator maintains packs.
- **Primary satisfaction shape:** Event Excitement — anticipation before the member night, a shared peak activity and photo moment, then event afterglow.
- **Founder fit:** holds

## Material assumptions
- Membership and chamber owners have recurring event-calendar needs and $99 is small relative to saved staff planning effort or engagement value.
- The pack remains a common, rights-owned digital format with no buyer-specific redesign or managed event service.
- The buyer's staff or VA can handle invitation, attendance, venue/material and hosting logistics as its normal role.
- AI creates the routine pack components and a human's run-of-show testing is once per shared edition rather than per buyer.
- Chamber service newsletters or community consultants can be incentivized to distribute packs, despite no partner agreement.
- Owners need new themes often enough to purchase fresh packs or seasonal bundles rather than recycle prior formats.

## Sensitivities
- If owners recycle a single popular event repeatedly, repeat revenue is materially weaker than the calendar-and-seasonal-pack scenario.
- At the stated price, paid acquisition or a substantial reseller share could turn a reusable pack into a weak-margin product.
- If attendance or member engagement is the buyer's primary success metric, the purchaser retains risks the pack cannot control.
- Any drift to Jack hosting or to custom event design would weaken founder independence, reuse and AI leverage.
- The strong C3PO assessment depends on human review staying once per shared edition rather than becoming routine fulfillment.

## Improvements separate from score
- Clarify the refresh logic that distinguishes a new purchasable event edition from a reusable prior pack.
- Specify fixed pack contents, preparation time, required materials and a staff checklist to bound buyer effort and support.
- Define a channel economics and rights model that states reseller incentives, allowed reuse and ownership of event assets.
- Make the AI-to-editor workflow explicit, including the standard for a shared run-of-show review.

## Unresolved questions
- Which membership or chamber-owner segment has both a recurring event obligation and a discretionary budget for a $99 format?
- Does the pack save enough planning time or improve enough member participation to beat free ideas and generic AI outputs?
- Will chamber service newsletters or community consultants carry the offer, on what economics and with what reach?
- How much local adaptation do buyers require before an event feels appropriate for their members?
- Can the seller retain transferable rights in all format, visual and prompt assets while customers execute them?

## Overall interpretation
Under WC1, this is a credible reusable B2B event-kit concept with clear Event Excitement, a transferable catalogue and an operating model that keeps Jack off stage. Its principal concept-fit weaknesses are limited inherent renewal, buyer-side execution and attendance risk, a less-than-obvious $99 value case against free ideas, and an unconfirmed route to payers; it is not evidence of demand, margins or partner access.

## Scope note
This is an independent concept-fit assessment of the stated WC1/WC1.1 scenario. It is not evidence of demand, customer results, margins, partner access, or a launch decision. Existing status remains unchanged.
