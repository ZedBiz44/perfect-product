## Assessment frame
- **Buyer and payer:** A course creator with one repeatable marketing course pays for a white-label action layer; the creator's learners use the prompts and reminders.
- **Offer and delivery:** A $197/month white-label implementation layer maps approved course lessons into AI-drafted action tasks and reminders. The creator supplies an outline, reviews the map, publishes it, and course staff—not ZedBiz—support learners.
- **Price hypothesis:** $197/month, conditional on the layer producing course implementation beyond the creator's existing worksheets and reminders.
- **Repeat-payment mechanism:** The creator keeps a monthly licence while running cohorts and needs prompts/tasks refreshed or kept aligned as course delivery changes.
- **Founder role:** Jack is assumed to provide occasional product input (0–2 hours/week); an operator manages course mappings and support, while course staff handle learners.
- **Primary satisfaction shape:** Tool in the hand: a learner receives a concrete next task and experiences completing the first course action; the creator sees learners use what was sold.
- **Founder fit:** conflict

## Material assumptions
- A course creator with recurring cohorts will pay $197/month for implementation support that is materially better than worksheets, reminders, or free AI.
- AI can reliably draft useful tasks and reminders from an approved lesson map, with human verification limited to shared mappings and exceptions.
- Course mapping and later lesson-alignment work can be bounded enough for worthwhile licence margins and delegated operator work.
- Course-platform implementers can be a repeatable reseller route with enough incentive to include the layer despite no stated partner access or terms.
- Shifting per-course mapping to an operator does not resolve the current direction's prohibition on custom client work as the core business.

## Sensitivities
- The assessment weakens if the target course is evergreen with no recurring cohorts or meaningful task updates.
- Margins and independence weaken if accurate course mapping or exceptions require senior bespoke judgment for each creator.
- No-Brainer fit depends on measured implementation improvement relative to existing low-cost alternatives.
- Buyer acquisition depends on a platform-implementer route that is proposed, not secured.

## Improvements separate from score
- Constrain the offer to a standardized course architecture and a bounded mapping template rather than open-ended implementation work.
- Define an observable implementation outcome and a comparison against the creator's existing worksheets/reminders.
- Specify the reseller workflow, incentive, and mapping responsibility before treating platform implementers as a dependable channel.
- Separate reusable cross-course action-pattern IP from creator-owned lesson content in the operating and contract design.

## Unresolved questions
- Will course creators repeatedly pay $197/month for this layer rather than use existing worksheets, reminders, or free AI?
- What standard course format keeps initial mapping, updates, and exceptions from becoming custom client-service work?
- Can AI task drafts meet quality requirements with only shared human verification?
- Which course-platform implementer has both the incentive and operational capacity to resell the layer?
- Who owns, can transfer, and can reuse the course-specific maps when a creator leaves?

## Overall interpretation
The WC1 scenario has a credible recurring licence, AI-centered shared production, and clear learner tool-in-the-hand satisfaction. Its fit is constrained by course-specific mapping and maintenance, unproven incremental buyer value, and an unproven reseller route. Most importantly, its operational core remains customized creator implementation work, which conflicts with the current no-custom-client-work founder constraint even if Jack's direct hours are delegated.

## Scope note
This is an independent concept-fit assessment of the stated WC1/WC1.1 scenario. It is not evidence of demand, customer results, margins, partner access, or a launch decision. Existing status remains unchanged.
