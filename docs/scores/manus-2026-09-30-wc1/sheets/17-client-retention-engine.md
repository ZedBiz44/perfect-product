## Assessment frame
- **Buyer and payer:** GoHighLevel (GHL) agency owner, paying the proposed $8 per agency client per month.
- **Offer and delivery:** A monthly white-labeled 'Your Marketing This Month' narrative plus one client-usable asset, drafted from client exports; the agency retains its keys and approves the output before sending it to its client.
- **Price hypothesis:** $8 per agency client per month in the WC1 scenario (within the recorded unvalidated ~$5–$10/client/month range).
- **Repeat-payment mechanism:** The payment occasion recurs with each monthly client-reporting cycle for as long as the agency retains client accounts.
- **Founder role:** Proposed product oversight only, assumed at 0–2 hours per week; an operator handles accounts and exceptions while agency staff approve client narratives.
- **Primary satisfaction shape:** Six Pack of Beer Desire: recurring monthly relief from part of the reporting/presentation task, weakened because the recipient may receive another generic report rather than new useful value.
- **Founder fit:** conflict

## Material assumptions
- Agencies have a recurring enough reporting need and perceive a distinct benefit worth $8 per client per month despite already performing client-retention and reporting work.
- Client exports can be connected, mapped, attributed, and checked reliably enough for a white-labeled monthly narrative.
- A trained operator and routine reviewers can own account exceptions and accuracy checks, leaving Jack at the proposed 0–2 hours per week.
- Reporting-tool educators would have sufficient incentive and access to refer agencies, although no referral arrangement is evidenced.
- Agency approvals and permitted use of client data can support the delivery process; underlying customer data and relationships remain agency/client-controlled.

## Sensitivities
- If standardized inputs and reliable attribution reduced account-specific review to exceptions, bars 2, 7, 9, and 10 would improve; if not, low-price human fulfillment dominates them.
- If agencies demonstrated that this exact monthly output prevented churn or improved client-perceived value beyond built-in reporting, bars 5 and 6 would improve; the current record preserves the opposite objection.
- A channel owner with a clear economic incentive and demonstrable payer access would strengthen bar 8; a referral share alone does not establish this.
- The extent of transferable value depends on contractual rights to templates, account relationships, and any permissioned operating data.

## Improvements separate from score
- A materially different design would need a standardized, self-serve data and exception path so routine agency/client-specific accuracy work is not the product's core fulfillment.
- A distinct, buyer-visible outcome tied to a hot moment would be needed to avoid asking agencies to repurchase their existing retention role; the related Save Kit is a separate hopper concept, not an improvement credited here.
- A viable distributor proposition would need a reason for reporting-tool educators or another channel owner to carry the offer despite competing reporting options.

## Unresolved questions
- What specific output or measured outcome would make an agency value this separately from GHL reporting, its own staff, or a generic AI summary?
- How much human time per client per month is required for data mapping, attribution disputes, factual correction, and agency approval?
- Can the necessary exports and permissions be obtained consistently without fragile integrations or client-data restrictions?
- Which channel owner can repeatedly reach qualified GHL agency payers, and what proven incentive would make that owner promote this offer?
- Which templates, data rights, and account relationships would legally transfer to a future owner?

## Overall interpretation
This WC1 model has a clear recurring payment mechanism and modest reusable template leverage, but its low-priced monthly delivery retains material account-specific data and accuracy work. More importantly, the recorded objection is structural: it sells GHL agencies a version of their own client-retention/reporting job without a distinct triggered outcome. It can be operator-run under the stated assumption, but it conflicts with the current no-custom-client-work core constraint. The recorded dead-but-ingredient status is therefore consistent with this concept-fit assessment and is not changed.

## Scope note
This is an independent concept-fit assessment of the stated WC1/WC1.1 scenario. It is not evidence of demand, customer results, margins, partner access, or a launch decision. Existing status remains unchanged.
