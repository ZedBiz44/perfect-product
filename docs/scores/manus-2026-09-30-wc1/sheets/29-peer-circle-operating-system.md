## Assessment frame
- **Buyer and payer:** Host product: an existing group, membership, chamber, or other community owned by the buyer. The group/community owner is the B2B payer and licenses the operating system at the proposed $99/month; its members receive the peer-circle experience.
- **Offer and delivery:** A recurring licence to shared peer-circle prompts, missions, and scoreboards. ZedBiz supplies the facilitation IP and bounded product support; the owner's staff or VA configures circles and facilitates the member-facing room, with no Jack-hosted community.
- **Price hypothesis:** $99/month per community, unvalidated. The price must beat free prompt sources and the owner's current staff-led engagement practice by reducing the effort of inventing and structuring peer interaction.
- **Repeat-payment mechanism:** Recurring circles need fresh missions, prompts, and facilitation materials, creating a continuing licence reason rather than a one-time playbook download.
- **Founder role:** Occasional product input only (working assumption: 0–2 hours/week); a product operator maintains formats and bounded support, while buyer staff run the rooms. Jack neither hosts nor serves as the community mayor.
- **Primary satisfaction shape:** Belonging without hosting: members can experience peer connection through shared missions while the buyer owns the room and Jack is absent from daily social facilitation.
- **Founder fit:** holds

## Material assumptions
- Existing community owners have staff capacity and a sufficiently persistent engagement problem to pay $99/month rather than use free prompts or their existing practices.
- Fresh missions and scoreboards retain perceived value after the first months, rather than becoming a reusable playbook the owner can run without renewing.
- AI can draft usable shared mission variants and summaries, with human review remaining edition-level rather than becoming bespoke per-community production.
- Community managers and membership consultants will find a reseller margin worthwhile and view the kit as complementary to, rather than competitive with, their service.
- ZedBiz owns the facilitation IP and can transfer licence relationships or assignable contracts; permissioned aggregate feedback is available if it is used to improve formats.

## Sensitivities
- A move from fixed shared formats to per-community diagnosis, moderation, or bespoke mission design would weaken bars 2, 4, 7, and 9.
- If group owners lack existing staff or an active membership, they must first create the social operating capacity, weakening bars 5, 6, and 9.
- If resellers cannot earn enough margin or view the OS as competitive, the named route to buyers remains a founder-sales burden, weakening bar 8.
- If missions are readily substituted by free AI or PLR, both renewal and the $99 value proposition weaken.

## Improvements separate from score
- Define a fixed onboarding/configuration checklist and explicit support boundary so facilitation does not become a hidden service engagement.
- Specify a measurable owner-facing job and trigger, such as a recurring member-engagement gap or planned peer-circle cohort, instead of the broad desire for peer energy.
- Package a demonstrable first-cycle outcome and renewal cadence that distinguish the licence from a static prompt library.
- Set a reseller offer, minimum margin, and non-competition positioning before relying on consultants or managers as a channel.

## Unresolved questions
- Which category of group owner has both a repeated peer-engagement job and a real budget for a $99/month facilitation licence?
- What evidence would show that fresh formats materially improve participation or retention relative to free AI-generated prompts and current staff practice?
- How much configuration, moderation, and support does one buyer actually need before the operating system becomes custom work?
- Can a reseller channel reach these owners without Jack becoming the continuing salesperson?
- What rights and consent structure would permit ownership and useful aggregation of participation feedback?

## Overall interpretation
WC1 is a credible B2B2C ingredient whose fixed-format licence avoids the rejected Jack-as-host model and has strong reuse, AI-assisted shared production, transferable IP, and scale logic. Its decisive weaknesses are commercial rather than mechanical: belonging is co-produced by the buyer's staff and members, the $99 renewal value must overcome free alternatives, and the proposed reseller route is not established. This is a conditional concept-fit assessment, not evidence of demand, channel access, margins, or operating results.
