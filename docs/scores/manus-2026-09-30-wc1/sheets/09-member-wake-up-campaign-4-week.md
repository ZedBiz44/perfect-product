## Assessment frame
- **Buyer and payer:** A paid-community owner serving local-business people, marketers, or practical operators; the owner, rather than the members, pays US$129 for the fixed campaign.
- **Offer and delivery:** A standardized, owner-run four-week Return–Complete–Prove–Continue campaign with posts, missions, resources, and tracking summaries. ZedBiz prepares and supports the shared pack; the buyer schedules posts and facilitates its own members.
- **Price hypothesis:** US$129 for the four-week campaign, within the recorded US$99–$149 founding-pilot band; this is an unvalidated hypothesis, not evidence of willingness to pay.
- **Repeat-payment mechanism:** A new member cohort or a later quiet period can create another fixed-campaign occasion. A monthly subscription or automatic renewal is not inherent in the design.
- **Founder role:** Under WC1, an operator prepares and supports the standardized pack, buyer staff run the room, and Jack supplies occasional product oversight assumed at 0–2 hours per week rather than member facilitation.
- **Primary satisfaction shape:** Harvest Gala: after four scheduled weeks, the owner can see a pile of completed member actions and visible wins. Six Pack is a supporting framing only, not the primary assessed shape.
- **Founder fit:** conditional

## Material assumptions
- A paid-community owner experiences quiet members as a sufficiently costly and recognizable problem to pay US$129 for a fixed reactivation campaign.
- The same campaign can serve multiple similar communities without buyer-specific diagnosis or more than light terminology, niche-example, and call-to-action substitutions.
- Buyer staff will schedule, post, facilitate responses, and review completions; member participation will be adequate for the promised harvest to be visible.
- AI can draft the shared missions, posts, and tracking summaries to a usable standard after shared human QA, without per-owner human fulfillment scaling with customers.
- A trained operator can prepare and support the pack while Jack remains in limited product oversight, and ZedBiz does not become the community host.
- Membership consultants have an incentive and practical access to bundle the campaign, despite no confirmed partner arrangement.
- ZedBiz can retain appropriate rights to its library, implementation examples, and permissioned observations so that the planned asset is transferable.

## Sensitivities
- The repeat score falls if quiet-member problems are irregular or owners treat the campaign as a one-off rescue rather than a recurring operating need.
- The No Brainer and easy-value fit weaken if owners cannot fit posting and facilitation into their existing workflow or members remain passive.
- The route-to-buyer fit depends heavily on a consultant's actual client access, margin, and willingness to carry an outside white-label campaign.
- Asset value is sensitive to retained rights for customer examples and observations; customer data is not automatically an owned resale asset.
- Margin and C3PO strength weaken if niche adaptation, support, or human quality review becomes per-owner work.

## Improvements separate from score
- A more explicit repeat occasion and reuse cadence would distinguish a repeat campaign product from a one-time rescue pack.
- A tightly defined owner workflow, baseline, and success metric would make the promised harvest and the buyer's required effort clearer.
- A fixed rights model for library improvements, examples, and permissioned performance observations would make the Gold mechanism more concrete.
- A specified consultant bundle, buyer handoff, and margin rationale would make the acquisition route less conditional.

## Unresolved questions
- Which precise paid-community segment has both recurring quiet-member pain and budget for a US$129 standardized campaign?
- What owner-level outcome—time saved, retention, renewal, lead, or participation threshold—makes a second purchase economically justified?
- Will owners post and facilitate enough of the sequence for the campaign to generate a visible Harvest Gala rather than unused materials?
- Can membership consultants reach this payer and profit from the bundle without requiring Jack's ongoing personal selling?
- What data, examples, and customer rights can be retained and transferred, and how much routine operator and QA time is required per edition?

## Overall interpretation
As a standalone hopper proposal, WC1 is a credible standardized, AI-produced, owner-run campaign with strong reuse, bounded founder involvement, a clear Harvest Gala, and shared-production scale. Its material trade-offs are a non-inherent repeat engine, a considered rather than effortless owner purchase, execution-dependent value, conditional asset rights, and an unproven consultant route. It remains campaign packaging rather than the current core direction.

## Scope note
This is an independent concept-fit assessment of the stated WC1/WC1.1 scenario. It is not evidence of demand, customer results, margins, partner access, or a launch decision. Existing status remains unchanged.
