## Assessment frame
- **Buyer and payer:** Proposed payer: a local-business owner/operator facing one named operating bottleneck.
- **Offer and delivery:** A fixed-scope, report-only diagnostic: the buyer completes structured intake and supplies examples; multiple AI perspectives analyze and assemble a report; a human analyst checks assumptions and recommendation plausibility; there is no consultation or implementation upsell.
- **Price hypothesis:** $197 per report in the stated unvalidated $97–$497 range; it is attractive only if the report saves more than $197 in delay, poor decisions, or alternative advisory cost despite free AI options.
- **Repeat-payment mechanism:** A different bottleneck or a quarterly review may prompt another report, but no continuing cadence, bundle, or demonstrated reason to renew the same diagnostic is specified.
- **Founder role:** Under WC1, a trained analyst performs bounded report sign-off; Jack handles rare product exceptions, assumed at 0–2 hours weekly. The role remains conditional on the review standard being transferable rather than bespoke diagnosis.
- **Primary satisfaction shape:** Tool in the hand: a stuck owner receives a clearer choice and next action. The supporting Coat of paint framing is not the primary WC1 shape.
- **Founder fit:** conditional

## Material assumptions
- A local-business operator has a sufficiently bounded bottleneck and can provide enough structured information for a fixed report without consultation.
- The $197 price is small relative to the cost of a delayed or poor operating decision and to available advisory alternatives, despite free general-purpose AI advice.
- Multi-perspective AI synthesis produces materially more useful and reliable guidance than a buyer can obtain from a single free AI interaction.
- A trained analyst can apply a documented, bounded plausibility standard, so review does not become bespoke consulting or require Jack on ordinary reports.
- Analyst review time, tool costs, acquisition cost, and support leave worthwhile margin at the proposed price.
- Bookkeepers or business advisers can and will introduce a fixed report under a viable referral arrangement while retaining their advisory relationships.
- The intake, evaluation rules, and report system are owned and transferable; confidential inputs are excluded unless rights are expressly obtained.

## Sensitivities
- If the analyst must investigate context, resolve ambiguity, or tailor recommendations substantially for each owner, the model shifts toward custom client work and weakens bars 2, 4, 7, and 10.
- If buyers view multi-AI synthesis as interchangeable with free general-purpose AI, the price-to-benefit case and repeat demand weaken materially.
- If adviser referrals do not provide repeatable access, the proposed route to buyers becomes founder-dependent.
- If a narrow recurring decision creates genuinely standardized inputs and reliable AI outputs, reuse, independence, AI leverage, and scale benefits improve.

## Improvements separate from score
- A sharply bounded, named decision job with fixed admissible inputs, output, exclusions, and report promise would better preserve the report as a product rather than a custom diagnostic.
- A documented quality rubric that permits verification sampling or exception escalation rather than substantive review on every report would improve the AI/human task split.
- An explicit, job-linked repeat occasion or multi-report structure would make the repeat-payment mechanism clearer without converting the offer into a retainer.
- A defined adviser-facing referral package, including why the report complements rather than replaces the adviser, would make the channel mechanism more concrete.

## Unresolved questions
- Which single operating bottleneck gives a local-business owner an immediate, budgeted reason to buy this fixed report?
- How much analyst time and tool cost is required per report, and can the stated price support worthwhile margin after acquisition and support?
- What quality standard identifies unsafe, generic, or unsupported AI recommendations, and can a non-founder apply it consistently?
- Does using multiple AI perspectives produce a decision-quality advantage that buyers cannot cheaply reproduce themselves?
- Will bookkeepers or advisers refer the report, under what incentive, and without turning the delivery into their custom advisory project?
- Which workflow refinements or observations can be owned and transferred while respecting confidentiality and permission?
- Is a quarterly review a genuine recurring buyer job or merely a possible additional sale?

## Overall interpretation
This is a productized report-only scenario rather than a consulting funnel, which keeps it closer to the recorded founder constraints. Its strongest mechanism is reusable AI-assisted report production with a conceivable analyst-operated steady state. Concept fit remains partial because repeat use, compelling paid value against free AI, partner access, and margin all depend on unproven conditions, while required per-report judgment risks a return to custom diagnostic work.

## Scope note
This is an independent concept-fit assessment of the stated WC1/WC1.1 scenario. It is not evidence of demand, customer results, margins, partner access, or a launch decision. Existing status remains unchanged.
