## Assessment frame
- **Buyer and payer:** Payer: a marketing agency serving local-business clients. Host product: the agency's existing client service/campaign offering, into which this add-on is resold or included; it is an ingredient rather than a standalone ZedBiz core product.
- **Offer and delivery:** A $197/month agency licence for one shared weekly retention asset and seasonal campaign editions. AI drafts and formats the shared editions, human QA checks offers and claims, and the agency selects, aligns, customizes, deploys, and follows up for its own clients; ZedBiz does not manage client accounts.
- **Price hypothesis:** $197/month per agency licence in WC1; the recorded catalog also lists an unvalidated ~$97–$497/month licence range or seasonal packs from ~$199.
- **Repeat-payment mechanism:** A monthly licence is justified by agencies' recurring client-value obligations, weekly fresh assets, and recurring seasonal campaign windows; agencies can resell or mark up relevant editions to their existing clients.
- **Founder role:** A campaign operator publishes and supports editions; Jack supplies occasional strategy only (working assumption: 0–2 hours/week), with no client-account management or community-host role.
- **Primary satisfaction shape:** Harvest Gala: a relevant seasonal campaign should let an agency and its client see a season's accumulated activity or results. Weekly drops are a supporting Six Pack cadence, but can dilute the harvest if they become an unfocused asset pile.
- **Founder fit:** conditional

## Material assumptions
- Agencies have a recurring budget and an incentive to buy or mark up fresh campaigns for local-business clients.
- A $197/month licence produces enough agency value to beat internal creation, existing campaign libraries, free AI, and the buyer's implementation effort.
- The asset remains a fixed reusable campaign unit; agencies, not ZedBiz, absorb client-specific customization, deployment, outcome support, and account management.
- An existing campaign-product customer list or reseller checkout can reach paying agencies without requiring constant founder selling.
- Campaign IP, edition history, and licence relationships are owned or permissioned and transferable.
- AI can perform shared drafting and formatting reliably enough that one human QA process and a campaign operator, rather than per-agency fulfillment, suffice.

## Sensitivities
- Without an owned or incentivized agency channel, the buyer-route score is materially weaker and acquisition could require founder selling.
- If ZedBiz performs client-specific customization, deployment, compliance review, or campaign management, reuse, founder independence, AI leverage, and ease of value all decline.
- A narrow, named season-and-niche campaign with a credible client economic result would strengthen the Harvest Gala and No Brainer judgments; generic retention content would weaken them.
- If campaign assets and agency relationships are not owned, permissioned, and assignable, the claimed transferable Gold is substantially reduced.
- If a small number of seasonal packs satisfies agencies, the monthly repeat mechanism is weaker than the licence framing implies.

## Improvements separate from score
- Define the fixed campaign unit, permitted customization boundary, and responsibility for claims, deployment, and results so the ingredient cannot drift into managed agency service.
- State the named host offer and acquisition asset behind the proposed customer-list or reseller route, rather than relying on a conditional generic host.
- Make each edition's seasonal occasion, local-business niche, and intended visible harvest explicit so the buyer encounters a coherent outcome rather than a content library.
- Specify licence and IP terms that preserve ownership of reusable assets and transferability of the catalogue and agency relationships.

## Unresolved questions
- Which agency segment has an existing budget and repeatedly sells seasonal campaigns or client-retention value to local businesses?
- What paying campaign-product customer list, reseller, or checkout supplies the proposed route to agency payers?
- Will agencies pay $197/month after comparing these assets with existing libraries, internal content creation, and general-purpose AI?
- What contribution margin remains after acquisition, ongoing editorial and claims QA, operator support, and niche-edition creation?
- Can a standardized seasonal campaign produce a sufficiently visible agency/client harvest without transferring outcome responsibility to ZedBiz?
- Which asset, data, and licence rights are actually owned or permissioned for future transfer?

## Overall interpretation
WC1 is a mechanically credible reusable, AI-assisted licence ingredient when hosted inside an agency's existing client-service offer: it has a repeat-payment mechanism, shared production, transferable-catalog potential, and scale economics. It remains conditional rather than a standalone core candidate because its buyer route is only proposed, its $197 differentiation against free or existing alternatives is unproven, and agency/client customization can easily turn the promise into service work. The catalog's hopper/add-on status is preserved; this is a concept-fit assessment, not evidence of demand or an approval to act.
