## Assessment frame
- **Buyer and payer:** An agency owner paying for a $299 quarterly packet intended for one specific award submission; this payer/use case is the WC1 scenario, not validated demand.
- **Offer and delivery:** AI turns an agency's GHL-derived evidence into a white-labeled case-study/award packet; the agency selects the award, supplies evidence and permissions, reviews the draft, and submits it after human fact, permission, and award-rule checks.
- **Price hypothesis:** $299 per quarterly award packet, within the catalog's unvalidated $199–$499 range.
- **Repeat-payment mechanism:** Occasional repeat use could arise from award seasons, other awards, or additional client cases, rather than from an inherent ongoing need.
- **Founder role:** A proposed editor manages submissions and source checks; Jack is assumed to make occasional standards decisions (0–2 hours per week), with no community-host role.
- **Primary satisfaction shape:** Trophy: a polished submission may feel submission-ready, but the actual desired trophy is awarded or withheld by an external judge.
- **Founder fit:** conditional

## Material assumptions
- A chosen award actually accepts agency submissions and has a buyer-relevant reason to submit; the cited source says GHL awards instead use GHL referral/affiliate data and have no submissions.
- Agencies will pay $299 for a packet despite unestablished award demand, urgency, and price-to-benefit.
- Agency data, client permissions, and award rules can be obtained and checked efficiently enough for an editor-led workflow.
- Award calendars or agency educators can reach prospective agency payers without prohibited promotion or sustained founder selling.
- Permissions allow case-study templates and selected proof assets to be reused or transferred; client results and award credibility are not automatically owned assets.

## Sensitivities
- The conclusion is most sensitive to whether a real target award accepts submissions and whether agencies regard winning it as valuable enough to fund a packet.
- Economics change materially with the time required to obtain permissions, reconcile GHL data, and satisfy award-specific rules.
- The buyer route depends on permissioned, incentive-compatible access through educators or organizers rather than assumed visibility on award calendars.

## Improvements separate from score
- If revisited, define one verified non-GHL award, its eligibility and submission rules, the exact agency payer, and a concrete existing-spend or business-value comparison before retaining an award offer.
- Codify data, permission, and factual-review procedures so the editor can resolve ordinary standards cases without Jack.
- Keep the reusable proof-asset factory ingredient distinct from the unsupported award occasion; any new application requires a separately labeled scenario.

## Unresolved questions
- Which, if any, award has a submission process an agency can use and a demonstrated incentive to win?
- Will agencies authorize the required client evidence and permissions at a cost compatible with a $299 packet?
- What human minutes per submission remain after AI drafting and data extraction?
- Can an educator or other channel reach agency owners with a permitted and economically viable offer?

## Overall interpretation
WC1 has reusable proof-packet and AI-drafting ingredients, but the named award-submission application conflicts with the documented GHL award reality and lacks a credible buyer trigger. Its dead-but-ingredient status is preserved: this is not a launch recommendation, and the reusable proof-asset factory cannot receive the benefit of an invented replacement occasion.

## Scope note
This is an independent concept-fit assessment of the stated WC1/WC1.1 scenario. It is not evidence of demand, customer results, margins, partner access, or a launch decision. Existing status remains unchanged.
