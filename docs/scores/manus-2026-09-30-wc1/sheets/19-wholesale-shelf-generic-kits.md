## Assessment frame
- **Buyer and payer:** A GHL/marketing agency pays a flat monthly licence to deploy generic seasonal/local campaign kits across its client base.
- **Offer and delivery:** Twelve generic seasonal campaign-kit editions per year, white-labelled for agency use; AI drafts and formats a shared edition, one human checks it, and the agency adapts, configures, launches, and follows up for each client.
- **Price hypothesis:** $297/month in the WC1 scenario, within the recorded unvalidated $197–$497/month range.
- **Repeat-payment mechanism:** New seasonal campaign deadlines create recurring occasions to use fresh editions and renew the monthly licence, provided agencies actually deploy the kits.
- **Founder role:** Jack supplies occasional product input (illustratively 0–2 hours per week); an operator manages production and access, while agencies perform all client-specific work.
- **Primary satisfaction shape:** Harvest Gala: an agency could show a season's campaign activity/results to clients, but generic kits may be unused rather than produce a visible harvest.
- **Founder fit:** conditional

## Material assumptions
- Agencies have enough suitable client campaigns and client volume for a $297 monthly generic-kit licence to be economical.
- A generic seasonal edition can be safely and usefully adapted without ZedBiz doing client diagnosis, configuration, or campaign management.
- AI-generated drafts can be brought to acceptable shared-release quality through one human review per edition.
- A trained operator can manage the catalogue, access, and routine support without recurring founder involvement.
- Agency content educators will have sufficient incentive and access to resell despite competing content shelves.

## Sensitivities
- The assessment changes materially if agencies use only a few kits annually rather than deploying them across a substantial client base.
- A specific niche, trigger, and promised outcome could improve the satisfaction and No Brainer fit, but would be a different concept from the assigned generic contents.
- If reseller access cannot be secured, acquisition may revert to founder selling and weaken the route-to-buyer score.
- If agency adaptation creates frequent support or bespoke requests, reuse, independence, AI leverage, and margins weaken together.

## Improvements separate from score
- Keep the recorded dead status; retain only the agency white-label distribution shape as an ingredient rather than reviving the generic shelf.
- Before considering any successor, specify one agency-observed job, seasonal trigger, niche, expected client outcome, and bounded adaptation path.
- Define reseller economics, self-serve onboarding, support boundaries, and rights to the reusable asset library before treating the channel or transferable asset as established.

## Unresolved questions
- Which agency-observed campaign job would make a licence worth paying for rather than using free AI or an existing library?
- How many agency clients and deployments are required for the proposed monthly price to create clear buyer value?
- Can generic assets be adapted safely enough across local-business clients without recurring custom support?
- Which content educator or other channel owner would promote the licence, on what terms, and why over competing shelves?
- What evidence would show that agencies deploy kits and obtain campaign results rather than accumulating unused content?

## Overall interpretation
The WC1 mechanics are assessable and support reusable, AI-assisted shared production, operator independence, and scale economies. They do not overcome the recorded reason this entry is dead: undifferentiated generic campaign content creates a weak reason to use, renew, or promote the $297 shelf. The assessment therefore preserves the dead-but-ingredient status and treats the agency distribution shape, not the generic kit contents, as the potentially reusable lesson.

## Scope note
This is an independent concept-fit assessment of the stated WC1/WC1.1 scenario. It is not evidence of demand, customer results, margins, partner access, or a launch decision. Existing status remains unchanged.
