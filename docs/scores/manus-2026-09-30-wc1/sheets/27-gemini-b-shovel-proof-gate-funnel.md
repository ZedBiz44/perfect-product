## Assessment frame
- **Buyer and payer:** In the WC1 scenario, a prospect for a ZedBiz offer uses the free gate; a failed prospect is the proposed payer of the $19 DIY fallback, while qualified prospects are routed to Jack-led consulting. The entry also notes an unselected alternative of selling the gate to other operators.
- **Offer and delivery:** An automated GoHighLevel assessment collects answers, with AI summarizing responses and suggesting a route. Failure leads to a $19 DIY follow-on; passing leads to bespoke consulting. The gate is a free funnel rather than the paid outcome.
- **Price hypothesis:** Free diagnostic; $19 DIY fallback in WC1. The linked catalogue price range of roughly $7–49 for DIY and $99–299 if productized for operators is explicitly unvalidated; consulting price is not stated.
- **Repeat-payment mechanism:** An optional ladder of DIY follow-ons and consulting is asserted, but no defined renewal, repeat trigger, or second paid SKU is specified. The free gate itself does not create recurring revenue.
- **Founder role:** Jack is proposed to deliver advisory work to qualified prospects and would therefore remain central to the paid, bespoke outcome; the gate can be systematized but does not remove that role.
- **Primary satisfaction shape:** Tool in the hand: a prospect can receive a concrete diagnostic and route. It is weakened because rejection/diagnosis alone does not resolve the underlying bottleneck.
- **Founder fit:** conflict

## Material assumptions
- A competent GoHighLevel/AI workflow can screen and route routine responses at practical marginal cost, without assuming flawless output or arbitrary integrations.
- A prospect experiencing an immediate business bottleneck will see enough value in the $19 DIY fallback to buy it despite free AI, PLR, and other alternatives.
- Partner educators could have a reason to share the free diagnostic, although no access, agreement, or referral terms are evidenced.
- Assessment logic and permissioned lead records can be retained and transferred lawfully; the source only conditionally describes those records as an accumulating asset.
- The assessed WC1 paid path remains bespoke consulting for qualified leads, rather than substituting an unmentioned self-serve productized outcome.

## Sensitivities
- If the paid outcome were only a bounded, reusable self-serve DIY product rather than consulting, bars 2, 4, 7, and 10 would require reassessment; that is not the WC1 scenario scored here.
- Bar 6 depends heavily on whether the $19 DIY item removes a specific costly bottleneck rather than merely restating a diagnosis.
- Bar 8 depends on actual partner incentives and access; the gate itself has no intrinsic acquisition loop.
- The limited Gold assessment depends on consent, ownership, and transferability of any lead records rather than treating customer data as automatically owned.

## Improvements separate from score
- A separately assessed design could make the paid outcome a fixed-scope self-serve product and eliminate the consulting route, rather than treating the reusable gate as a lead generator for bespoke work.
- A future specification would need to define the DIY result, the payer's immediate trigger, support boundary, and an explicit repeat-payment reason.
- Any partner-distribution design would need a clear channel-owner incentive and data-permission model.

## Unresolved questions
- Is this meant to remain an internal Shovel proof worksheet, become a paid DIY product, or function as a consulting funnel?
- What specific useful action or completed outcome does the $19 DIY fallback deliver, and why is it superior to free AI or existing DIY resources?
- What repeat occasion would make a prior DIY buyer pay again?
- Who can credibly distribute the gate to the proposed payer, under what incentive, and without founder-led selling?
- What consulting scope, price, and staffing model would follow a pass, and can it coexist with the no-custom-client-work constraint?
- What consent, retention, and transfer rights attach to submitted assessment data?

## Overall interpretation
WC1 is a reusable automated screening ingredient with a plausible low-ticket fallback, but not a strong Perfect Product fit in its selected form. Its commercial destination is Jack-led bespoke consulting, creating direct build-once/sell-many and runs-without-founder conflicts, while repeat demand, purchase value, and distribution remain unvalidated. The recorded hopper status and founder conflict should remain visible.

## Scope note
This is an independent concept-fit assessment of the stated WC1/WC1.1 scenario. It is not evidence of demand, customer results, margins, partner access, or a launch decision. Existing status remains unchanged.
