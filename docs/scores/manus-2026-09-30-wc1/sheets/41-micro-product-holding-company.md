## Assessment frame
- **Buyer and payer:** For the scored first deal, an event planner is the end buyer and payer for a licensed event-planner template; the holding company pays a seller through acquisition or revenue-share terms. The portfolio has no single retail buyer beyond this illustrative template lane.
- **Offer and delivery:** Acquire or revenue-share an existing event-planner template product with transferable rights, then sell a self-serve license: the planner buys, downloads it, fills in event details, checks it, and uses the resulting plan or client deliverable. An operator handles sales and routine support; the holding company supplies portfolio-level automation, checkout, and marketing systems.
- **Price hypothesis:** No acquisition price, revenue-share rate, or retail template price is set. The conditional hypothesis is that a license can be priced well below the time and error cost of creating an event plan from scratch, while retained receipts cover rights, updates, support, and distribution.
- **Repeat-payment mechanism:** The same planner may buy updated editions, adjacent event-format templates, or a broader license. This is an optional repeat ladder rather than an inherent subscription, and a one-off download has limited recurrence.
- **Founder role:** Jack selects and integrates acquisition or revenue-share targets and resolves exceptions; a trained operator can handle sales and product support after transfer. The ongoing holding-company deal-selection role remains a founder-dependence risk.
- **Primary satisfaction shape:** Tool in the hand: an event planner can use the template to create a usable plan or client deliverable on an upcoming event job.
- **Founder fit:** conditional

## Material assumptions
- A target template product can be acquired or revenue-shared with assignable IP, customer-list, brand, and contract rights rather than merely access to a seller's personal reputation.
- A licensed template has a price-to-time-saved relationship that is compelling versus free templates, despite no selected retail price or demonstrated willingness to pay.
- Support, updates, and rights costs remain bounded enough that repeat licensing produces worthwhile net receipts after acquisition and distribution costs.
- AI can reliably handle structured template adaptations, update drafts, and routine support with human quality review and exceptions rather than requiring human fulfillment per buyer.
- Relevant event-industry marketplaces, educators, seller partnerships, or an acquired permissioned customer list can provide a repeatable buyer route without Jack continuously selling personally.

## Sensitivities
- The assessment changes materially if the deal lacks transferable IP, customer-contact permissions, brand rights, or assignable contracts.
- A price that cannot clearly beat the time and quality cost of free-template alternatives weakens the buyer value and repeat-purchase case.
- If target screening and integration remain founder-only work at portfolio scale, independence and scale benefits weaken.
- A chamber-speaking acquisition or a maintenance-heavy WordPress plugin should be assessed separately; neither inherits this template-lane score.

## Improvements separate from score
- Define target-screening criteria for transferable rights, seller dependence, customer permissions, support history, and conservative net receipts before considering a deal.
- Specify the template license, the expected planner time saved, and comparison with free alternatives to make the price-to-benefit proposition testable.
- Document an operator playbook that separates transferable support and integration work from Jack's deal-selection exceptions.
- Define an AI workflow and quality controls that make adaptation and support core repeatable production rather than incidental assistance.
- Choose and document one channel with its access requirements and economics rather than treating marketplaces, educators, partnerships, and acquired lists as interchangeable.

## Unresolved questions
- Which actual target has provable transferable rights, permissioned customer access, bounded support, and a seller-independent product?
- What retail price, license scope, repeat cadence, and conversion argument would make the template preferable to free alternatives?
- What acquisition or revenue-share terms leave worthwhile net receipts after updates, support, distribution, and integration costs?
- Which buyer route is contractually accessible and can repeatedly reach event planners without continuing founder selling?
- Can AI adapt the template and resolve most routine support at sufficient quality, and how much human review is required per buyer?

## Overall interpretation
The reusable event-planner-template acquisition lane is a conditionally plausible product structure: repeat licensing, transferable IP, self-serve use, and shared portfolio operations can fit several bars. Its material weaknesses are unselected target and deal economics, unresolved price differentiation, uncertain channel transfer, and Jack's continuing acquisition/integration role. This is a concept-fit reassessment only and does not validate a generic holding company, an acquisition, chamber speaking, or a WordPress-plugin business.

## Scope note
This is an independent concept-fit assessment of the stated WC1/WC1.1 scenario. It is not evidence of demand, customer results, margins, partner access, or a launch decision. Existing status remains unchanged.
