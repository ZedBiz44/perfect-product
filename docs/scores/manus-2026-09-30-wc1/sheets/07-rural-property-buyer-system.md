## Assessment frame
- **Buyer and payer:** Working scenario: a single-region rural real-estate brokerage pays for the licence; rural property buyers use the kit. Agents, land lenders and close partners remain recorded alternative payers, not commitments.
- **Offer and delivery:** A fixed, broker-branded single-region pre-offer kit: lead-capture page, property-visit scorecard, professional-question list and automatic follow-up. The brokerage sends it at buyer intake; AI drafts and packages edition assets, a qualified regional reviewer verifies editions, and broker staff provide property-specific advice.
- **Price hypothesis:** $997 initial/founding licence plus an optional $97/month update licence. This is an unvalidated hypothesis that must outperform a brokerage's existing checklist and free buyer-guide alternatives.
- **Repeat-payment mechanism:** The same licence serves successive buyer cohorts; further payments could come from additional office licences or substantive regional-update renewals. A static checklist alone gives the payer little reason to renew.
- **Founder role:** Jack is an occasional editor, assumed at 0–2 hours per week. An operator maintains editions with a qualified regional reviewer; broker staff remain responsible for individual-property advice.
- **Primary satisfaction shape:** Tool in the hand: at a property viewing, the buyer uses the scorecard to notice an overlooked issue and ask the right professional before making an offer.
- **Founder fit:** conditional

## Material assumptions
- A fixed, no-customization brokerage licence can be branded and distributed without converting into custom client work.
- A $997 licence has enough lead-qualification or buyer-service value to a brokerage to beat its existing and free guide alternatives.
- A qualified regional reviewer can maintain accurate, bounded question sets at a cost that preserves licence margin, while professional advice stays with the brokerage and specialists.
- AI can draft checklists, assemble source changes and package follow-up assets, with human work limited principally to shared edition verification rather than per-buyer fulfillment.
- The business can own or assign the regional kit IP, maintained question sets and licence contracts for a future operator or buyer.
- Brokerage trainers or land-lending networks have a workable incentive and access path to introduce a fixed licence, although none is secured.
- Regional changes are substantive and frequent enough that at least some payers value the optional update licence.

## Sensitivities
- The score is highly sensitive to whether channel owners perceive measurable intake or qualification value above free buyer guides and their own materials.
- Regional legal, water, access, zoning and financing differences may make reviewer cost and liability boundaries much heavier than a shared-kit model assumes.
- Renewal fit falls if regional updates are infrequent or are not materially more useful than the original static checklist.
- The buyer route weakens sharply if trainer, lender-network or brokerage access requires ongoing founder-led selling rather than a repeatable partner mechanism.
- Scale benefits are chiefly within one region; adding jurisdictions may dilute rather than compound margins.

## Improvements separate from score
- A renewalable version could be defined around a bounded regional change log and update asset, rather than relying on a static checklist for subscription logic.
- The offer could make the brokerage-side outcome explicit: a fixed intake and qualification workflow distinct from a generic buyer guide.
- A consistent jurisdiction template, reviewer sign-off boundary and no-professional-advice language would make the reusable product boundary clearer.
- A transferable fixed licence and self-serve branding handoff would better protect the no-custom-work and operator-run design.

## Unresolved questions
- Will a named rural brokerage or lender pay $997 when detailed land-buying guidance is already free and generic templates are inexpensive?
- What single region and buyer-use case can be covered safely, and what is the recurring cost and availability of qualified local review?
- What changes would make a $97/month update licence materially valuable rather than optional housekeeping?
- Which trainer, brokerage group or land-lending network has the buyer access and economic reason to distribute the licence without continual founder selling?
- Can broker branding, delivery and follow-up remain fully fixed-scope while preserving useful local relevance?

## Overall interpretation
WC1 makes this assessable as a fixed channel-owner licence rather than the rejected direct-to-consumer acreage guide. It has a clear Tool in the hand experience, an operator-capable delivery design and shared AI-assisted production. Its commercial fit remains conditional on paid channel-owner value, a credible renewal reason, partner access and economically bounded regional review. All ten bars are assessable; the 14/20 concept-fit result has no zero but is not a shortlist signal or evidence of demand.
