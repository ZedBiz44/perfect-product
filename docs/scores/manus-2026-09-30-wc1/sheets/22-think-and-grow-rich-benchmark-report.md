## Assessment frame
- **Buyer and payer:** An agency owner who subscribes to an as-yet-unidentified host product would pay $79 quarterly for the optional benchmark layer; the scenario does not establish an actual host product or payer base.
- **Offer and delivery:** One shared quarterly benchmark report built from permissioned comparable campaign outcomes, with the owner selecting a relevant segment and using the comparison to inform a decision.
- **Price hypothesis:** $79 per quarterly report, conditional on comparisons being relevant and trustworthy enough to outperform free reports; this is an unvalidated catalogue hypothesis.
- **Repeat-payment mechanism:** Fresh quarterly observations can support recurring planning and a new comparison each quarter, provided the underlying data continues to be useful.
- **Founder role:** A research operator collects data and an analyst checks interpretation; Jack is assumed to make occasional standards decisions (0–2 hours per week), rather than deliver each report.
- **Primary satisfaction shape:** Tool in the hand: a relevant peer comparison helps an owner decide what action to take; it is not itself a credible Trophy outcome.
- **Founder fit:** conditional

## Material assumptions
- A host product with paying agency-owner subscribers exists and can offer the report without founder-led selling.
- Agencies provide enough permissioned, rights-cleared outcome records to form useful comparable cohorts, and those rights are transferable.
- The shared report's AI-assisted preparation plus human comparability review can be funded by the proposed price and scale.
- Relevant, trustworthy comparisons change an agency owner's quarterly planning decision enough to justify $79 versus free information.

## Sensitivities
- The assessment changes materially if no host product supplies a reachable agency-owner payer base; bar 8 cannot be resolved from the current scenario.
- Sparse, biased, non-comparable, or non-transferable outcome data weakens the repeat value, benchmark asset, satisfaction, and scale mechanisms together.
- If human analysis must be bespoke by agency rather than performed once per shared cohort, build-once economics and C3PO fit weaken further.

## Improvements separate from score
- Name the host product, its payer segment, and the actual in-product or partner route through which the optional report is offered.
- Specify a minimum viable permission, cohort, normalization, and quality-governance design before relying on benchmark claims.
- Constrain the first report to one decision, comparable cohort, and action format so relevance and buyer effort are explicit.

## Unresolved questions
- Which established host product has paying agency-owner subscribers and permission to offer this layer?
- Will agencies share rights-cleared outcome data, and what privacy, consent, and comparability standards apply?
- What evidence would make a quarterly benchmark sufficiently decision-changing for an agency owner to pay $79 rather than use free information?
- How much human analyst time is required per quarterly shared edition as the dataset grows?

## Overall interpretation
This is a plausible retention ingredient rather than a standalone offer: a permissioned benchmark archive could become valuable, reusable decision support with a quarterly renewal reason. Its central dependency remains unresolved—there is no named host product or demonstrated acquisition route—and data permissions, comparability, and human interpretation constrain economics and AI leverage. Coverage is incomplete because bar 8 is U.
