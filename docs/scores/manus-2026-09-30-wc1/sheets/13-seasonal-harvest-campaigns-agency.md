## Assessment frame
- **Buyer and payer:** A home-service agency pays for the shared seasonal campaign edition and may mark it up to its own local-business clients; trainers and supplier marketing programmes are proposed channels, not payers.
- **Offer and delivery:** A fixed, niche-specific $399 spring-opening campaign pack: AI drafts messages, landing-copy variants and follow-up assets; one shared human QA checks the edition; the agency supplies local offer/list/budget details and runs the campaign.
- **Price hypothesis:** $399 per agency for one spring-opening niche edition (currency is not specified in the source).
- **Repeat-payment mechanism:** Annual seasonal windows and later-season editions give an agency reason to buy a refreshed offer again rather than treat the pack as a permanent one-off.
- **Founder role:** A campaign operator maintains the shared seasonal edition, agencies operate client campaigns, and Jack is assumed to provide occasional positioning input of roughly 0–2 hours per week.
- **Primary satisfaction shape:** Harvest Gala: after the seasonal work, the agency and its clients can see a countable collection of enquiries or bookings; Tool in the hand is a supporting interpretation of the ready-to-run pack.
- **Founder fit:** conditional

## Material assumptions
- A fixed niche/season pack can be useful enough for a home-service agency to buy at the stated $399 hypothesis rather than rely on existing assets.
- Agencies will handle local adaptation and campaign execution without turning ZedBiz into a custom-service provider.
- A trainer or supplier marketing programme can have sufficient margin or member-value incentive to place the pack, although no access or agreement is asserted.
- Permissioned campaign examples and agency relationships can be retained and assigned so the growing seasonal catalogue is transferable.
- AI drafting plus one shared human QA is adequate for shared edition production; local details remain the agency's responsibility.

## Sensitivities
- The assessment is most sensitive to whether agencies have an existing paid seasonal-add-on motion and see a $399 niche pack as incremental value rather than duplicate material.
- The buyer route is sensitive to a channel owner's willingness to distribute a third-party pack and to the economics of any margin or member-value arrangement.
- Harvest satisfaction and renewal depend on local campaign execution and results, which the shared pack does not control.
- Gold depends on retaining permissioned outcome examples and transferable agency relationships rather than merely accumulating unassignable customer data.

## Improvements separate from score
- Define a fixed inclusion and localization boundary that keeps the pack from becoming custom agency work.
- State the exact seasonal outcome, client type and evidence the agency may use to show the harvest, rather than relying on a generic reactivation claim.
- Specify the channel economics, placement model and partner responsibilities needed for trainers or supplier programmes to distribute the edition.
- Document the operator playbook and quality standard needed to replace recurring founder positioning input.

## Unresolved questions
- Which home-service agency profile has a recurring budget and a demonstrated seasonal campaign resale motion?
- What niche-specific offer and asset scope make the $399 hypothesis clearly incremental to existing agency templates or AI tools?
- Can a trainer or supplier programme place the product repeatedly under terms that preserve margin without founder selling?
- What result-sharing permissions and privacy rules allow examples or aggregate learning to become transferable assets?
- How much localization and support does an agency actually need before the model becomes bespoke work?

## Overall interpretation
This WC1 scenario has strong product mechanics: a recurring seasonal reason to buy, shared AI-assisted production, a transferable seasonal catalogue and fixed-cost scale benefits. Its concept fit is conditional rather than selected: current direction records the seasonal campaign shelf as an add-on ingredient, and the commercial case turns on an unproven agency willingness to pay, a usable partner route and a disciplined fixed-pack boundary. The proposed model avoids hosting and direct managed client work only while agencies retain local execution.

## Scope note
This is an independent concept-fit assessment of the stated WC1/WC1.1 scenario. It is not evidence of demand, customer results, margins, partner access, or a launch decision. Existing status remains unchanged.
