## Assessment frame
- **Buyer and payer:** Business-broker firm payer purchasing a fixed-scope licence; the broker's business-owner seller is the end user of the intake and readiness materials.
- **Offer and delivery:** A $997 fixed digital-sale-file licence: broker-branded seller workbook, no-password asset inventory, operations/key-person prompts, broker intake, and non-legal transition checklist. The broker brands and sends it; seller supplies records; AI organizes inventory and drafts gap prompts; the broker verifies sale-file records.
- **Price hypothesis:** $997 fixed licence for repeated seller intake, with possible additional-office, seller-volume, and substantive-update payments. This is an unvalidated hypothesis rather than market evidence.
- **Repeat-payment mechanism:** Additional offices, seller-volume licences, and substantive updates are named repeat-payment occasions; the base single licence is otherwise one-time.
- **Founder role:** Jack supplies occasional commercial perspective (assumed 0–2 hours/week); a product operator maintains shared forms with subject review, while brokers retain transaction and record-verification work.
- **Primary satisfaction shape:** Tool in the hand: a broker receives a branded, repeatable intake tool and a seller can turn scattered sale records into an organized digital-asset inventory.
- **Founder fit:** conditional

## Material assumptions
- A broker firm has a repeated seller-onboarding gap that its current checklist or free seller guides do not adequately solve.
- A $997 fixed-scope licence is small relative to avoided broker chasing and sale-file friction.
- The forms and branding can remain standardized rather than becoming custom transaction, valuation, legal, or consulting work.
- AI can reliably organize submitted inventories and generate useful missing-item prompts, with broker verification remaining appropriate.
- A product operator can maintain the shared template, leaving Jack only occasional oversight.
- Broker training networks or succession-adviser newsletters can provide a repeatable referral route despite no access or agreement being established.

## Sensitivities
- A broker's actual repeated intake friction, rather than the seriousness of a business sale, determines whether the system has paid value.
- The result changes materially if $997 requires bespoke branding, legal review, transaction advice, or ongoing broker support.
- Free BizBuySell guides and lower-priced broker toolkits make differentiation, payer urgency, and price sensitivity central.
- The buyer route depends on third-party training/newsletter access that is proposed but not evidenced.
- The practicality of AI-assisted organization depends on safe handling of sensitive, incomplete seller records and the quality of broker verification.

## Improvements separate from score
- A documented, narrow broker workflow gap that generic free guides do not solve would strengthen the No Brainer and Gold assessments.
- A clearly bounded, self-serve branding and support model would protect reusable margins, independence, and ease of value.
- A defined privacy-safe inventory workflow and explicit division between AI prompts and broker verification would strengthen the C3PO assessment.
- A repeatable channel with aligned referral economics would strengthen the buyer-route assessment.

## Unresolved questions
- What exact seller-intake step currently consumes repeated broker time or causes lost quality, and how often does it occur?
- Which elements are sufficiently distinctive from free seller guides and existing broker toolkits to justify $997?
- What licence, branding, update, support, and data-handling terms preserve fixed scope without drifting into client work?
- Can the stated AI workflow handle sensitive records safely and usefully without creating substantial per-file human fulfillment?
- Which channel owner can credibly reach broker-firm payers, on what incentive, and with what access?

## Overall interpretation
WC1 describes a conditionally productized broker tool with strong reusable-template, operator-transfer, satisfaction, and scale mechanics. Its central weakness is commercial rather than structural: business-sale information is already widely free, so the scenario has only a partial No Brainer and route to market until a specific broker gap, price fit, and channel are established. This is a concept-fit assessment of the stated WC1 scenario, not evidence of demand, commitments, margins, or operating performance.
