## Assessment frame
- **Buyer and payer:** Intended payer: agencies and other client-owning businesses, including associations, chambers, and franchise systems, paying for shelf access or resale capability. Their SMB clients are end recipients and may pay the channel owner for the underlying supplier offer; suppliers may instead fund a disclosed commission or margin. The actual payer mix is not set.
- **Offer and delivery:** An operator-managed wholesale and white-label shelf: a reusable catalog, ordering, and account layer lets a channel owner choose, brand, activate, and deliver supplier offers to SMB clients. AI maps the catalog, provides product guidance, adapts content, and triages routine support; suppliers fulfill their specific offers, while people handle commercial terms and delivery exceptions.
- **Price hypothesis:** Unsettled. WC1.1 allows disclosed membership or licence fees, resale margins or supplier commissions, and shared savings where an agency's net benefit versus separate suppliers remains meaningful; it does not assume every transaction has every revenue stream.
- **Repeat-payment mechanism:** Recurring platform or membership access, recurring products on the shelf, and repeat purchases across the catalog provide several stated ways for the same channel owner or SMB relationship to generate later payments.
- **Founder role:** Jack may recruit or negotiate anchor suppliers and initial channel relationships. A trained operator is intended to run the shelf and supplier support, but independence is only partial until supplier and channel relationships, terms, and escalation procedures transfer from Jack.
- **Primary satisfaction shape:** Tool in the hand: a channel owner gains a branded, usable shelf for serving clients; Harvest Gala from comparable savings is supporting rather than primary.
- **Founder fit:** conditional

## Material assumptions
- A defined initial supplier shelf can leave agencies meaningful net value through lower sourcing cost, broader choice, easier delivery, or client-retention value versus their alternatives.
- Supplier agreements permit workable wholesale, white-label, commission, margin, or shared-savings terms, and their responsibilities cover ordinary fulfillment rather than transferring all support to ZedBiz.
- Catalog integrations, channel relationships, permissioned demand records, and supplier agreements can be owned or assigned on transfer; customer data is not presumed to be resaleable without permission.
- A common interface plus AI catalog mapping, guidance, content adaptation, and support triage can handle routine scale, while an operator—not Jack—handles recurring supplier and reseller support.
- Agency educators, supplier partner networks, or associations have adequate incentive and access to introduce the shelf without Jack continually personally selling it.

## Sensitivities
- If the initial shelf consists of offerings that require substantial agency-by-agency implementation or support, the reusable-margin, founder-independence, AI, and easy-value assessments weaken.
- If suppliers do not grant sufficient wholesale economics, white-label rights, data permissions, or contract assignability, both the Gold asset and the buyer savings proposition weaken.
- If aggregate purchasing volume is too fragmented to improve supplier terms, the principal Newton's Rule mechanism weakens even if a reseller catalog remains viable.
- A different chosen payer—supplier, channel owner, or end SMB—would change the trigger, price-to-benefit case, and route-to-buyer analysis.

## Improvements separate from score
- The scenario would be more assessable with one initial supplier category, one primary payer, and a single disclosed fee or margin path rather than several possible revenue streams.
- A documented operator playbook for supplier onboarding, reseller support, exceptions, and relationship handoff would strengthen the system-independence case.
- Defining which activation and client-support steps are automated, supplier-owned, agency-owned, or exceptional would clarify both the C3PO and easy-value boundaries.
- A named channel mechanism with its economic incentive and access requirement would make the acquisition route more concrete.

## Unresolved questions
- Which initial supplier offer produces a repeated, credible saving or client-retention benefit for a particular agency-type payer?
- What exact fee, commission, margin, or shared-savings model leaves a worthwhile margin after integration, onboarding, support, and exceptions?
- Which supplier terms permit white-label resale, data use, contract assignment, and a transferable customer or channel asset?
- How much customization, technical activation, and client delivery must each participating agency perform before it realizes value?
- Can a named agency, association, or supplier channel introduce the shelf repeatedly without continuing founder-led recruitment?

## Overall interpretation
WC1.1 describes a conditionally coherent wholesale-shelf structure with a direct scale advantage and potentially accumulating transferable assets. Its weaker fits are not lack of proof alone: the broad, unspecified initial shelf leaves material per-account support, AI-core-work, price-to-benefit, channel access, and buyer-effort trade-offs. This is a concept-fit reassessment only, not evidence of demand, supplier terms, savings, or a recommendation to act.
