## Quick read
Shovel Your Biz-Shit is a weekly drop for people who own Skool groups, memberships and paid communities. Each week the owner gets ONE useful idea or concept for their members, a few ready-made posts to get people talking about it, and a small pack of supporting content. The concept adds to the group's main topic and never competes with the owner's own expertise. It works across many niches, so Jack's "jack of all trades" range is the niche, and AI does the drafting while Jack checks it and adds his experience.
## The driveway edges: why the owner looks good
A contractor is pouring a neighbour's driveway. Jack walks over and gives the crew a good tip on finishing the edges. The contractor may have known it or not, but he doesn't always remember to tell his guys. The driveway is still his job, his crew and his name on the truck. The tip just makes the finish better.
That is this product.
- **It's the owner's group.** They post the concept as their own content, in their own voice. Members see the owner bringing them something useful.
- **Jack is the quiet supplier, not a guru.** He doesn't show up in the group, run calls, or build a following. He hands the owner a good tip each week.
- **It adds, it never overshadows.** The concept sits beside the group's main focus. An AI group still learns AI from the owner. A video group still learns video from the owner. The drop adds one fresh, practical angle they didn't have time to find.
- **One standout thing a week, not volume.** This is the six-pack principle: cheap, easy to grab, satisfying right away, pays off again around day 3, and a fresh one arrives next week.
## What's in each weekly drop
Every drop has the same three parts.
### 1. The Concept (the main thing)
One idea, tip, method or "what's new and what matters" breakdown, picked for that niche. Short and plain. The owner should be able to read it in 5 minutes and post it the same day.
- A clear title the owner can use as a post headline
- A short plain-English explainer (about 150–300 words)
- A "try it in 10 minutes" step so members can act on it
- A "where this fits" note for the owner, so it supports their main teaching instead of crossing it
### 2. Ways to use it for engagement (2–4 sample posts)
Ready-to-post ideas that turn the concept into replies:
- A poll or quick-pick question
- A "show your work" or try-it challenge
- A **day-3 follow-up** post, the second payoff of the six-pack
- Sometimes a hot-take or "what do you think?" thread
- **One comment-to-get post, every week.** Example: "Comment EDGES and I'll send the checklist." Members comment the keyword, and the supplementary content (part 3) is what they get. This ties the concept, the posts and the extras together. In the Sep 28 Facebook research, these posts got far more comments than normal posts.
### 3. Supplementary content
One or two small extras that go with the concept. One of them is what members get when they comment the keyword. Pick from:
- A short explainer or cheat sheet (1 page)
- A checklist
- A graphic or Canva template
- A copy-paste AI prompt
- A short video script or talking points the owner can record if they want
### How it differs from PLR

|  | Traditional PLR | Shovel Your Biz-Shit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What you get | About 1,000 words + graphics | One standout concept + 2–4 engagement posts + a small supplementary pack |
| Goal | Content to reuse or resell | Give *your* members one useful new thing this week, and get them talking about it |
| Format | Articles, ebooks | A concept, posts, prompts, checklists, graphics, short scripts |
| Timing | Usually monthly packs | Weekly, with a day-3 second use |
| Volume | Often a lot, mostly filler | Small on purpose: one great thing, not a pile |
| Built for | Bloggers, marketers, coaches | Skool / community / membership owners, by niche edition |

### EXAMPLE DROP 1 (fully worked): AI / OpenClaw owners group
> **Example only**, to show the format. The two "update" items below are **illustrative placeholders, not a real OpenClaw release**. In a real drop, the research agent pulls the actual release notes and Jack checks them.
**The Concept: "The 2 things in this update a business owner actually needs (and what to skip)"**
Every update comes with a long list of changes. Most business owners don't need most of it. They need to know which 2 changes save them time this week, and what they can safely ignore.
- **Example change 1 (illustrative): tasks that run on a schedule.** Business use: have your agent send you a Monday morning summary of last week's leads and unanswered emails. Try it in 10 minutes: set up one weekly task and let it run.
- **Example change 2 (illustrative): the agent remembers more between sessions.** Business use: keep a standing "about my business" note so you stop explaining your business every time. Try it in 10 minutes: write a 10-line business brief and save it where the agent can use it.
- **Skip for now:** developer-only settings and anything marked experimental. Come back to them later if you need them.
**Where this fits (note to the owner):** Your group teaches setup and building. This drop doesn't teach setup. It answers the question your members ask after every update: "So what does this mean for my business?"
**Ways to use it for engagement:**
1. **Poll (post day 1):** "New update is out. Which of these 2 changes would save you more time this week? A) Scheduled tasks B) Better memory C) Neither, I'm still getting set up"
2. **Try-it challenge (post day 1 or 2):** "10-minute challenge: set up one scheduled task using change #1. Post a screenshot of it. I'll pin the most useful one."
3. **Day-3 follow-up:** "It's been 3 days. Did your scheduled task run? Reply ✅ worked, ⚠️ half worked, or ❌ didn't. If it didn't, post what happened and we'll sort it out together."
4. **Hot-take thread (optional, later in the week):** "What's one feature everyone got excited about that you've never actually used? Why not?"
5. **Comment-to-get post (day 1 or 2):** "Want the before-you-update checklist and the 1-page cheat sheet? Comment EDGES and I'll send the checklist." Members comment the word, and the owner sends the supplementary content below. (In Facebook groups, send the link in a reply or DM, since some groups block links in posts.)
**Supplementary content:**
- **Cheat sheet:** "This update in 60 seconds: 2 to use, the rest to skip" (1 page)
- **Copy-paste prompt:** "Here is my business in 2 lines: \[your business\]. Here are the update notes: \[paste notes\]. Which 2 changes would save me the most time this week? Explain in plain English and give me a 10-minute first step for each."
- **Checklist:** "Before you hit update: 5 checks" (sent with the cheat sheet to everyone who comments EDGES)
- **Graphic:** square post image, "2 to use. Skip the rest." with space for the two change names
- **60-second video script (optional):** talking points: (1) there's a new update, (2) here are the 2 changes that matter for business owners, (3) try change #1 today, (4) post your result in the group
### EXAMPLE DROP 2 (shorter): Video / YouTube group
> **Example only.** General ideas, no specific tool named.
**The Concept: "5 ways AI can help edit your YouTube Shorts"**
1. Cut dead air and filler words ("um," long pauses) automatically, then check the cuts by hand.
2. Make captions with AI, then fix names and key words yourself.
3. Paste your transcript and ask AI for 3 stronger opening lines. Pick the best one for your first 2 seconds.
4. Have AI find 3 short clips worth posting inside one long video.
5. Get 5 title ideas and a short description, then edit them so they sound like you.
**Where this fits:** The owner still teaches filming, story and style. This drop just shows a few ways to get the editing done faster.
**Ways to use it for engagement:**
- **Poll:** "Which of these 5 have you tried? Pick all that apply."
- **Before-and-after challenge:** "Edit one Short using just one of the 5 ideas. Post the old and new version."
- **Day-3 follow-up:** "Which one saved you the most time? Which one was a waste? Be honest."
**Supplementary content:** a checklist ("Edit a Short in 15 minutes") and a copy-paste prompt for idea #3.
### EXAMPLE DROP 3 (quick): Local-business coaching group
> **Example only.**
**The Concept: "The 5-second neighbour test for your Google Business Profile"**
Could a stranger tell in 5 seconds what you do, where you are, and when you're open? If not, fix the top of the profile first: business description, main category, hours, and a real photo of your place or your work.
- **Engagement posts:** "Post your profile's first line. The group gives it the 5-second test." · Poll: "When did you last update your profile? A) This month B) This year C) Um..." · Day-3 follow-up: "Did you change one thing? Post it."
- **Supplementary content:** a 10-point profile checklist and a prompt that rewrites a business description in plain language.

Jack has 25 years of local-business marketing, an MBA in digital marketing, a banker's eye for what makes money, and years of farming, where you fix whatever breaks. Most suppliers pick one topic. Jack's range lets one format serve many groups. It just needs the right packaging.

The three parts (concept, engagement posts, supplementary content) stay the same every week. What changes is the niche edition. Each edition is the same for every buyer in that niche, so it stays a product, not custom work.

## Why owners need this (background research)
### The problem owners face
- **Feeds go quiet.** After launch week, posts get fewer replies. A quiet feed tells new members the group is dead.
- **Churn.** Members leave once they finish a course, unless something new and useful keeps coming.
- **Pressure to produce every week.** Grok Research found that the top AI Skool groups make \$200K+ MRR on about 2.5k–3.5k members by selling a result. That means owners must keep producing fresh, usable material every week. That's the supplier gap.
- **Platform limits.** Skool owners can't schedule posts, and "email all members" is capped at once every 72 hours. So every post has to be ready, simple, and worth it.
- **Burnout.** Most owners are also running a business, coaching, or working a job. Ideas run dry fast.
### What Skool research shows (Skool research, Sep 28, 2026)
- **Engagement beats more content.** In the Skoolers community, a retention thread by Tommy Driscoll (89 comments) landed on this point. That's why each drop is one concept plus posts that get replies, not a pile of content.
- **Owners are asking for ready-made material:** post templates, a ready-made 30-day content calendar, and design templates. Course templates reportedly stopped churn among affiliates.
- **Themed weeks work.** AI Money Group (Jeff Hunter, \~455 members, \$49/mo) runs a themed week: Money Monday, tool days, Proof Friday, Set-and-Forget Saturday, Sunday Reset, plus a Tuesday mission call. This supports the Engagement Kit add-on below.
- **Homework / mission threads and the "Start Here" post beat news posts.**
- **Top groups keep members with time-based unlocks and weekly calls.** Example: AIS+ unlocks a client module at 30 days and a scale module at 90 days.
### Why supply beats being another guru
- The guru market is crowded and loud. Everyone is selling "how to grow your Skool."
- Owners don't need another course. They need something they can **use today**.
- A supplier doesn't compete with the gurus. The gurus' students are our buyers.
- Jack doesn't need to be famous or a great writer. AI does the drafting. Jack needs a good eye for what's useful, which he has.
- Supply is repeatable: the same product every week for many buyers, with no client calls.
## How it's made each week
AI agents do the research and the writing. Jack doesn't write the drops. He picks, checks, and adds a line of real-world experience where it helps.

| 8. Ship | Delivery agent | Uploads to the vault and sends the delivery note on drop day. |

### Quality control
- Facts in "what's new" concepts must link to a real source. If it can't be checked, it doesn't ship.
- A banned-phrase list (e.g., "unlock your potential," "in today's fast-paced world").
- A "would a good owner post this in their own group?" test on every concept.
- A tracker so no concept repeats within 6 months.
- Buyer feedback: a 1-click "did this get replies?" rating on each drop.
### Delivery
- **Email:** A short weekly delivery note with a link and a "start here" line. This is a delivery note, **not** a newsletter. No essays from Jack.
- **Vault (Skool classroom or Notion):** Every past drop is stored and searchable by niche and type (concepts, posts, prompts, checklists).
- **Maybe a Skool group of its own, with the feed mostly off:** Used as a delivery shelf, not a community. Jack doesn't host or moderate it.

## Add-on: Weekly Engagement Kit (cross-sell)
This was the core product in the first version of this page. It's now an add-on for owners who want a full week of posts, not just the concept. It sells in the Pro tier (or as a stand-alone add-on, to test). The kit is built around the week's concept where it fits, so the two work together.
### The weekly cadence: a themed-day plan
Modeled on what works in top groups (themed days, mission threads, a weekly call, Friday proof). The owner copies and posts each day's piece, since Skool can't schedule posts.

| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sunday (drop day)** | Reset | The kit lands (email + vault) with the week's concept. Optional "Sunday Reset" post: "What's your focus this week?" |
| **Monday** | Monday prompt | The week's standout discussion prompt + poll. Kicks off the mission. |
| **Tuesday** | Mission call | Live-call topic + 30-minute run sheet. |
| **Wednesday (day 3)** | Midweek mission check-in | The **second use**: a check-in on the mission. Pulls in members who missed Monday. |
| **Thursday** | Tool / tip day | A quick resource or tip post from the bonus content. |
| **Friday** | Proof / wins | A proof-or-wins thread: members post what they finished. |
| **Next Sunday** |  | Fresh kit. |

Each kit also includes **one "email all" message** for the week, because Skool caps email-all at once every 72 hours.
### What's in the kit
**1. MAIN: "Here's what you need this week that you can just use in your group"**
Each week has **one standout piece** (marked "Start here"), with supporting pieces around it:
- **Monday discussion prompt** + 3 follow-up replies the owner can use to keep the thread going
- **A poll** tied to the week's theme
- **A mission / homework thread** with a **day-3 check-in** post (mission threads beat news posts)
- **A Friday proof / wins thread starter**
- **A live-call topic + run sheet** (30 minutes, timed)
- **A welcome / "Start Here" post refresh** and onboarding script for new members
- **A re-engagement DM** for quiet members
- **A time-based unlock idea** (e.g., what to unlock at 30 days) to give members a reason to stay after the course
- **The week's "email all" message** (one per week, to fit Skool's 72-hour cap)
- **Graphics or a Canva template** for the prompt, mission and wins posts
**2. LIGHT: A little "how to grow your group"**
Kept small and at the back, so we don't fight the gurus:
- **One small tactic a week**, in 5 lines or less. Example: "Add one question to your join form that tells you what to post about."
**3. BONUS: Content that helps in their group**
- **A short lesson** (a 5-minute read or talking points)
- **A swipe file** (e.g., 10 post hooks)
- **A checklist**
- **A mini-module** they can drop into their Skool classroom (useful as a locked or time-based unlock)
### EXAMPLE: A fully worked sample kit week
> **This is an example only**, to show the format. Theme: **"The One-Thing Week"**
**Start here (the standout piece): The "One Thing" Mission**
- **Monday post:** "Quick one: What's ONE thing you'll finish by Friday? Not five. One. Drop it below and I'll check back Wednesday. 👇"
- **3 follow-ups for the owner:**
	1. "Love it. What's the first 15-minute step?"
	2. "What usually gets in the way of this one?"
	3. "Who else here is working on something similar? Tag them."
- **Tuesday mission call (30 min):** 0–5 welcome and early wins · 5–15 hot seat: two members share their one thing and what's blocking it · 15–25 group fixes · 25–30 everyone commits to a Wednesday update.
- **Day-3 check-in (Wednesday):** "Halfway check. How's your ONE thing going? Reply with 🟢 on track, 🟡 slipping, or 🔴 stuck. If you're 🔴, tell us why and we'll help."
- **Thursday tip:** "The 15-minute rule: if it takes under 15 minutes, do it now. Post one thing you knocked out today."
- **Friday proof thread:** "Proof Friday. Did you finish your ONE thing? Post it, even if it's small. Finished beats perfect."
**Poll (Monday):** "What steals most of your focus? A) Email B) Social media C) Meetings D) My own ideas"
**Email-all message (send Monday):** "This week we're doing the One Thing Mission. Pick one goal, post it in the group, and we'll check in Wednesday. Takes 30 seconds: \[link\]"

## Distribution ideas
How community owners could find it, without Jack hosting a group or writing a newsletter:
- **Free sample drop:** One full weekly drop (concept + posts + extras) as a lead magnet. Let owners feel the "crack a beer" moment.
- **Free 30-day calendar:** Owners are explicitly asking for one. Give a sample away as the top-of-funnel hook, and use it to show the Engagement Kit add-on.
- **Skool's owner hangouts:** Be useful in places like the Skoolers community (e.g., retention threads) and around Skool Games (value posts, sample concepts, no hard pitching).
- **Affiliate deals with group owners and gurus:** Pay a recurring commission to people who teach community building. Their students are our buyers, so we're a partner, not a rival.
- **Resell rights as a distribution engine:** A paid resell tier lets other sellers carry the product to their buyers. Evelyn Weiss's \$7/mo hub claims 5,359+ coaches, and Template Tribe runs a resell-rights paid tier.
- **Bundle partners:** Get included in community-launch bundles or tool deals (e.g., alongside scheduling or community-management tools).
- **Owner forums and subreddits:** Answer "what do I post?" questions with a real sample concept and a link.
- **Marketplace listings:** List the free sample and starter crate on digital-product marketplaces and PLR stores.
- **Niche edition outreach:** Short, direct outreach to owners in each niche (starting with local-business coaching) with a free sample drop from their edition.

## Risks and open questions
- **Stepping on the owner's expertise.** If a concept overlaps what the owner already teaches, or contradicts it, it makes them look bad. Every drop needs a "where this fits" note, and Jack's pick step must check for overlap.
- **Niche breadth can make quality uneven.** Jack knows local business deeply, but not every niche equally. Launch one edition at a time, source-check every "what's new" concept, and only add an edition when the last one is solid.
- **Fast-moving topics go stale.** An AI "what's new" concept can be out of date in a week. Ship close to drop day and link sources.
- **PLR is crowded and cheap.** Buyers may see this as "just more PLR." We need to show it's different: one concept, niche-specific, weekly, with ready posts.
- **Free alternatives exist.** StickyHive's free templates and free AI tools set a low bar. Why pay? (Answer to test: curation, freshness, and a niche angle the owner didn't have time to find.)
- **Quality vs. generic AI slop.** If a drop reads like a chatbot wrote it, we lose trust fast. Jack's review is the moat, and it has to be real.
- **Proving the engagement lift.** We need before/after proof (replies, active members) from early users. How do we collect it without doing client work?
- **Willingness to pay.** Will owners pay monthly, yearly, or only buy once? Does the kit upsell sell?
- **Big free Facebook groups may not pay.** The large OpenClaw/AI group owners (100K to 450K members) are a second buyer type to test, but their groups are free and none showed a priced offer. They may like the drop and still not pay for it. Test with one or two owners before building for them. The Facebook research was also a partial sample (about 10 to 20 posts per group).
- **Owner's voice.** Can shared material feel like the owner's own voice? How much editing will they need?
- **Platform limits and risk.** No post scheduling and a 72-hour email-all cap mean owners post by hand. Skool may change features. Drops should work on any platform.
- **The working name.** "Shovel Your Biz-Shit" is memorable but may put off some buyers or marketplaces. Test it, and keep "Shovels for Community Builders" as a clean fallback.
