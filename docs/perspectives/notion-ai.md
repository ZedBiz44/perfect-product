## Overview
The work under Perfect Product is converging on a quiet, productized supplier business rather than another community, newsletter, coaching offer, or software-specific product.
The strongest direction is to supply repeatable, ready-to-use campaigns and assets to people who already own an audience. They bring the customers, trust, and delivery channel. ZedBiz brings the practical idea, finished materials, production system, and quality control.
This uses Jack's real strength: cross-domain practical judgment. He does not have to become the public expert on one tool, perform online every day, or educate thousands of end buyers. AI and automation remain production tools behind the scenes rather than the headline.
The immediate goal should not be to build a large subscription library. It should be to prove that one standardized campaign creates a measurable result for several similar buyers. If that works, the campaign can become recurring inventory and eventually a transferable business system.
## Three key thoughts
### 1. Build a wholesale product factory
The better question is not, “What should Jack sell to thousands of people?” It is, “Who already has customers and repeatedly needs something useful to deliver or sell to them?”
Potential buyers include:
- Paid community and membership owners
- Local publishers and directory operators
- Associations
- Coaches with established audiences
- Agencies that need client-facing assets
The channel owner should be treated as the customer, not merely an affiliate. ZedBiz supplies finished inventory that the owner can deliver under their own name. Their members may never need to know ZedBiz exists.
This removes the hardest unsolved problem: Jack personally manufacturing attention from zero.
### 2. Sell a measurable campaign, not “weekly content”
A weekly drop is a delivery method, not a strong product promise. “Fresh content every week” is too easy to compare with cheap PLR and free AI output.
The initial product should be a fixed four-week campaign built around a measurable operating result, such as:
- Reactivate quiet members
- Give new members a useful first win
- Produce credible member success stories
- Keep members active after they finish a course
- Turn existing members into leads for the owner's next offer
The weekly concept, posts, checklist, comment-to-get asset, and supporting material become components of that campaign.
The first commercial test should be the same four-week campaign sold to three established owners. If it creates a measurable result, the recurring subscription becomes the replenishment plan. This proves value before creating another endless production commitment.
### 3. Build performance intelligence as the moat
AI can generate posts, prompts, templates, and checklists. A content vault by itself will become increasingly commoditized.
The valuable asset is the evidence showing what causes each type of audience to act. Every campaign should capture:
- Whether the owner actually posted each item
- Replies and participation generated
- Member actions or completed challenges
- Leads or sales produced
- The owner's rating and comments
- What failed and under what conditions
Over time, this creates a private performance dataset and operating playbook. The agents can generate candidates, the system can select ideas using accumulated evidence, and Jack can review exceptions rather than write everything.
That is what makes the business defensible and sellable. A future buyer could acquire the production process, customer licenses, performance data, and operating rules without depending on Jack's personality or daily presence.
## Recommended next move
- Select one class of established audience owner.
- Define one measurable four-week result they already care about.
- Sell the identical pilot to three owners before building a subscription library.
- Track usage and outcomes from every component.
- Turn the winning campaign into recurring inventory.
- Codify Jack's review decisions so another trained operator can eventually run the system.
The emerging business is a quiet, white-label supplier for people who already own attention. The long-term asset is not a pile of content. It is a measured system for repeatedly creating material that gets a specific audience to act.
## Trial phase
The cleanest first test is a four-week Member Wake-Up Campaign for paid community owners. Do not build a subscription yet. Prove that owners will use the material and that members respond.
### Pick one beachhead
Start with owners of paid communities serving local-business owners, marketers, or practical operators.
Reasons to start here:
- Jack understands their members.
- The owner already earns recurring revenue.
- An inactive member has a visible financial cost.
- Owners need fresh reasons for members to return.
- They can test the campaign without technical setup.
### Float the problem before pitching the product
Post about the pain and result, not the product name. Test three angles over seven days.
**Post A: Problem test**
> Paid community owners, quick question. What is harder right now: getting new members or getting existing members to come back and participate?
	I’m looking at a simple four-week system for waking up quiet members without adding more live calls or another course. Curious which problem is actually costing owners more.
**Post B: Behaviour test**
> If I handed you a complete member-engagement campaign every Monday, what would make you actually use it?
	A) Ready-to-post discussion<br>B) Member challenge<br>C) Comment-to-get resource<br>D) Re-engagement message<br>E) A full weekly campaign built around one result
**Post C: Offer test**
> I’m looking for three paid-community owners to test a four-week Member Wake-Up Campaign.
	You get one complete campaign each week: the main post, follow-ups, a member action, a downloadable resource, and a simple results tracker.
	No consulting, no new software, and no extra calls. You post it inside your existing community and report what happened.
	Comment WAKE-UP or message me if you have a paid group with at least 50 members.
Use LinkedIn, relevant Facebook groups where promotion is allowed, and direct messages to community owners Jack already knows.
### Measure buying signals, not ordinary engagement
Likes from marketers and AI enthusiasts are weak evidence. Measure:
- Qualified community owners responding
- Owners volunteering information about their problem
- Requests to see the sample
- Owners willing to install the campaign
- Owners willing to pay
The first gate should be:
- 20 targeted owner conversations
- At least five requests for a sample
- Three pilot customers
- At least two willing to pay
Charge US\$99 to US\$149 for the complete four-week founding pilot. Free testers frequently praise products they never use. A paid pilot proves the problem matters.
### Trial success criteria
Continue only if:
- At least two of three owners complete the four weeks.
- Owners post at least 75% of the supplied material.
- At least half the campaign posts beat the community’s normal participation.
- Owners report meaningful preparation time saved.
- At least two owners want another month.
- Jack’s review and support stay below two hours per week.
If owners buy but do not post, the product is too difficult or does not match their workflow. If they post but members do not act, the campaign is weak. If members act but owners will not renew, the financial outcome is unclear.
## Building the MVP
### MVP offer
**Working name:** Member Wake-Up Campaign
**Promise:**
> Wake up quiet members and create four visible wins in four weeks, without adding another course or live call.
This is stronger than selling “weekly content” because it names a result.
### Build only Week One before selling
Create one complete sample containing:
- One practical weekly concept
- One primary discussion or mission post
- Three short owner replies to keep the discussion moving
- One day-three follow-up
- One comment-to-get resource
- One Friday proof or wins post
- One re-engagement message for inactive members
- One small graphic
- One results form
For Weeks Two through Four, prepare titles and short outlines only. Finish them after the first pilots pay and provide baseline information.
### Suggested four-week campaign
**Week One: Return**
Give inactive members a tiny, low-pressure reason to show up again.
**Week Two: Complete**
Run one small challenge members can finish within the week.
**Week Three: Prove**
Turn completed work into screenshots, examples, testimonials, or member wins.
**Week Four: Continue**
Move participants toward the owner’s next module, offer, event, or renewal reason.
This creates a clear progression rather than four disconnected content packs.
### Simple delivery system
Use existing tools:
- Notion for the customer portal and campaign vault
- GHL or email for weekly delivery notifications
- Canva for one reusable graphic template
- Notion Forms or Google Forms for weekly owner feedback
- A Notion database for results and performance tracking
Each pilot receives the same core campaign. Only allow light substitutions such as terminology, niche examples, and the owner’s call to action. Do not create a different campaign for every customer.
### Track the moat from day one
For every campaign component, record:
- Delivered
- Posted or not posted
- Date posted
- Normal engagement baseline
- Comments or replies
- Members completing the action
- Leads or sales produced
- Owner rating
- Time saved
- Would use again
- Owner’s exact objection or requested change
This is how the MVP becomes a real system rather than another content service.
### Do not build yet
Avoid:
- A full website
- A new Skool community
- A large content vault
- Multiple niche editions
- Automated personalization
- White-label software
- An affiliate program
- Annual subscriptions
- Fifty templates
- Custom client campaigns
Sell and complete three identical pilots first.
## Immediate move
Create one polished Week One sample, publish the three social posts, and directly approach 20 qualified community owners.
The key question is not whether people like the concept. It is:
> Will an established owner pay, post the material, get a visible response, and ask for the next campaign?
If the answer is yes three times, build the subscription.
