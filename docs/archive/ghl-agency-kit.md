## Bottom line
> **Correction: No GoHighLevel agency product is recommended. GoHighLevel is a useful tool in the ZedBiz toolbox, not a market, credibility base, or standalone business for Jack.**
Recommended working offer: **No Lead Left Behind: GHL Agency Kit**
- **Buyer:** GoHighLevel agencies and experienced GHL consultants serving local businesses.
- **Promise:** Install one ready-to-use lead-response system that gives every new lead an immediate response, a clear next step, and a follow-up path without rebuilding a client account.
- **Founding price:** US\$249, one-time, for the first ten buyers. Include US\$99/year for optional updates after the first year.
- **Delivery:** Downloadable workflow or snapshot, install video, setup checklist, copy pack, testing checklist, and approval-mode rules.
- **Support boundary:** One consolidated written setup question per buyer, answered in a weekly batch. No account access, custom mapping, imports, calls, group moderation, or bespoke troubleshooting.
- **Capacity rule:** Stop selling new copies if the kit cannot be installed in a clean demo account in under 30 minutes or if support exceeds five hours a week.
This buyer can understand the problem, install a system, and resell it to clients. That avoids the local-business education and onboarding wall documented in Why They Don't Buy. It also gives ZedBiz a channel buyer instead of many individual small-business buyers.
## What the review says
- Perfect Product has defined the right bars: repeatable revenue, build once and sell many times, an asset that can be sold, and a business that does not require Jack as the daily host.
- The Skool research confirms that generic AI groups are crowded and the high earners usually depend on a large audience plus frequent founder access. That is the wrong operating model for Jack.
- Field Notes is a better founder-fit model than a community, but it is a slow audience-building asset. It is not the fastest route to sales next week.
- Why They Don't Buy correctly rules out selling self-serve marketing systems to many ordinary local businesses. They often lack time, money, technical comfort, and the vocabulary to install them.
- Earlier work repeatedly surfaced three promising assets: WordPress capability, GoHighLevel systems, and directory or publisher distribution. The missing move is to turn one existing asset into one small product for one capable buyer.
## What has been missed so far
### The buyer should be a channel, not the end client
A local business owner usually creates teaching, chasing, and setup work. An agency or capable consultant already understands leads, workflows, pipelines, and client value. One agency buyer can use the same kit many times. That is a much better match for an online product.
### A sellable product needs a support contract before launch
The support ceiling must be designed in, not negotiated after the first complaint. The product page needs clear prerequisites, installation time, supported versions, what is included, what is not included, and the only support path.
### The product must be shelf-ready, not a research topic
A course, club, or broad AI system invites endless content creation. A first product should be one job, one buyer, one installation, and one visible result. The rule is simple: if it needs a discovery call or customer credentials to work, it is not the first product.
### Sales proof is the next research
There is plenty of market research. The next unknown is not what could work. It is whether a defined buyer will pay for a narrowly described kit before it is expanded. Paid orders beat votes, likes, and group members.
### Distribution needs a quiet route
Do not build a social-posting treadmill. Start with a short sales page, a demo video, direct email to known GHL and WordPress contacts, three potential affiliate or referral partners, and relevant marketplace listings where permitted. The target is buyer conversations and paid orders, not daily public engagement.
## Five-hour operating model
- **Product upkeep:** One hour each week to maintain a changelog, test the current release, and update one asset only when a platform change breaks it.
- **Buyer support:** One weekly written support batch capped at 90 minutes.
- **Sales and partners:** One 60-minute block for direct outreach or partner follow-up. No daily social obligation.
- **Improvement:** One 90-minute block to improve the most common installation failure or add the next paid job pack.
- **Review:** One 30-minute scorecard review: sales, refunds, install success, support time, repeat purchases, and renewal interest.
If those limits are exceeded, reduce scope or price the next tier higher. Do not solve the problem by quietly becoming the customer’s unpaid technician.

## The test I would run next week
### Offer
Sell the **No Lead Left Behind: GHL Agency Kit** as a founding release to ten GHL agencies or consultants at US\$249. Do not call it a club, academy, or managed service.
### Assets needed before the first sale
- A clean demonstration account with one lead source, one immediate response, one reminder, one owner notification, and one test lead.
- A ten-minute demo video showing the problem, the workflow, the install steps, and the result.
- A one-page sales page with prerequisites, inclusions, exclusions, licensing, support boundary, refund terms, and an update policy.
- An automated delivery folder with the snapshot or workflow, copy pack, video, checklist, and changelog.
### Sales route
- Email or message a small number of known GHL agency contacts and WordPress or marketing partners directly.
- Ask three people who already serve agencies whether they will refer a buyer for a share of the first sale.
- List the kit in one relevant permitted marketplace or partner channel.
- Do not spend the week trying to grow a group or produce daily social content.
### Decision rule
- Continue only after paid orders prove the promise, the install succeeds, and support remains inside the five-hour weekly ceiling.
- If buyers hesitate, record their exact objection and change the offer only when the same objection repeats.
- If the first kit cannot meet the support boundary, do not build a bigger suite. Narrow the job further.
## Questions Jack should decide
- The first buyer and niche must be selected from Jack's genuine experience, differentiated proof, and a quiet, realistic sales route. GHL agency is expressly ruled out.
- No GoHighLevel workflow or snapshot should be treated as the next product without a separate reason it is uniquely right for Jack.
- What licence is intended: one agency account, one client account, or a limited number of client installs?
- Is US\$249 a fair founding price for the first version, or should the first ten buyers receive a lower US\$149 validation price in exchange for written feedback?
