## ⚡ Executive summary — start here
- **The concept:** a paid community for a buyer to be named, where Jack shows up for exactly one bounded thing — a weekly 1–2 hour Q&A — and everything else is run by a VA "mayor," his own agent fleet, and structure.
- **The promise (draft):** "Weekly live answers + daily ops intel for \[niche\] — without the guru circus."
- **Jack's time:** 1–2 hrs/week, scheduled, substantive. Zero daily check-ins, zero small talk, zero comment-section duty.
- **The money:** recurring membership (≈\$29–49/mo) → playbook tier (\$97–149/mo) → advisory/implementation (\$1.5–2.5K/mo). Free group + paid letter as feeders.
- **The bet:** a founder who hates hosting can still own a community if the *system* hosts. The fleet eats its own dog food — an agent-run community for operators.
- **Open decisions:** (1) the buyer — agency owners vs. the OpenClaw/agent-ops lane; (2) whether a community layer happens at all (Field Notes needs none of this).
## The core idea
- Most paid communities are hostage to the host: the founder must be charming daily or the thing dies.
- This model inverts it: the founder is a scheduled *event*; the *system* is the host.
- Five mechanisms carry the daily load (next section).
- Why Jack can win here: anti-guru with live proof (17-agent fleet in production). Blunt + receipts beats charming + vibes for buyers burned by the mire.
## How it runs without Jack hosting it
- **1. The Laurel model — the only founder-time.** One weekly 1–2hr Q&A, otherwise absent. The promise is "ask Jack anything Thursday," not "hang out with Jack."
- **2. VA "mayor" — the social layer.** Welcomes members, warms threads, notices who's gone quiet, collects Q&A questions all week. Part-time Philippines, ≈\$400–1,200/mo. Standard community COGS, not a hack.
- **3. The fleet as daily engine — the knowledge layer.** A community agent scans daily: answers FAQs from the playbook, welcomes new members, celebrates wins, drafts the weekly digest, escalates the 5% that needs Jack into the Q&A queue.
- **4. Structure over personality — the rhythm layer.** Scheduled recurring threads (Monday wins, Wednesday Q&A collection, Friday ops radar). Member levels and leaderboards. Tooling (e.g. StickyHive-type schedulers for Skool) automates posting cadence, moderation, and auto-FAQ.
- **5. Content-fed discussion — the fuel.** The Field Notes letter + short videos drop into the group; discussion orbits the work. Jack never has to invent a topic.
## What Jack does vs. doesn't do (founder-fit)
**Does:**
- Weekly 1–2hr Q&A — can do it from anywhere, can skip a week (set "3 per month" expectation up front)
- Review the handful of escalated questions the fleet couldn't answer (≈30 min/week)
- Record short videos when he feels like it (feeds YouTube + the group)
**Doesn't:**
- Daily check-ins (morning / noon / night)
- Small talk, icebreakers, "great question!! 🙌" replies
- Comment-section duty or DM office hours
- Onboarding hand-holding (VA + agent own it)
## Platform options
- **Skool — paid community home.** Native payments, discovery engine, classroom, gamification (levels/leaderboard). Tooling exists for scheduling, AI moderation, analytics. Where the AI buyer crowd already gathers. *Pick if the paid community is the product.*
- **Facebook Group — free feeder.** Where Laurel runs hers; where the 34K–106K OpenClaw members already sit. Terrible monetization, great reach. *Pick as the free audience layer feeding paid elsewhere.*
- **Discord — probably not.** Best AI bot ecosystem (mature moderation + chat bots), but the demographic is tinkerers, not wallets.
- **Circle / Mighty Networks — brand pick.** Nicer branding and control, but no discovery engine — you're renting software, not distribution. *Pick if brand matters more than growth.*
- **Recommended stack:** free Facebook group as feeder + paid Skool as home + the letter (email) underneath both as the tripwire.

## Target market options — money × pain × reachability
- **Agency owners (5–50 staff):** money ✓ / AI pain ✓ (clients asking, margins squeezed) / reachable ✓ (Skool, FB, YouTube). *Best buyer on paper. Open question: Jack's stomach for the adjacent-mire.*
- **Marketing operators / freelancers:** money \~ (thinner) / pain ✓ / reachable ✓. *Good rank-and-file for the community; unlikely to buy the top tier.*
- **OpenClaw / agent-ops practitioners:** money ✗ (tinkerers) / pain ✓ / reachable ✓ (FB groups, uncaptured). *Great feeder audience, weak buyer — monetize via the ladder, not the community itself.*
- **AI enthusiasts / hobbyists:** money ✗ / pain is curiosity, not cost / reachable ✓. *Audience, not market. Free tier only.*
## Content level
- **Practitioner (recommended).** "What broke this week" only makes sense to people already operating. Matches Jack's proof. Filters out tourists.
- **Beginner.** Bigger market, but needs courses, not field notes — and beginner content backed by advanced proof reads as guru. *Not recommended.*
- **Mixed.** Beginner questions answered by the fleet/VA from the playbook; advanced discussion reserved for the Q&A. *Possible compromise; watch for dilution.*
## Content pillars — what members actually get
- Weekly field note — what broke, what changed, the pattern (feeds from the letter)
- Monthly incident deep-dives — one real failure, fully dissected
- Playbook drops — versioned SOPs from live fleet operations
- Ops radar — OpenClaw / GHL / model version changes and what they break
- Weekly live Q&A — the event (recorded, archived, timestamped)
- Wins + teardowns — members post, VA/agent curate, Jack reacts on the Q&A
- Templates & blueprints — agent configs, prompt packs, checklists (config products, never a managed fleet)
## The money: value ladder
- **Free** — Facebook group and/or free Skool. Audience, reach, feeder.
- **\$7–15/mo — the letter (Field Notes).** Paid tripwire, cheapest yes.
- **\$29–49/mo — the community.** Daily engine + weekly Q&A + full archive.
- **\$97–149/mo — playbook tier.** Versioned SOP library, blueprints, templates, quarterly workshops.
- **\$1.5–2.5K/mo — advisory / vertical implementation.** Bounded, high-touch, few seats.
- Annual plans at ≈10x monthly for cash up front; founding-member pricing for the first 100.
## Upsells & cross-sells
- Playbook library access — one-time packs or tier upgrade
- Fleet blueprint packs — configs + prompts (product, not service)
- Quarterly live workshops — bounded events, recorded straight into the archive
- "Ask the fleet" async channel — agent-answered tier between community and playbook
- Annual plan upgrades — "2 months free" framing
- Affiliate revenue — only on tools the fleet actually runs
- Cross-sell: bounded done-with-you GHL/ZedNow setups (⚠️ fixed scope only — the onboarding wall killed the unbounded version)
- Cross-sell: the letter to free-group members; the community to letter subscribers
- Downsell: churned members → letter tier (keep them in the ecosystem, don't lose them to nothing)
## Operating model: who does what
- **Jack:** 1–2 hrs/week Q&A + ≈30 min reviewing escalations. Total ≈2–3 hrs/week.
- **VA mayor:** ≈1–2 hrs/day — welcomes, warms, collects, posts scheduled threads. \$400–1,200/mo.
- **Fleet community agent:** daily scan — FAQ answers, new-member welcomes, win celebrations, digest draft, escalation queue. Marginal cost ≈ zero (runs on the existing fleet).
- **Tooling:** scheduler / moderation / analytics ≈ \$50–100/mo (if Skool).
- **Total cost before revenue:** ≈ \$500–1,300/mo + 2–3 hrs/week of Jack.
## Launch sequence
- **Phase 0 — decide the buyer.** Agency owners vs. OpenClaw lane. Everything downstream hangs on this.
- **Phase 1 — feeder (60–90 days).** Free group live + letter publishing. Proof before charging.
- **Phase 2 — seed.** Founding 50–100 members at founding pricing. VA-led onboarding. Jack does the weekly Q&A from day one.
- **Phase 3 — systematize.** Fleet agent online, scheduled threads running, Jack drops to Q&A-only.
- **Phase 4 — ladder.** Playbook tier opens, then advisory seats.
## Risks & open questions
- Cold start: 0→100 members needs a human with social energy (the VA) — budget it, don't wish it away.
- Buyer-stomach question unresolved (agency owners vs. OpenClaw lane).
- Enthusiast audiences engage but don't pay — feeder vs. buyer must stay separated.
- Q&A goes stale if questions dry up — the collection system is load-bearing.
- Jack's travel / weeks off — "3 Q&As per month" expectation set at launch.
- Distribution is still the gap (trait #9) — free group + letter + YouTube shorts are the acquisition engine, unproven.
- Scope creep back into services — every offer must pass the killed list: no audits, no managed fleets, no unbounded onboarding, no host treadmill.
## Scorecard check — 11 bars
- ✅ Pays more than once — recurring membership + ladder.
- ✅ Built once, sold many — content and systems amortized; the Q&A is bounded recurring labor, amortized across all members.
- 🔶 One-sentence promise — draft exists, needs the buyer filled in.
- ✅ Cheap first yes — free group / \$7 letter.
- ✅ Value ladder — free → letter → community → playbook → advisory.
- 🔶 Deletes a bottleneck — deletes "figuring out AI ops alone" (trial-and-error time). Softer than the product examples.
- ✅ Evergreen demand — AI churn regenerates the problem monthly.
- ❌ Own distribution — the gap. Feeder strategy unproven.
- 🔶 Sellable asset — member base + library are sellable; Jack's Q&A ties value to him, buyer discount applies.
- 🔶 Runs without founder — VA + fleet + structure carry it; the weekly Q&A is the remaining tie (guest hosts? rotating experts? open).
- ✅ No host treadmill — this entire page exists to satisfy that bar.
